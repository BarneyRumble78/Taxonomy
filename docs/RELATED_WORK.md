# Pyramids of Knowledge: related work and positioning brief

Prepared 8 October 2026. Every URL below was opened for this brief. If a page did not give a detail, the entry says so; it is not filled from memory. "Unverified" marks a detail I could not confirm from an opened page.

Key to the "Relation" column. **Sim** = similarity to Pyramids. **Diff** = difference from Pyramids.

## 1. Library and bibliographic classification

| Scheme | What, who, when | Relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC)** | Melvil Dewey, a 44-page pamphlet in 1876. Current edition DDC 23 (2011), now online only as WebDewey, updated quarterly. OCLC has maintained it since 1988. Ten main classes: 000 Computer science, information & general works; 100 Philosophy & psychology; 200 Religion; 300 Social sciences; 400 Language; 500 Science; 600 Technology; 700 Arts & recreation; 800 Literature; 900 History & geography. | **Sim:** a single hierarchy of all knowledge with a fixed top level. **Diff:** classifies documents for shelving and is enumerative. | **Library of Congress Classification (LCC)** | Developed from 1897; first outline 1904. | **Universal Decimal Classification (UDC)** | Paul Otlet and Henri La Fontaine, starting from a translation of DDC in the late 19th century. Created around 1900; managed by FID, and by the UDC Consortium since 1 January 1992. Main classes 0–9, with class 4 vacant. Common auxiliaries cover place, language, time and form. ## 2. Faceted classification

| Item | What, who, when | Relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colon Classification (CC)**, S. R. Ranganathan | Developed 1924–28; first published 1933 (Madras Library Association). Seven editions; the 7th (1987) was posthumous; the 6th (1960/63) is the most used. **PMEST** (Personality, Matter, Energy, Space, Time) was named in the 4th edition (1952). **Phase relations:** six types, each with an indicator digit: general (a), bias (b), comparison (c), difference (d), tool (e), influencing (g). They operate at three levels, giving 18 relations, marked with `&`. Analytico-synthetic: the classifier analyses a subject into facets and synthesises a number. | **Sim:** this is the foundational precedent for splitting a subject into orthogonal facets (cf. Faces O/M/W/B). Two phase relations are direct precedents for Pyramids' cross-field rules. **Tool phase** ("statistics applied to biology") ≈ "uses [owner id]". **Influencing phase** ≈ border rules. "Energy" (action, process) is a partial analogue of method. **Diff:** PMEST categories describe a document's *subject*. None encodes *what licenses* a statement. Phase relations type the link between subjects, not the evidential role. | https://www.isko.org/cyclo/colon_classification (M. P. Satija, ISKO Encyclopedia, 22 Mar 2017; v2.0 5 Apr 2017) |

## 3. Phenomenon-based classification

| Item | What, who, when | Relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Integrative Levels Classification (ILC)** | Claudio Gnoli and an ISKO Italia working group; linked to the León Manifesto (2007). ILC1 was frozen in 2011 with 7,052 classes and facets. ILC2 (2019) has 10,845, with 25 main classes a–y ordered by levels of reality (Hartmann, Feibleman), e.g. *m* organisms, *q* language, *y* knowledge. It is published as SKOS linked data and is "freely and fully available" at iskoi.org/ilc. ILC3 is referenced in 2024. The scheme's licence is not stated on the page. | **Sim:** it rejects disciplines as the primary unit, as Pyramids does when it treats fields as Face B "bridges". Disciplines are reachable as a perspective facet, much as bridges pair a cell with methods. An ordered levels sequence resembles Pyramids' Comte-like field order. **Diff:** ILC classifies *phenomena* (what something is about). Pyramids adds *how it is known* (M) and *what licenses it* (W) as separate faces. ILC is the most direct "you must cite this" precedent for an object-face. | https://www.isko.org/cyclo/ilc (C. Gnoli, ISKO Encyclopedia, v1.0 13 Feb 2020; v1.4 19 Mar 2024) |
| **Information Coding Classification (ICC)**, Ingetraut Dahlberg | Rooted in her 1974 dissertation. About 6,500 knowledge fields; full description in 2014. Nine **ontical levels**: structure & form; matter & energy; cosmos & earth; biosphere; anthroposphere; sociosphere; material products; intellectual products; spiritual products. Nine **aspects** (the "Systematifier") apply to every field: general (theories, principles), object area, activity area, properties, persons, institutions, influences, applications, information/synthesis tasks. | **Sim:** the Systematifier is the closest structural precedent for a *uniform cross-field grid*. Every field is subdivided by the same aspects, as Pyramids imposes the same four faces and seven method roles. "Applications to other fields" ≈ "uses". **Diff:** the aspects are facets of a *field*, not roles of a *sentence*. There is no warrant vocabulary. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Coding_Classification (isko.org/cyclo/icc blocked by robots.txt) |

## 4. Outlines of all knowledge

| Item | What, who, when | Relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Britannica Propædia** | Introduced in 1974 with the 15th edition; designed and edited by Mortimer J. Adler over eight years. "Outline of Knowledge": 10 parts, 41 divisions, 167 sections. Parts: 1 Matter and Energy; 2 The Earth; 3 Life on Earth; 4 Human Life; 5 Human Society; 6 Art; 7 Technology; 8 Religion; 9 The History of Mankind; 10 The Branches of Knowledge. Adler was lead author of Part 10. | **Sim:** the closest single-author precedent for a top-down outline of all knowledge in prose, which is also Minto-like. The split between Parts 1–9 (*what is*) and Part 10 (*the disciplines that know it*) foreshadows Pyramids' split between Face O and Faces M/B. **Diff:** it indexes encyclopedia articles, and Part 10 describes disciplines rather than warrants. There is no assignment rule, crosswalk or reliability test. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propædia ; https://www.britannica.com/topic/Propaedia |
| **Bacon's division of learning** | *Advancement of Learning* (1605), revised in *De augmentis scientiarum* (1623). Three faculties map to three kinds of learning: memory → history, imagination → poesy, reason → philosophy. History divides into natural, civil, ecclesiastical and literary. | **Sim:** it divides knowledge by the *faculty that produces it*, an early "how it is known" axis, akin to Face M/W. **Diff:** it is a single dimension, and faculties are not warrants. | https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/francis-bacon/ (M. Peltonen, SEP) |
| **Comte's hierarchy of the sciences** | *Cours de philosophie positive*, 6 vols, 1830–1842. The order is mathematics, astronomy, physics, chemistry, biology, sociology. Generality decreases and complexity increases along it, and each science depends on its predecessors. | **Sim:** the classic justification for ordering field pyramids and for "uses" running in one direction, so that lower fields are owners. **Diff:** a reviewer will flag its reductionist assumptions. Pyramids' border rules are bidirectional and include law, politics and philosophy, which Comte's order does not accommodate. | https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/comte/ (M. Bourdeau, SEP, 2008; rev. 13 Jan 2026) |

## 5. Formal (top-level) ontologies

| Item | What, who, when | Relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic Formal Ontology (BFO)** | Barry Smith and Pierre Grenon, with major contributions from Alan Ruttenberg. Standardised as **ISO/IEC 21838-2:2021**, published 30 Nov 2021. The standard gives definitions of BFO-2020 terms and relations, OWL 2 and Common Logic axiomatisations, and requirements for conforming domain ontologies. The BFO site reports more than 550 users. BFO is a "genuine upper ontology" with no special-science terms. | **Sim:** an upper layer shared by many domain modules, analogous to Pyramids' shared M1–M7 and W1–W13 across fields. Its hub-and-module design parallels the R1 registry. **Diff:** BFO types *entities* (it is realist), not sentences or warrants. A Pyramids claim is closer to an information content entity *about* a BFO entity. A crosswalk from Face O to BFO categories is feasible and would impress ontologists. | https://www.iso.org/standard/74572.html ; https://bfo-ontology.github.io/ |
| **DOLCE** | Descriptive Ontology for Linguistic and Cognitive Engineering. ISTC-CNR Laboratory for Applied Ontology, WonderWeb project, first released 2002/2003. Standardised as **ISO/IEC 21838-3:2023** (19 Sep 2023), with OWL 2 and CL axiomatisations. | **Sim/Diff:** as for BFO. DOLCE is "descriptive" (cognitive and linguistic) rather than realist, which is arguably closer to a claim-centred scheme. The page I opened did not list DOLCE's top categories. | https://www.iso.org/standard/78927.html ; https://www.loa.istc.cnr.it/dolce/overview.html |
| **SUMO** | Suggested Upper Merged Ontology, in SUO-KIF, with Adam Pease as technical editor. About 25,000 terms and 80,000 axioms including domain ontologies. Mapped to all of WordNet. Owned by IEEE; extensions are under GNU GPL. | **Sim:** one formal backbone with domain extensions. **Diff:** a logic-heavy, entity-typing ontology, not epistemic classification. | https://www.ontologyportal.org/ |
| **Wikidata** | Wikimedia knowledge graph launched 29 Oct 2012, built on MediaWiki/Wikibase and licensed CC0. Statements have a property and a value, plus **qualifiers** and **references**. About 1.65 billion statements (early 2025). | **Sim:** the largest *statement-level* graph, where each claim carries references. It is the obvious identifier hub for objects named in Face O. **Diff:** the reference is a source citation, not a typed warrant. Nothing records *what kind* of support a statement has (proof versus trial versus stipulation). That is a gap Pyramids could fill, for example as a Wikidata property proposal. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikidata (wikidata.org not fetchable by the tool) |

## 7. Argument and warrant models; claim-level graphs

| Item | What, who, when | Relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Toulmin model** | S. Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument* (1958). Six components: claim; grounds (data); **warrant** (licenses the step from grounds to claim); **backing** (supports the warrant); qualifier; rebuttal. Toulmin distinguished "field-dependent" from "field-invariant" aspects of argument. | **The direct ancestor of Face W.** Pyramids' "warrant type + standard of acceptance + typical defeaters" maps onto warrant/backing + qualifier + rebuttal. Field-dependence is Toulmin's version of Pyramids' claim that warrant types differ by field. **Diff:** Toulmin analyses a single argument. Pyramids proposes a *controlled vocabulary* of warrant types and assigns each claim-cell its default warrant, which Toulmin did not attempt. The opened page did not confirm that Toulmin made *warrants* specifically field-dependent. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Toulmin ; https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/argument/ (C. Dutilh Novaes, SEP, 2021) |
| **Argumentation schemes** | D. Walton, C. Reed and F. Macagno, *Argumentation Schemes*, Cambridge UP, 2008, 443 pp. Contains **65 schemes**, each evaluated by **critical questions**. | **Sim:** the closest precedent for a *typed catalogue of warrants with defeaters*. Critical questions ≈ Pyramids' "typical defeaters". **Diff:** the schemes are dialogue-general (argument from expert opinion, from analogy…), not tied to fields. Provenance ≈ Face M. **Diff:** provenance is free-form RDF, not a controlled method or warrant typology. ## Positioning

Every element of Pyramids has a precedent. Facets come from Ranganathan and BC2. A uniform cross-field aspect grid comes from Dahlberg's Systematifier. Phenomena over disciplines comes from ILC. Separating technique from object comes from PhySH and MeSH. Claim-sized units come from nanopublications and ORKG. Warrants come from Toulmin and Walton.

What appears distinctive, cautiously stated, is the **combination**. The unit classified is a *sentence*, not a document or entity. Each sentence gets one owning cell, and other fields link to it with "uses" rather than holding copies. Every cell is assigned a *warrant type from a controlled list*, with acceptance standards and defeaters. Border rules between fields are stated as testable assignment rules. I found no scheme among those reviewed that types claims by what licenses them across all fields. Wikidata references and nanopublication provenance record *that* support exists, not *what kind*. MeSH publication types come closest, for one field.
