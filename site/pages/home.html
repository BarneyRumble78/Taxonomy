You're handed claims all day. Few of them come labelled.

A report says it's "established". A model answers with total confidence. A briefing slides a guess in beside a measured fact, in the same font and the same tone.

Which parts are proven? Which are observed, estimated or simply assumed? And if one of them falls, what falls with it?

Usually you can't tell. Not quickly, anyway. So you either trust too much or check everything by hand.

We put a label on every claim. A precise one.

Taxonomy works at the level of the claim. Each claim gets four things:

  • Owner field. Which discipline is responsible for it. A statistical claim is answerable to statistics, not to whoever repeats it.
  • Warrant. The type of evidence behind it: proof, measurement, observation, expert consensus, model output and so on. [[CONFIRM final evidence-type list from the standard]]
  • Status. Where it stands right now: settled, contested, provisional, retracted. [[CONFIRM status vocabulary]]
  • Dependencies. What else has to be true for it to hold.

With that in front of you, you can see what's solid and what's soft. You can state each claim as strongly as its evidence allows, and no stronger.

What we don't do: we don't claim to decide what's true. We show what kind of support a claim has, and we make it hard to overstate it. The judgement stays with you.

Three reasons to work with us.

1. Truth-seeking is the job, not a slogan.

Our rule is simple: a claim must not be stated with greater certainty than its evidence warrants. Everything we build enforces that rule, and that includes how we talk about our own results.

2. Built on mathematics.

Mathematics is our reference domain because it's the field where warrant is clearest. A proof is a proof and a conjecture is a conjecture. We test our methods there first, then carry them into messier fields.

3. Open, stable and citable.

The Minerva standard is open. It covers 25 fields and gives 984 stable IDs to the concepts inside them, so a claim you tag today points to the same place next year. [[CONFIRM ID stability policy wording]]

We hold ourselves to the same rule.

We built a frozen test set of 203 claims and scored our own early classifier against it. The honest baseline: 62.1% on owner field and 37.9% on warrant. That's a starting point, not a finish line. We publish it because a company that asks you not to overstate claims shouldn't overstate its own. See the evaluation →

Meet the founder.

I'm Chris Townsend, founder and CEO of Taxonomy and an AI architect based in New Zealand.

I started Taxonomy because I kept running into the same problem. Smart systems and smart people state things more firmly than the evidence lets them. So I set out to build tools that keep track of the difference.
