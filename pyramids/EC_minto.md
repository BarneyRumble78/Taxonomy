## Introduction

**Situation.** Economics studies how scarce resources are allocated among competing uses.

**Complication.** Its topics come from several published schemes that mix scales, so allocation settled by one person, by a market or by a whole society sits side by side without a common order.

**Question.** How is everything in economics divided?

**Answer.** Economics divides by the scale at which an allocation is settled, from the single chooser to markets, to the mechanisms and institutions that govern them, to the aggregate economy, and finally to welfare and public choice for the whole society.

## Governing thought

Economics divides by the scale at which an allocation is settled, from the single chooser to markets, to the mechanisms and institutions that govern them, to the aggregate economy, and finally to welfare and public choice for the whole society.

## Key line

Group of scales of allocation. Basis: kinds. Dimension: the scale at which an allocation is settled. Order: degree. From one agent, to a market, to the whole society.

1. Choice is the scale at which allocation is settled by a single person, household or small group deciding under its own preferences, information and limits. `EC.K1`
2. Markets are the scale at which allocation is settled through exchange among many buyers and sellers, in firms, industries, labour and finance. `EC.K2`
3. Mechanisms and institutions are the scale at which allocation is settled by rules, organisations and bargaining that shape how markets and agents operate. `EC.K3`
4. The aggregate economy is the scale at which allocation is settled across the whole national or world economy, through prices, money, growth, trade and history. `EC.K4`
5. Welfare and public choice is the scale at which allocation is settled collectively for the whole society through governments, public provision and the assessment of well-being. `EC.K5`
