# IK — Indigenous knowledge (ANZSRC 45)

New field, added at the owner's direction. Standard v2 cited, not copied. Read with p2_ANZSRC45.md, which gives the claim-type table.

**Status: drafted outside the tradition. Every placement is provisional until Māori-led review, and review led by Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander and Pacific peoples for their claims. Reviewers may move a claim, refuse a split, or withhold a claim. Some kōrero is tapu and not for open classification.**

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Indigenous knowledge systems, mātauranga Māori among them, hold knowledge about people, land, practice and authority, transmitted by their own institutions.
**Complication.** Placed only by subject, mātauranga is split across BI, EA, HI, LA, LN and RE, and its own ordering principles vanish. Placed as one bucket, it becomes a residual "other", which the standard forbids.
**Question.** What does an Indigenous-knowledge pyramid own that no other pyramid owns, without letting W13 license measurement?
**Answer.** It owns claims about a knowledge system as a system: its ordering principles, its relations to land, its governance, its transmission, and its research and data. Each claim is in one of two modes: ·S (study, W6–W8) or ·M (tradition-internal, W13). Measurements, dated facts, legal duties and word meanings keep their owners.

## 2. Governing thought
Indigenous knowledge is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five aspects of a knowledge system: ordering principles, relations to land and waters, governance and custom, transmission, and research and data.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the aspect of a knowledge system the claim is about.** Order: from the system's principles to its relation with outside research.
1. Ordering principles
2. Relations to land and waters
3. Governance and custom
4. Transmission
5. Research and data

A people (iwi, hapū, nation) is a substrate, not a cell. A ·M sentence names its iwi or hapū, because authority is local.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### IK.O.A1 Ordering principles
**SCQA.** A knowledge system orders the world by its own principles. What are they, as held? Key line: whakapapa as an ordering principle, categories of status, cyclical time.
- IK.O.A1.1 Whakapapa as ordering — genealogy as the principle that relates people, atua and the natural world. Whakapapa as human descent is HI; whakapapa of atua is RE.
- IK.O.A1.2 Categories of status — tapu and noa as ordering categories outside rite (in rite, RE.O.A1.2).
- IK.O.A1.3 Cyclical and ancestral time — "ka mua, ka muri" (walking backwards into the future) as a stated orientation (·S W8; ·M W13).

### IK.O.A2 Relations to land and waters
**SCQA.** Knowledge systems hold relations to particular places. What is claimed about that relation? Key line: kaitiakitanga, maramataka, place knowledge.
- IK.O.A2.1 Kaitiakitanga — guardianship as obligation. Its measured environmental effect is EV.O.A4.4.
- IK.O.A2.2 Maramataka — the lunar calendar as a system for timing activity. An ecological observation it records (a fish run) is BI or EA under W4; the system itself is IK.
- IK.O.A2.3 Place knowledge — names, boundaries and histories of places, held by mana whenua. The landform is EA; the dated event is HI.

### IK.O.A3 Governance and custom
**SCQA.** Communities govern themselves by custom and authority. Key line: tikanga as custom, rangatiratanga, marae kawa, customary institutions.
- IK.O.A3.1 Tikanga as custom — correct practice in social life. In rite before atua it is RE.O.A3.1. As recognised by a state court it is LA.O.A1.3.
- IK.O.A3.2 Rangatiratanga — authority and self-determination as held. Its Treaty and legal dimension is LA.O.A4.4; its political dimension is PO.
- IK.O.A3.3 Marae kawa — the protocols of a particular marae (local, ·M).
- IK.O.A3.4 Customary institutions — rūnanga, hui as decision forums.

### IK.O.A4 Transmission
**SCQA.** Knowledge lives only if transmitted. How is it passed on, and who may hold it? Key line: institutions of learning, oral and performed transmission, restricted knowledge.
- IK.O.A4.1 Institutions of learning — whare wānanga, past and present. Modern wānanga as tertiary institutions are ED.
- IK.O.A4.2 Oral and performed transmission — kōrero tuku iho, mōteatea, haka. Their poetics are AR; their language is LN.
- IK.O.A4.3 Restricted knowledge — knowledge held by designated people and not for open classification. The pyramid records that restriction exists, never the content.

### IK.O.A5 Research and data
**SCQA.** Outside research has often taken Indigenous knowledge without consent. What rules govern research and data? Key line: Kaupapa Māori research, data sovereignty, consent and benefit.
- IK.O.A5.1 Kaupapa Māori research — research by, with and for Māori, under Māori control (Smith, *Decolonizing Methodologies*, 1999).
- IK.O.A5.2 Data sovereignty — Te Mana Raraunga principles; the CARE principles. The records framework is IS.O.A5.4.
- IK.O.A5.3 Consent and benefit — Wai 262 (*Ko Aotearoa Tēnei*, 2011) on flora, fauna and mātauranga (UNVERIFIED date). Legal effect is LA.

### Heuristics register (outside the count)
Ask who holds the knowledge before asking what it is; "he aha te mea nui o te ao? He tangata" (people first); no placement without the holders.

## 6. Face W — Warrants

The two-mode rule (as in RE): a ·S claim owes W6, W7 or W8 and never W13. A ·M claim owes W13, from whakapapa, kaumātua, tohunga and kawa, and W13 does not travel. W13 never licenses a measurement, a date, a legal duty or a word's meaning in the language, and it does not outrank W3 or W4 in their own cells. Those warrants likewise do not overrule a ·M sentence on its own terms. They are different sentences.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place by the aspect of the knowledge system, then by mode (·S or ·M).
2. A measurement, a dated fact, a legal duty and a word's meaning keep their owners (BI, EA, HI, LA, LN). The tradition may be the record they cite.
3. A claim about rite before atua is RE; a claim about custom in social life is IK.
4. Tikanga recognised by a state court is LA.
5. Rongoā efficacy for a named condition is MD under W3; the rite of rongoā is RE or IK.
6. No residual "Indigenous" cell exists. A claim that fits no IK cell goes to its subject's owner.
7. Māori-led review may withhold any claim.

## 10. External crosswalk
**ANZSRC 45** Indigenous studies has 20 groups (checked in p2_ANZSRC45.md): 4501–4506 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander; 4507–4512 Māori; 4513–4518 Pacific Peoples; 4519 Indigenous data, methodologies and global studies; 4599 a residual.
Each people group pairs a people (substrate) with a topic. The topics land as follows: culture, language and history → IK.A1–A4, with HI and LN; education → ED; environmental knowledge → IK.A2; health and wellbeing → MD; law and governance → IK.A3 and LA; sciences → IK.A2 with BI and EA; 4519 → IK.A5.
Coverage: 19 of 20 land (as substrate × cell); 4599 is refused as a residual.

## 11. Validation
Sizes: key line 5. Face O 5 cells + 16 sub-cells = 21. M roles 5. Heuristics 3. W rows 5, each split ·S/·M. B bridges 6.
Key line, word for word: Ordering principles. Relations to land and waters. Governance and custom. Transmission. Research and data.
CONTESTED: the whole pyramid. One alternative holds that mātauranga is a whole system owning its phenomena (ecology and health stay with the tradition). It is flagged here and not adopted. A second alternative holds that IK should not exist as a pyramid at all, and claims should be split by subject as in p2_ANZSRC45.md. Reviewers decide.
OPEN: who convenes review, and how disagreement between iwi is recorded.
