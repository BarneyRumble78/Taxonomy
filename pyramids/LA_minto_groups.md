# LA Minto groups

## LA.O.A1
- **Legal claims are classified by the kind of legal relation they state, from persons' standing through institutions and territorial law to cross-border and general law.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds follow the structure of the legal field, moving from individual standing to institutions, then domestic law, cross-border law, and law as a whole._
  - **Persons' legal standing covers the rights, status and private-law relations that bind individuals and entities to one another and to the state.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the individual's own entitlements to relations among private parties._
    - **Rights and protections that law confers on persons and entities define the entitlements and immunities one holds against others.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from general entitlements to special exemptions and humanitarian protections._
      - Rights and privileges
      - Privileges and immunities
      - International humanitarian and human rights law
    - **Law and personal identity or status covers the legal relations that attach to family ties, gender, race and ethnicity, and the health of persons.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from family relations to identity groups to health._
      - Family law
      - Law, gender and sexuality (incl. feminist legal scholarship)
      - Race, ethnicity and law
      - Medical and health law
    - **Concepts that state the legal standing of a person or entity in relation to a state or to the law.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from individual status to the status of states._
      - Nationality
      - Legal status
      - Rights of states
    - **Branches of law that govern relations between private parties, such as individuals, businesses and users of digital spaces.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from general private law to specialised fields._
      - Civil law
      - Commercial law
      - Cyberspace law
      - Legal systems
      - Comparative law
  - **Authority and institutions cover the relation between the state and the individual through the settling of disputes, enforcement, practice and the making of law.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from processes of resolution and enforcement to the making and practice of law._
    - **Processes for resolving disputes and enforcing liability address how conflicts between parties are settled or punished.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from informal mediation to arbitration and criminal prosecution._
      - Mediation
      - International arbitration
      - International criminal law
      - Justice
    - **The making, changing and study of law, together with those who practise it, concern how legal rules originate and develop.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from the creation of rules to their reform, history and practitioners._
      - Legislation
      - Law reform
      - Legal history
      - Lawyers
    - **Law and its institutions, technologies and professional practices covers the relations between law and reform, science, education, media and sport.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from legal reform and education to technology, media and sport._
      - Law reform
      - Law, science and technology
      - Legal education
      - Media and communication law
      - Sports law
    - **Concepts that concern the relation between authority and the individual, through the institutions that apply, enforce and uphold law.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the general principle to the institutions that apply it._
      - Administration of justice
      - Law enforcement
      - Legal profession
      - Police
      - Rule of law
  - **Domestic and subject-field law governs social, technological and resource relations within a particular legal system or territory.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from subject fields to territorial bodies of law._
    - **Domestic and sectoral bodies of law regulate particular areas of social and technological relations within a legal system.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from personal relations to technology-related regulation._
      - Family law
      - Computer law
      - Law of space
      - Law of the air
    - **Subject fields of law govern claims arising from a particular domain of human activity or resource, such as water use or work.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Subject-matter fields within domestic law._
      - Water law
      - Labour law
    - **National and regional law states the legal relations that hold within a particular territory of the Americas.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Most specific kind: bounded by territory._
      - America. North America
      - Canada
      - United States
      - Latin America - Mexico and Central America -
      - South America
  - **Cross-border law governs relations between states, international actors and different legal systems, including comparative and customary norms.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from comparative study to international law proper._
    - **Comparative and cross-jurisdictional law concerns relations between different legal systems and regional legal orders.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from general comparison to regional systems and conflicts between systems._
      - International and comparative law
      - Asian and Pacific law
      - Comparative law
      - Conflict of laws (incl. private international law)
      - European Union law
    - **International and comparative law covers the legal relations among states, international organisations and other actors across borders, in trade, oceans, space and general public law.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure of the international law field, from specific regimes to the residual category._
      - International trade and investment law
      - Ocean law and governance
      - Public international law
      - Space, maritime and aviation law
      - International and comparative law not elsewhere classified
    - **Concepts that concern relations between states and the international and customary norms that bind them.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the general field to its instruments and sources._
      - International law
      - International instruments
      - Customary law
    - **International law states the legal relations among states and their shared spaces, such as the seas.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Law between states rather than within one._
      - Law of nations
      - Law of the sea
  - **Law as a whole and in context holds general, theoretical, contextual and residual claims about the nature of law and its setting.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from law in its social setting to general theory and residual categories._
    - **The general law in context category and its residual category hold claims about law in context that fit no more specific subfield.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the parent category to the not-elsewhere-classified residual._
      - Law in context
      - Law in context not elsewhere classified
    - **Law and its social, cultural and intellectual setting covers the relations between law and society, the humanities, religion and theory about the nature of law.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from society and culture to theoretical reflection on law._
      - Law and humanities
      - Law and religion
      - Law and society and socio-legal research
      - Legal theory, jurisprudence and legal interpretation
    - **General and residual categories that study law as a whole, its theory, its systems and its comparison across jurisdictions.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the general field to theory, systems and residual categories._
      - Law
      - Legal theory
      - Other law and legal studies
      - Other law and legal studies not elsewhere classified
    - **Law in general and its theory covers claims about the nature, sources and comparison of law as such, independent of any one jurisdiction or subject.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Most general kind: law considered as a whole._
      - Natural law
      - Law in general. Comparative and uniform law. Jurisprudence
      - LAW IN GENERAL. COMPARATIVE AND UNIFORM LAW. JURISPRUDENCE
      - Periodicals -- Continued

## LA.O.A2
- **Areas of law are distinguished by the kind of legal relation a claim states, such as liability, market dealing, public duty, private obligation or ownership.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure_
  - **State and legal liability concern the relation in which a public authority or other party is answerable for harm or wrongdoing.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from liability of the state to liability generally._
    - State liability
    - Legal liability
  - **Commercial and corporate law governs the relations among businesses, financial institutions, corporations and associations in market activity.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the general field to specific commercial relations._
    - Commercial law
    - Commercial law
    - Corporations and associations law
    - Commercial law not elsewhere classified
  - **Tax and labour law govern the duties that bind individuals and entities to the state and to employers or employees.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from labour relations to tax obligations._
    - Labour law
    - Taxation law
    - Banking, finance and securities law
    - Not-for-profit law
  - **Private law of obligations covers the relations of promise, trust and civil duty that arise between private parties.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the general field to contract and equity._
    - Private law and civil obligations
    - Contract law
    - Contract law
    - Equity and trusts law
    - Private law and civil obligations not elsewhere classified
  - **Property and intellectual property law govern the relation of ownership and exclusive rights over things and creations.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from intellectual property to tangible property._
    - Intellectual property law
    - Intellectual property
    - Property law (excl. intellectual property law)

## LA.O.A3
- **Legal claims about crime and wrongdoing are sorted by the legal relation each claim states, whether public or explanatory.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure. Groups are ordered by structure, from the public state-offender relation to explanatory and private-wrong concepts._
  - **Concepts whose claims state a public relation between the state and an offender, covering how crime is answered, policed, categorized and defined as a public wrong.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, following the path from the public-wrong rule to its enforcement and response._
    - **Legal fields in which the claim states a relation between the state and an offender who has committed a public wrong.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure: general criminal law entries first._
      - Criminal law
      - Criminal law
    - **Crime-type studies examine specific categories of offending defined by the harm done, the offender or the method used.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from technological and environmental crime to organized, state and terrorist offending._
      - Cybercrime
      - Environmental crime
      - Organised crime
      - State crime
      - Terrorism
    - **Social control studies concern the organized actors and tools, public and private, that police and monitor conduct in the name of justice.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from public policing to private security to technological surveillance to justice concerns._
      - Police administration, procedures and practice
      - Private policing and security services
      - Technology, crime and surveillance
      - Crime and social justice
    - **The justice process covers how the state and the injured respond to a crime after it is committed, through sanction, treatment, trial and victim standing.**
      _basis: parts, order: structure. Ordered along the sequence of the justice process._
      - Penal sanctions
      - Courts and sentencing
      - Correctional theory, offender treatment and rehabilitation
      - Victims
  - **Concepts that explain, classify or critique crime and wrongdoing, including the private wrong between individuals in tort, rather than stating a state-versus-offender rule.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from causal explanation to critical approaches to the private wrong relation._
    - **Explanatory criminology states why crime occurs by tracing its causes, theories and social origins.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the general field to specific causal and theoretical approaches._
      - Criminology
      - Causes and prevention of crime
      - Criminological theories
      - Sociological studies of crime
    - **Critical and identity-based approaches examine how power, gender and race shape who is defined as criminal and how they are treated.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the general critical stance to gender and then race-based analyses._
      - Critical approaches to crime
      - Gender and crime
      - Race/ethnicity and crime
    - **Concepts that study or classify crime and wrongdoing, including the private wrong relation between individuals in tort, rather than a state-versus-offender rule.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure: criminological studies first, then tort law._
      - White collar crime
      - Criminology not elsewhere classified
      - Tort law

## LA.O.A4
- **Legal claims are classified by the legal relation they state between individuals, public authorities, and the natural world.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds are arranged from claims about state power over people, to claims about individual standing against the state, to claims about duties and rights concerning nature and resources._
  - **Administrative and general public law covers the claims that govern how public authorities exercise power over people.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from general public law headings to administrative law._
    - Public law
    - Public law
    - Public law not elsewhere classified
    - Administrative law
    - Administrative law
  - **Constitutional and rights law covers claims about the limits of state power and the fundamental rights that individuals hold against the state.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from constitutional foundations to specific rights._
    - Constitutional law
    - Constitutional law
    - Domestic human rights law
    - Privacy and data rights
  - **Status and benefit law covers claims in which individuals seek protection, entitlement, or special status from the state or its forces.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure across the kinds of individual-state relationship._
    - Migration, asylum and refugee law
    - Military law and justice
    - Welfare, insurance, disability and social security law
  - **Environmental protection law covers duties owed to nature, animals, and the climate that the law imposes on people and institutions.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from the general environmental heading to specific protective fields._
    - Environmental and resources law
    - Environmental law
    - Climate change law
    - Animal law
  - **Resource use law covers claims about the rights to extract, use, and allocate energy and natural resources.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure from the specific resource field to the residual category._
    - Mining, energy and natural resources law
    - Environmental and resources law not elsewhere classified

## LA.O.A5
- **Legal-system concepts are divided into four kinds according to the legal relation each claim states.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered structurally from institutions to procedures to activities to field-level categories._
  - **Adjudicating institutions are bodies with authority over parties, deciding legal disputes and applying the law to them.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from general institutions to specific kinds of tribunals and courts._
    - Legal institutions (incl. courts and justice systems)
    - Administrative tribunals
    - Courts
    - International courts
  - **Procedural frameworks are the rules that govern how a case moves through the legal system, binding the parties and the court to set steps.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from general procedure to its civil and criminal branches._
    - Legal procedure
    - Civil procedure
    - Criminal procedure
  - **Claim-pursuing activities are the ways individuals assert, defend and resolve their rights through lawyers, litigation and access to justice.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from access to justice, to the profession, to resolving disputes._
    - Access to justice
    - Legal practice, lawyering and the legal profession
    - Litigation, adjudication and dispute resolution
  - **Field-level categories are the broad umbrella and residual classes, including youth justice, that cover legal systems as a whole.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the overall field to its specialised and residual classes._
    - Legal systems
    - Youth justice
    - Legal systems not elsewhere classified
