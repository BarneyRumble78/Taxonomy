# BU — Business, trade, tax and accounting

Standard v2 cited, not copied. Diagnosis: audit_mind_econ_business.md. v1 source: twelve.txt 3730–3917.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Business knowledge states how firms record, finance, organise and sell, and how they trade and pay tax across borders.
**Complication.** v1 mixed practice with theory. It said Incoterms allocate title (they never do), confused VAT with sales tax, and shared finance theorems with EC.
**Question.** By what dimension can each business claim be placed once, with economic theory in EC and legal duty in LA?
**Answer.** By the practice of the firm the claim is about: its accounts, its finance, its operations and organisation, its marketing, and its trade and tax.

## 2. Governing thought
Business is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five practices of the firm: accounts, finance, operations and organisation, marketing, and trade and tax.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### BU.O.A1 Accounts
**SCQA.** *Situation.* A firm first keeps a record of what it owns, owes and earns. Owners, lenders, managers and tax authorities all read that record. *Complication.* The rules for the record are conventions, set by standard setters, not discovered laws. Different readers need different reports, and someone must check that the record is true. *Question.* What do accounts claim, under which rules, and with what assurance? *Answer.* Accounts claim four things: that the record balances, that reports follow a stated standard, that internal costs and budgets inform decisions, and that an auditor has tested the reports.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: accounting practices; dimension: who the record serves; order: from the ledger, to outside readers, to managers, to the checker). Members (4): The record · Reporting standards · Management accounting · Audit and assurance.

- BU.O.A1.1 **The record: double entry posts every transaction as equal debits and credits, so assets = liabilities + equity.** This is an identity of the convention (W12), not an empirical law.
  - BU.O.A1.1.1 A trial balance that fails to agree proves a recording error. A trial balance that agrees does not prove the absence of error, since omissions and misposted pairs still balance.
  - BU.O.A1.1.2 Accrual accounting recognises income when earned and expense when incurred, not when cash moves. Its hypotheses are a defined period and disclosed estimates such as depreciation.
  - BU.O.A1.1.3 Profit and operating cash flow can differ in sign for the same period. The cash flow statement reconciles them through working capital and non-cash items.
- BU.O.A1.2 **Reporting standards: IFRS (IASB) and US GAAP (FASB) set what general-purpose financial statements must contain.** They differ, for example in that US GAAP permits LIFO inventory and IFRS does not.
  - BU.O.A1.2.1 Both frameworks rest on a conceptual framework of recognition and measurement. The framework guides standard setters; it does not override a specific standard.
  - BU.O.A1.2.2 Measurement bases include historical cost and fair value. Fair value is graded by input level, from quoted prices to unobservable model inputs.
  - BU.O.A1.2.3 Choice of standard is set by the reporting jurisdiction (LA). The content of the standard is BU (uses LA.O.A1.1).
- BU.O.A1.3 **Management accounting: costing, budgeting and variance analysis produce internal reports for decisions, not for outside readers.** No external standard binds them.
  - BU.O.A1.3.1 Cost-volume-profit analysis gives the break-even volume as fixed cost over unit contribution. It holds only where costs split into fixed and variable, and price and unit cost are constant over the range.
  - BU.O.A1.3.2 Activity-based costing assigns overhead by the activities that cause it. It assumes activities and their cost drivers can be identified and measured.
  - BU.O.A1.3.3 Variance analysis splits the gap between actual and budget into price and quantity parts. Its findings are only as sound as the budget standard.
- BU.O.A1.4 **Audit and assurance: an audit gives reasonable, not absolute, assurance that statements are free of material misstatement (W11).** Materiality is a judgement about what would change a reader's decision.
  - BU.O.A1.4.1 The audit risk model plans testing from inherent, control and detection risk. It is a planning aid, not a measurement, and assumes the three risks are assessed separately.
  - BU.O.A1.4.2 Statistical audit sampling projects misstatement from a sample to the population. It requires random selection and a tolerable misstatement set in advance.
  - BU.O.A1.4.3 Auditor independence is a precondition of the opinion. Its legal duty is LA; its content as a practice standard is BU.

Named objects: double-entry identity, accrual basis, trial balance, cash flow statement, IFRS, US GAAP, conceptual framework, fair value hierarchy, cost-volume-profit analysis (fixed/variable split; constant price and unit cost), activity-based costing (identifiable cost drivers), variance analysis (realistic standard), audit risk model (separable risks), statistical audit sampling (random selection; preset tolerable misstatement), materiality.

### BU.O.A2 Finance
**SCQA.** *Situation.* Firms raise and invest capital at a price set by markets. *Complication.* The theorems that price capital are mathematical results under strong hypotheses (MA.B.C4). Equilibrium market prices are economics (EC.O.A2). What is left is the firm's use of these results. *Question.* How should a firm value projects, choose its funding, price risk and read market prices? *Answer.* By five practices: valuation, capital structure choice, risk pricing, derivative pricing and the reading of market efficiency, each stated with its hypotheses.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: finance decisions; dimension: what the firm prices; order: a project, the firm's funding, risk, contingent claims, then the market as a whole). Members (5): Valuation · Capital structure · Risk and return · Derivatives · Market efficiency.

- BU.O.A2.1 **Valuation: a project is worth the net present value of its cash flows at the opportunity cost of capital.** Discounted cash flow assumes the forecast and the rate.
  - BU.O.A2.1.1 Net present value and internal rate of return agree on accept-or-reject for a single conventional project. They can rank mutually exclusive projects differently when scale or timing differ.
  - BU.O.A2.1.2 Terminal value often carries most of a discounted cash flow valuation. The result is therefore sensitive to the assumed long-run growth rate.
  - BU.O.A2.1.3 Real options analysis values the right to delay, expand or abandon. It assumes the option can be priced from a traded or replicating asset (uses MA.B.C4). CONTESTED for non-traded projects.
- BU.O.A2.2 **Capital structure: Modigliani–Miller states that, with no taxes, no bankruptcy costs, symmetric information and perfect capital markets, firm value does not depend on the debt–equity mix.** With corporate tax, debt adds a tax shield. Theorem MA.B.C4; use BU.
  - BU.O.A2.2.1 Trade-off theory sets the debt level where the marginal tax shield equals marginal expected distress cost. Its prediction of target leverage has mixed empirical support (W4).
  - BU.O.A2.2.2 Pecking-order theory predicts firms prefer internal funds, then debt, then equity, under information asymmetry (uses EC.O.A2.3). Tests give partial support.
  - BU.O.A2.2.3 The weighted average cost of capital discounts firm cash flows only if leverage is held at a constant target ratio.
- BU.O.A2.3 **Risk and return: CAPM states that, with mean–variance investors, homogeneous expectations, frictionless markets and unlimited risk-free borrowing and lending, expected excess return is linear in beta.** Empirical support is weak (W4 defeaters).
  - BU.O.A2.3.1 Mean–variance portfolio selection finds the lowest-variance mix for a target return. It assumes means and covariances are known; estimation error degrades it in practice.
  - BU.O.A2.3.2 Multi-factor models add size, value and other factors to market beta. Which factors are priced, and why, is CONTESTED between risk and behavioural accounts.
  - BU.O.A2.3.3 Value at risk gives a loss level exceeded with a stated small probability. It assumes a return model stable over the horizon and says nothing about loss beyond that level.
- BU.O.A2.4 **Derivatives: Black–Scholes prices a European option assuming log-normal prices, constant volatility and rate, continuous frictionless trading and no dividends.** Theorem MA.B.C4; use BU.
  - BU.O.A2.4.1 Put–call parity links European put and call prices on the same asset, strike and expiry. It needs only no arbitrage, not a price model.
  - BU.O.A2.4.2 Implied volatility differs across strikes for the same expiry (the smile). This is evidence against the constant-volatility hypothesis.
  - BU.O.A2.4.3 Hedging with derivatives reduces a stated exposure at the cost of basis risk. Hedge accounting rules are BU.O.A1.2.
- BU.O.A2.5 **Market efficiency: the efficient-market hypothesis comes in weak, semi-strong and strong forms.** Tests face the joint-hypothesis problem: each needs a model of normal returns.
  - BU.O.A2.5.1 An event study measures the abnormal price response to an announcement. It assumes a known event date, no other news in the window, and a model of normal returns.
  - BU.O.A2.5.2 Documented anomalies, such as momentum, are evidence against some form of efficiency or against the normal-return model. Which one is the joint-hypothesis problem. CONTESTED.
  - BU.O.A2.5.3 Duration measures a bond's price sensitivity to yield. It holds for small parallel shifts in the yield curve.

Named objects: net present value (forecast cash flows; risk-adjusted rate), internal rate of return, terminal value, real options, Modigliani–Miller propositions (uses MA.B.C4), trade-off theory, pecking-order theory, WACC (constant target leverage), CAPM (mean–variance investors; homogeneous expectations; frictionless markets), mean–variance portfolio selection (known moments), factor models, value at risk (stable return model), Black–Scholes model (uses MA.B.C4), put–call parity (no arbitrage), implied volatility, efficient-market hypothesis, event study (known date; clean window; normal-return model), duration (small parallel shifts).

### BU.O.A3 Operations and organisation
**SCQA.** *Situation.* Firms turn inputs into outputs through people and processes. *Complication.* Some operations claims are theorems (MA.O.A5), some are empirical regularities, and some are frameworks. Behaviour inside firms belongs to MS, rivals' responses to ST, and contract theory to EC. *Question.* What claims about running processes and organisations does business own? *Answer.* The firm's practice of operating processes, managing supply chains, designing structure, managing people and founding new ventures.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: operating practices; dimension: what the firm organises; order: the process, the chain of firms, the structure, the people, then new firms). Members (5): Operations · Supply chains · Organisation design · People management · Entrepreneurship and innovation.

- BU.O.A3.1 **Operations: Little's law L = λW holds for any stable queueing system in steady state (theorem MA.O.A5).** Its use to size capacity and work in progress is BU.
  - BU.O.A3.1.1 In an M/M/1 queue, waiting time rises without bound as utilisation approaches one. This needs Poisson arrivals, exponential service and one server (uses MA.O.A5).
  - BU.O.A3.1.2 Theory of constraints claims throughput is set by a single bottleneck at any time. Gains elsewhere do not raise output until the bottleneck moves.
  - BU.O.A3.1.3 Statistical process control charts signal drift from a stable baseline. They assume independent samples and a baseline in statistical control.
- BU.O.A3.2 **Supply chains: inventory and logistics decisions link firms, and the bullwhip effect amplifies demand variance upstream.** The effect arises when each stage forecasts from the orders it receives and lead times are positive.
  - BU.O.A3.2.1 The economic order quantity minimises ordering plus holding cost. It assumes constant known demand, fixed order cost, constant holding cost and no shortages.
  - BU.O.A3.2.2 The newsvendor model sets one-period stock where the probability of meeting demand equals the critical ratio. It needs a known demand distribution and known shortage and surplus costs.
  - BU.O.A3.2.3 Sharing point-of-sale data upstream reduces the bullwhip effect where forecasting from orders was its cause.
- BU.O.A3.3 **Organisation design: structure, governance and agency set who decides and who monitors.** The agency problem in principal–agent form is EC.O.A2.3; its corporate remedy is BU.
  - BU.O.A3.3.1 Boards, audit committees and executive pay are corporate remedies for agency costs. Their legal form is LA; their design is BU.
  - BU.O.A3.3.2 The make-or-buy boundary of the firm draws on transaction cost economics (uses EC.O.A3.3). The firm's sourcing decision is BU.
  - BU.O.A3.3.3 Contingency theory claims no single structure is best; fit depends on environment, size and technology. CONTESTED as weakly testable.
- BU.O.A3.4 **People management: human resources and industrial relations govern hiring, pay, safety and bargaining.** Group behaviour is MS.
  - BU.O.A3.4.1 Structured interviews and validated tests predict job performance better than unstructured interviews. This holds where a job analysis defines what is assessed.
  - BU.O.A3.4.2 The hierarchy of controls ranks safety measures from removing the hazard to protective equipment. It is a ranking convention (W12), not an empirical law.
  - BU.O.A3.4.3 Employment law and collective bargaining duties are LA (uses LA.O.A2.1). The firm's pay and bargaining practice is BU.
- BU.O.A3.5 **Entrepreneurship and innovation: founding and scaling firms is a practice of the firm.** A rival's response to a move is ST.
  - BU.O.A3.5.1 The Bass diffusion model forecasts adoption from innovation and imitation coefficients. It assumes a fixed market potential and one adoption per buyer.
  - BU.O.A3.5.2 The resource-based view claims sustained advantage needs resources that are valuable, rare, hard to imitate and organised for use (VRIO). It is a framework with weak predictive tests.
  - BU.O.A3.5.3 New-firm survival falls with age in most national data. The rate differs by country and sector (UNVERIFIED for NZ figures).

Named objects: Little's law (uses MA.O.A5; stable system, steady state), M/M/1 queue (Poisson arrivals; exponential service; one server), theory of constraints (single bottleneck), control charts (stable baseline; independent samples), bullwhip effect (order-based forecasts; positive lead times), economic order quantity (constant demand; fixed order cost; no shortages), newsvendor model (one period; known distribution and costs), board governance, make-or-buy decision, contingency theory, hierarchy of controls, structured selection, Bass diffusion model (fixed potential; single adoption), resource-based view.

### BU.O.A4 Marketing
**SCQA.** *Situation.* Firms must find and keep customers. *Complication.* Customer cognition is MS and the equilibrium price is EC. Many marketing claims are frameworks without stated hypotheses. *Question.* What testable claims do marketing practices make? *Answer.* Claims about who the customers are and what they are worth, how products and brands are valued, how prices are set in practice, and how goods reach buyers.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: marketing practices; dimension: what the firm offers the customer; order: the customer, the offer, its price, then the route to market). Members (4): Customers · Products and brands · Pricing practice · Channels.

- BU.O.A4.1 **Customers: segmentation and consumer behaviour group buyers by need and response.** The cognition is MS.
  - BU.O.A4.1.1 Customer lifetime value discounts expected future margin from a customer. It assumes stable or forecast retention and margin and a chosen discount rate.
  - BU.O.A4.1.2 A/B testing compares two versions by random assignment. It assumes no interference between users and an adequate sample (W3).
  - BU.O.A4.1.3 Segmentation is useful only if segments differ in response and can be reached separately. This is a testable condition, not a definition.
- BU.O.A4.2 **Products and brands: firms design offers as bundles of attributes and build brands that buyers recognise.** Trade mark protection is LA.
  - BU.O.A4.2.1 Conjoint analysis estimates the value of each attribute from stated choices. It assumes the product splits into additive attributes and stated choices reflect real ones.
  - BU.O.A4.2.2 SERVQUAL scores service quality on five dimensions. Its fit to a given service is an empirical question; the five-factor structure is CONTESTED.
- BU.O.A4.3 **Pricing practice: firms set list prices, discounts and fare classes.** Market equilibrium price is EC.
  - BU.O.A4.3.1 Price elasticity of demand measures how quantity responds to price. It must be estimated in the relevant price range with other influences controlled (uses EC.O.A1.1).
  - BU.O.A4.3.2 Littlewood's rule protects high-fare capacity while the expected high-fare revenue exceeds the low fare. It assumes fixed perishable capacity and low-fare demand arriving first.
  - BU.O.A4.3.3 The Van Westendorp meter gives an acceptable price range from survey answers. It assumes respondents know the category and stated answers reflect behaviour.
- BU.O.A4.4 **Channels: retail, digital and distribution routes carry goods and services to buyers.** Channel choice trades reach against margin and control.
  - BU.O.A4.4.1 Adding a direct channel can reduce sales through existing intermediaries. The size of this effect is an empirical question for each market.
  - BU.O.A4.4.2 Digital attribution assigns credit for a sale across touchpoints. Rule-based attribution is a convention; causal claims need experiments (BU.O.A4.1.2).

Named objects: customer lifetime value (stable retention and margin), A/B test (random assignment; no interference), segmentation, conjoint analysis (additive attributes; valid stated choices), SERVQUAL, price elasticity (uses EC.O.A1.1), Littlewood's rule (fixed perishable capacity; low-fare first), Van Westendorp meter, channel conflict, attribution models.

### BU.O.A5 Trade and tax
**SCQA.** *Situation.* Firms sell across borders and owe tax. *Complication.* The rules come from private bodies (ICC), treaties and states. Legal force is LA and incidence is EC. v1 confused Incoterms with title and VAT with sales tax. *Question.* What does the firm's practice of trade and tax claim? *Answer.* It claims how delivery risk and cost are allocated, how goods are classified at the border, how tax is computed, how related parties price their dealings, and how cross-border payment is secured.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: cross-border practices; dimension: what the firm must settle at or after the border; order: delivery, entry, tax, related-party price, then payment). Members (5): Trade terms · Customs and trade compliance · Tax computation · Transfer pricing · Payments and trade finance.

- BU.O.A5.1 **Trade terms: Incoterms 2020 (ICC, eleven rules) allocate delivery tasks, costs and the passing of risk between seller and buyer.** They do not allocate title. Title passes under the sale contract and the governing law (LA).
  - BU.O.A5.1.1 Incoterms apply only when the contract incorporates them. They are a private convention (W12), not law.
  - BU.O.A5.1.2 Under the F- and C-group rules, risk passes at shipment. Under C-group rules the seller still pays main carriage (uses LA.O.A2.1).
- BU.O.A5.2 **Customs and trade compliance: tariff classification (Harmonized System, W12) and rules of origin decide the duty a good attracts.** Their legal force is LA.
  - BU.O.A5.2.1 The Harmonized System classifies goods at six digits internationally. National schedules add further digits.
  - BU.O.A5.2.2 Preferential tariffs under a trade agreement apply only to goods meeting its rules of origin. Firms must hold evidence of origin.
- BU.O.A5.3 **Tax computation: a VAT is charged at each stage on the value added, with credit for input tax; a retail sales tax is charged once at the final sale.** New Zealand GST is a VAT-type tax (15%).
  - BU.O.A5.3.1 Under a credit-invoice VAT, tax collected equals tax on final sales if every stage claims credit. Exemptions break the chain.
  - BU.O.A5.3.2 Taxable income differs from accounting profit through timing and permanent differences. Deferred tax accounting records the timing part (uses BU.O.A1.2).
  - BU.O.A5.3.3 Who bears a tax is EC (uses EC.O.A2.5). The duty to pay is LA (uses LA.O.A2.5). The computation is BU.
- BU.O.A5.4 **Transfer pricing: related parties must price their dealings at arm's length (OECD guidelines, W12).** Its legal force is LA.
  - BU.O.A5.4.1 The comparable uncontrolled price method assumes sufficiently comparable independent transactions exist. Where none do, profit-based methods are used.
  - BU.O.A5.4.2 Advance pricing agreements fix a method with a tax authority ahead of time. Their binding effect is LA.
- BU.O.A5.5 **Payments and trade finance: letters of credit (UCP 600, W12) secure payment against documents.** Banks deal in documents, not goods.
  - BU.O.A5.5.1 Under a documentary credit, the bank pays on documents that comply on their face. Discrepant documents permit refusal.
  - BU.O.A5.5.2 Open account trading shifts payment risk to the seller. Credit insurance or factoring can transfer it.

Named objects: Incoterms 2020 (contractual incorporation), Harmonized System, rules of origin, credit-invoice VAT, retail sales tax, NZ GST, deferred tax, arm's-length principle, comparable uncontrolled price method (comparables exist), advance pricing agreement, UCP 600, documentary credit, factoring.
