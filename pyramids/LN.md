# LN — Language

Standard v2 cited, not copied. Built up from prototype_history_language.md; its five members are kept.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Linguistics states how human languages are produced, built, understood, varied and changed. It is taught by levels: phonetics through pragmatics, plus sociolinguistics and historical linguistics.
**Complication.** v1 had no home for language. PL owned meaning as a concept, MS owned processing and CS owned language models. No pyramid owned the grammar of a language, its sound system or its history.
**Question.** What partition places every claim about language once, with stated borders to PL, MS, CS and AR?
**Answer.** Place a claim by the aspect of language it is about: signal, form, meaning, variation in use, or diversity.

## 2. Governing thought
Language is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five aspects of language: signal, form, meaning, variation in use, and diversity.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the aspect of language the sentence is about.** Order: from the signal to the family of languages.
1. Signal
2. Form
3. Meaning

A particular language is a catalogue entry on the same five cells. There is no "exotic languages" bucket.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### LN.O.A1 Signal
**SCQA.** Language is carried by speech or sign. How is the signal produced, perceived and organised? Key line: phonetics, phonology, sign phonology.
- LN.O.A1.1 Phonetics — articulation, acoustics, perception. Source–filter theory of speech (Fant, 1960) cites PH acoustics.
- LN.O.A1.2 Phonology — the phoneme and the minimal pair; distinctive features (Chomsky and Halle, *The Sound Pattern of English*, 1968); Optimality Theory (Prince and Smolensky, 1993).
- LN.O.A1.3 Sign phonology — handshape, location and movement as parameters (Stokoe, 1960). Sign languages are full natural languages.
- LN.O.A1.4 Writing systems — the IPA is a convention (W12); orthography is not sound.

### LN.O.A2 Form
**SCQA.** Signals combine into words and sentences by rules. How are they built? Key line: morphology, syntax, the lexicon.
- LN.O.A2.1 Morphology — morphemes; inflection versus derivation.
- LN.O.A2.2 Syntax — constituency and recursion; dependency versus phrase structure; agreement.
- LN.O.A2.3 The lexicon — word classes, argument structure.
- LN.O.A2.4 Formal grammars — the Chomsky hierarchy is MA.O.A1; LN uses it to class natural-language phenomena. Whether natural languages are mildly context-sensitive is CONTESTED.

### LN.O.A3 Meaning
**SCQA.** Forms carry meaning, alone and in context. What do expressions mean? Key line: lexical semantics, compositional semantics, pragmatics.
- LN.O.A3.1 Lexical semantics — sense relations; Saussure's arbitrariness of the sign (*Cours*, 1916).
- LN.O.A3.2 Compositional semantics — Frege's principle (concept cited from PL); Montague grammar.
- LN.O.A3.3 Pragmatics — Grice's cooperative principle and implicature (concept PL, empirical patterns LN); presupposition; deixis; politeness.
- LN.O.A3.4 Grammaticalised meaning — e.g. te reo Māori pronouns distinguish dual from plural, and inclusive from exclusive.

### LN.O.A4 Variation in use
**SCQA.** Every language varies by speaker, group and situation. How and why? Key line: social variation, register, contact and multilingualism, endangerment and revitalisation.
- LN.O.A4.1 Social variation — the sociolinguistic variable (Labov, 1966).
- LN.O.A4.2 Register and genre — situational variation; diglossia (Ferguson, 1959).
- LN.O.A4.3 Contact and multilingualism — code-switching; pidgins and creoles.
- LN.O.A4.4 Endangerment and revitalisation — language shift. Te reo Māori: kōhanga reo from 1982; the Māori Language Act 1987 made it an official language of New Zealand.

### LN.O.A5 Diversity
**SCQA.** Languages change, descend from ancestors and differ in structure. Key line: change, descent and families, typology and universals.
- LN.O.A5.1 Change — regular sound change (the Neogrammarian hypothesis, 1870s); Grimm's law (1822); Verner's law (1875); grammaticalisation.
- LN.O.A5.2 Descent and families — the comparative method; Proto-Indo-European; Austronesian, which includes te reo Māori.
- LN.O.A5.3 Typology and universals — Greenberg's universals (1963), most of them statistical tendencies rather than exceptionless laws.
- LN.O.A5.4 Origin of language — OPEN.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. The system is linguistics; the process is mind (MS).
2. Meaning as a concept is PL; meaning in a language is LN.
3. Formal languages are MA; their use to class natural languages is LN.
4. Language models are CS; whether their outputs show a grammatical generalisation is LN.
5. The poem is AR; the metrical phonology it uses is LN.
6. A scripture's doctrinal sense is RE; its grammar is LN.
7. A word in te reo is LN; its tradition-internal meaning in tikanga is IK.
