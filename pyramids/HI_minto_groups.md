## HI.O.A5
- **Heritage, archive and museum studies divides into four sub-areas, each making a different assertion about how the human past is stored, kept, experienced or remembered.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure. Four sub-areas of the field, each distinguished by what it asserts about the human past._
  - **Heritage, archive and museum studies is the overall field that contains the specialised sub-areas of preserving and presenting the past.**
    _basis: parts, order: structure. Parent field and its residual category._
    - Heritage, archive and museum studies
    - Heritage, archive and museum studies not elsewhere classified
  - **Archival, critical and collections-based studies treat records, museum collections and their interpretation as the stored evidence of the human past.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from records, to critique, to collections._
    - Archival, repository and related studies
    - Critical heritage, museum and archive studies
    - Heritage collections and interpretations
    - Museums. Collectors and collecting
  - **Management, conservation and digital approaches assert that the human past survives only through active care, protection and technical preservation of its remains.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from management, to conservation, to materials, to digital._
    - Cultural heritage management (incl. world heritage)
    - Heritage and cultural conservation
    - Materials conservation
    - Digital heritage
  - **Tourism, visitor and intangible heritage concern how the past is experienced, performed and passed on to living audiences.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from audiences to intangible practices._
    - Heritage tourism, visitor and audience studies
    - Intangible heritage
  - **Collective memory, mythology and heraldry represent the past through shared remembered stories and inherited emblems of lineage rather than through documented evidence.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from most diffuse shared memory, to narrative myth, to formalised heraldic symbols._
    - Collective memory
    - Mythology
    - Heraldry
    - HERALDRY
    - Heraldry
