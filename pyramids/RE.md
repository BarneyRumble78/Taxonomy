Repairs made here: "two sides" wording replaced by four faces; key line restated as one plural noun on one dimension; every named object given a stable ID; one W row per Face O cell, A-level and sub-cell; the six bridges given B-table rows; HI no longer marked provisional (now prototyped); philosophy of religion pointed at PL's rebuilt IDs (PL.O.A1.1, PL.O.A2.2); ANZSRC 50 crosswalk added; Māori claims cross-referenced to T/p2_ANZSRC45.md.

**Two-mode rule (binding, from the prototype).** Every RE sentence carries one mode. **·S (study)** owes W6, W7 or W8. **·T (theology)** owes W13, with W9 or W8. The ID is cell plus mode: RE.O.A1.1·T. W13 appears only in ·T.
- **RE-1 Tag.** Every RE sentence carries ·S or ·T.
- **RE-2 Separate warrants.** W13 never warrants a ·S sentence. W6 or W7 alone never warrants a ·T sentence.
- **RE-3 Report is not assertion.** A ·S sentence may report what a tradition teaches. Reporting is not adopting.
- **RE-4 Premises cross one way, labelled.** A ·T sentence may cite a ·S result as a premise. It must name the authority that makes the premise bear on doctrine.
- **RE-5 One sentence, one mode.** A sentence needing both modes is two sentences.
- **RE-6 Methodological agnosticism.** ·S brackets the truth of the ultimate claim (Smart; Berger's "methodological atheism", 1967, UNVERIFIED date). A ·S sentence that denies a doctrine has become a PL sentence and owes W9.

## 1. SCQA

**Situation.** Universities treat religion in two ways. Religious studies describes and compares religions from outside any one of them. Theology argues within a tradition from its authorities. **Complication.** No other pyramid owns either mode. PL declines religion as practice; MS files religion as a sector. LCC divides Christianity by topic into five subclasses but other traditions by name. A "world religions" bucket appears where schemes run out. **Question.** Which single partition places every religious claim exactly once, without merging the warrant of study with the warrant of faith? **Answer.** Four objects of the claim, each in two modes, with the tradition as substrate and the discipline as bridge.

## 2. Governing thought

Religion and theology is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges. Its objects are claims about four objects of the claim: the ultimate and the human condition, authoritative text, practice, and community and institution.

## 3. Key line

- **Logic.** Inductive.
- **Plural noun.** objects of the claim.
- **Dimension.** What the sentence is about.
- **Order.** Structural, from the referent to its carriers: what is ultimate, the text that discloses it, the practice that answers it, the community that carries it.

1. The ultimate and the human condition
2. Authoritative text
3. Practice
4. Community and institution

**MECE test.** Exclusive: by the subject rule, each sentence has one subject. "Genesis 1 is a priestly composition" is about a text (A2). "God created from nothing" is about the ultimate (A1), even when its warrant is a text. Exhaustive: religious experience is A3.2 as discipline, or MS.O.A1 as mental process. Sacred place is A1.2 (status), A3.1 (use in rite) or A4.1 (governance). Ethics and religious law are A3.3. Oral text is inside A2, which prevents a bias toward written scripture.

**CONTESTED (order).** An anthropological order would run community first. The order is structural, not a ranking.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### RE.O.A1 The ultimate and the human condition

**SCQA.** *S.* Traditions make claims about God, gods, Brahman, emptiness or atua, about the world's dependence, and about human ends. *C.* Theism, nondualism and Buddhist analysis look like rival departments. They answer three questions in common. *Q.* Which doctrinal questions are distinct? *A.* Three, by the relatum of the claim.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: doctrinal questions · dimension: the relatum of the claim · order: structural, the ultimate, the world, the person.
1. **RE.O.A1.1 The ultimate itself** claims what is ultimate and what may be said of it.
2. **RE.O.A1.2 The world's relation to the ultimate** claims creation, emanation, cosmogony, providence, revelation and sacred status.
3. **RE.O.A1.3 The human end** claims the human condition and its goal: sin and salvation, bondage and liberation, death and after.

**Named objects.**
- **RE.O.A1.1.1 The Trinity**, defined at Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381). As true: ·T. Its conciliar history: ·S.
- **RE.O.A1.1.2 Tawḥīd and the divine attributes.** Ashʿarī, Māturīdī and Muʿtazilī answers differ.
- **RE.O.A1.1.3 Createdness of the Qurʾan** and the *miḥna* (from 833, UNVERIFIED date). Muʿtazilīs held it created; Ashʿarīs uncreated.
- **RE.O.A1.1.4 Apophatic theology.** God is known by negation. Pseudo-Dionysius (c. 500); Maimonides' negative attributes.
- **RE.O.A1.1.5 Nirguṇa and saguṇa Brahman.** Advaita (Śaṅkara, c. 8th century) against Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja).
- **RE.O.A1.1.6 Śūnyatā** (Nāgārjuna, c. 2nd century) as a Madhyamaka claim. Its argument alone, from shared premises, is PL.
- **RE.O.A1.1.7 Ein Sof** in Kabbalah.
- **RE.O.A1.1.8 Io as supreme being** in some Māori traditions (·S). CONTESTED: its pre-contact antiquity is disputed. See T/p2_ANZSRC45.md.
- **RE.O.A1.1.9 Otto's numinous** (*Das Heilige*, 1917, UNVERIFIED date) as a ·S theory of the sacred.
- **RE.O.A1.2.1 Creation ex nihilo.**
- **RE.O.A1.2.2 Whakapapa cosmogony** from Ranginui and Papatūānuku. Human descent from a named tupuna is HI.O.A1.
- **RE.O.A1.2.3 Tapu and noa** as statuses of persons, places and things.
- **RE.O.A1.3.1 Karma and saṃsāra; mokṣa; nirvāṇa.**
- **RE.O.A1.3.2 Grace and synergy.** Salvation by grace alone against cooperation of grace and will.
- **RE.O.A1.3.3 The beatific vision.**
- **RE.O.A1.0.1 The axial age** (Jaspers, 1949: roughly 8th to 3rd centuries BCE). A ·S comparative claim (W6) of a shift toward transcendence. CONTESTED: critics find no common denominator and no clean break. Alternative home: HI.O.A2 (periodisation). Filed at A-level because it spans A1.1–A1.3.

### RE.O.A2 Authoritative text

The cell covers written and oral text alike.

**SCQA.** *S.* Traditions hold some texts as scripture or authoritative tradition; some are written, some recited. *C.* Canon studies, textual criticism and exegesis are separate fields. They follow one text through its life. *Q.* Which text claims are distinct? *A.* Three, by stage of the text's life.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: text claims · dimension: the stage of the text's life · order: chronological, status, transmission, interpretation.
1. **RE.O.A2.1 Canon and status** claims which texts count as authoritative, and in what rank.
2. **RE.O.A2.2 Transmission and establishment** claims how the text was composed, transmitted and fixed.
3. **RE.O.A2.3 Interpretation** claims what the text means.

Order note: canon is listed first because rank is what makes a text an object here. CONTESTED: historically, composition precedes canon; the alternative order is A2.2, A2.1, A2.3.

**Named objects.**
- **RE.O.A2.1.1 Biblical canons.** The Tanakh; Protestant, Catholic (with deuterocanonical books) and Ethiopian Orthodox canons differ in extent.
- **RE.O.A2.1.2 Śruti and smṛti.** Heard and remembered texts in Hindu traditions.
- **RE.O.A2.1.3 Pāli Tipiṭaka**, written down in Sri Lanka c. 1st century BCE; the Chinese and Tibetan canons.
- **RE.O.A2.2.1 The ʿUthmānic codex** (c. 650) and the *qirāʾāt* (canonical readings).
- **RE.O.A2.2.2 Ḥadīth and *isnād* criticism.**
- **RE.O.A2.2.3 Dead Sea Scrolls** (found from 1947) as textual witnesses (·S).
- **RE.O.A2.2.4 Mishnah** (c. 200 CE) and the Jerusalem and Babylonian Talmuds.
- **RE.O.A2.2.5 Vedic oral transmission** by fixed recitation patterns.
- **RE.O.A2.2.6 Karakia and kōrero tuku iho** as oral text, transmitted in whare wānanga. Placement subject to Māori-led review (T/p2_ANZSRC45.md).
- **RE.O.A2.3.1 Four senses of scripture:** literal, allegorical, moral and anagogical. Origen gave the first three; Cassian added the anagogical. As a claim about how scripture means: ·T. Its use as a procedure is RE.M.B2.
- **RE.O.A2.3.2 Tafsīr *bi'l-maʾthūr* and *bi'l-raʾy*:** by transmitted report and by reasoned opinion.
- **RE.O.A2.3.3 Midrash and PaRDeS.**
- **RE.O.A2.3.4 Mīmāṃsā rules** of Vedic interpretation.

### RE.O.A3 Practice

**SCQA.** *S.* Adherents worship, pray, fast, meditate, keep law and live by codes. *C.* Liturgics, spirituality, ethics and religious law are separate departments. *Q.* Which practice claims are distinct? *A.* Three, by whom the act addresses.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: practice claims · dimension: whom the act addresses · order: structural, the sacred, the self, others.
1. **RE.O.A3.1 Worship and rite** claims acts addressed to the sacred.
2. **RE.O.A3.2 Discipline and formation** claims acts addressed to transforming the self.
3. **RE.O.A3.3 Conduct and law** claims acts addressed to others, and the norms that govern them.

The dimension is attested inside traditions. Fiqh divides *ʿibādāt* (worship) from *muʿāmalāt* (dealings). Jewish thought divides duties toward God from duties toward others. The pyramid uses the division as a dimension, not as an authority. An act addressed to two is filed by the claim's subject. Ramadan fasting as a duty toward God is A3.1; as self-formation it is A3.2. CONTESTED: the alternative files such acts once, under the tradition's own primary label.

**Named objects.**
- **RE.O.A3.1.1 Eucharist and sacrament.**
- **RE.O.A3.1.2 Ṣalāt and ḥajj.**
- **RE.O.A3.1.3 Pūjā and śrauta ritual.**
- **RE.O.A3.1.4 Karakia in performance; pōwhiri; tangihanga.** Subject to Māori-led review.
- **RE.O.A3.1.5 Rites of passage** (van Gennep, 1909, UNVERIFIED date): separation, transition, incorporation. A ·S comparative claim.
- **RE.O.A3.1.6 Veneration of icons** and the ruling of Nicaea II (787). The icon's form and making are AR.O.A1 and AR.O.A2.
- **RE.O.A3.2.1 Meditation** (*śamatha*, *vipaśyanā*); yoga; dhikr. Its neural correlates are MS.O.A2.
- **RE.O.A3.2.2 The Rule of Benedict** (c. 530); musar.
- **RE.O.A3.2.3 Bhakti** as devotional discipline.
- **RE.O.A3.3.1 Fiqh rulings in *muʿāmalāt*.**
- **RE.O.A3.3.2 Halakha** in the Shulchan Aruch (printed 1565, UNVERIFIED date); dharmaśāstra.
- **RE.O.A3.3.3 Natural law in Aquinas** (*Summa* I–II q. 94) as moral theology (·T). The same argument from shared premises is PL.O.A4.
- **RE.O.A3.3.4 Kashrut and halal.** Filed by subject: as worship A3.1, as conduct A3.3.
- **RE.O.A3.3.5 Tikanga as correct practice** before atua and people; manaakitanga as obligation to guests. Tikanga as part of NZ law is LA.O.A1 (*Ellis v R* [2022] NZSC 114).

### RE.O.A4 Community and institution

**SCQA.** *S.* Religions are carried by communities with offices, members and histories. *C.* Ecclesiology, church history and sociology of religion overlap. The tradition's own order differs from the general sociology of organizations. *Q.* Which community claims are distinct? *A.* Three, by what holds the community together.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: community claims · dimension: what holds the community together · order: structural, authority, boundary, continuity.
1. **RE.O.A4.1 Authority and office** claims who may teach, rule, ordain or decide.
2. **RE.O.A4.2 Membership and boundary** claims who belongs, and what puts a member or teaching outside.
3. **RE.O.A4.3 Continuity and division** claims succession, lineage, reform and schism.

**Named objects.**
- **RE.O.A4.1.1 Papacy and councils.**
- **RE.O.A4.1.2 The ʿulamāʾ and the four Sunni *madhhabs*; the Twelver imamate.**
- **RE.O.A4.1.3 The rabbinate and the *posek*.**
- **RE.O.A4.1.4 Saṅgha and *vinaya*** as the monastic community's rule. Its membership rules are filed at A4.2 by subject.
- **RE.O.A4.1.5 Tohunga and kaumātua; marae *kawa***, which varies by iwi and hapū. Subject to Māori-led review.
- **RE.O.A4.2.1 Excommunication, *ḥerem*, *takfīr*.**
- **RE.O.A4.2.2 Orthodoxy and heresy.** In ·S, insider labels are marked as reported.
- **RE.O.A4.3.1 Apostolic succession.**
- **RE.O.A4.3.2 Guru-śiṣya *paramparā*; Śaṅkara's *maṭhas*.**
- **RE.O.A4.3.3 The East–West schism (1054); the Reformation (from 1517); the Sunni–Shīʿa division after 632** (·S).
- **RE.O.A4.3.4 Ringatū** (Te Kooti, c. 1868, UNVERIFIED date) and **the Rātana Church** (1925, UNVERIFIED date) as Māori Christian communities.
- **RE.O.A4.3.5 The "gate of ijtihād closed" thesis**, rejected as history by Hallaq, "Was the Gate of Ijtihad Closed?" (*IJMES* 16, 1984) (·S).

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place by the subject of the sentence (the ultimate, the text, the practice, the community), then by mode.
2. ·S and ·T are never merged. The same sentence, asserted as true within a tradition, is ·T. Reported as held, it is ·S.
3. An argument from premises any reasoner may grant (cosmological, ontological, problem of evil) is PL. The same argument from scripture is RE ·T.
4. The dated event of a tradition's past is HI, cited by RE ·S.
5. A civil duty arising from a religion is LA. A canon-internal duty is RE ·T.
6. The neural or psychological process of religious experience is MS. The discipline as practice is RE.O.A3.2.
7. A measurement offered as doctrine is refused. Its measurement goes to its science; the doctrine stays in ·T without the measurement's warrant.
8. Comparison does not inherit any canon's W13.
9. Māori and other Indigenous religious claims follow p2_ANZSRC45.md and need Māori-led review.
10. Pilot repair (claim 19). A dated event in a text's history (when the Mishnah was redacted) is HI.O.A1. RE.O.A2.2 keeps claims about the transmitted text as text: variants, recensions and readings.
11. Pilot repair (claim 46). Reporting what a theologian held is RE·S. Evaluating an argument whose premises any reasoner may grant is PL, even when a theologian made it.
