# ANZSRC 45 — Indigenous Studies: scope note (no new pyramid)

**Status.** Written as a scope note. The owner has since added a pyramid, IK (p2_IK.md), which owns claims about a knowledge system as a system. This table still governs which subject claims stay with other owners. Claims are homed in existing pyramids by R1. Every placement is provisional until Māori-led review.

**Authority.** ANZSRC 2020 Division 45 has 20 groups: 4501–4506 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander; 4507–4512 Māori; 4513–4518 Pacific Peoples; 4519 Indigenous data, methodologies and global studies; 4599 Other (checked: https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/view/subjects/450000.html). Each people-group pairs a people (substrate) with a topic (sector). So 19 of 20 land as substrate × sector, never as cells. 4599 is a residual, not a cell.

## Where W13 applies

Only inside RE·T. It warrants sentences about correct relation to atua, tapu and noa, or the authority of kōrero tuku iho, argued from whakapapa, kaumātua, tohunga and marae kawa (RE.O.A1–A4 (·T)). Authority is local, so a ·T sentence names its iwi or hapū.

## Where W13 does not travel

W13 never licenses a measurement, a date, a legal duty or a word's meaning in the language. A mātauranga record can be evidence in those cells, weighed by their warrant (W4, W7, W8, W10). Those warrants likewise do not overrule a ·T sentence on its own terms. They are different sentences.

## Contested placement (R6)

CONTESTED. An alternative holds that mātauranga Māori is a whole system that owns its phenomena. On that view ecological and health claims stay with the tradition. This note does not adopt it; it splits sentences by R1. The cost: a holistic claim becomes several sentences. Reviewers may reject the split itself.

## Māori-led review (binding)

These placements were drafted outside the tradition. They need review led by Māori scholars and holders of the relevant mātauranga, by iwi or hapū where the claim is local. Reviewers may move a claim, refuse a split, or withhold a claim from filing. Withholding is valid: some kōrero is tapu and not for open classification. Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander and Pacific claims follow the same rule, led by those peoples. OPEN: who convenes review, and how disagreement between iwi is recorded.
