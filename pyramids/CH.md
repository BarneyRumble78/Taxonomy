# CH — Chemistry

Standard v2 cited, not copied. Diagnosis: audit_physics_chemistry.md. v1 source: twelve.txt 1182–1516. Phase 2.2 deepening: each A-cell has its own SCQA, key line and named objects with conditions.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Chemistry states what substances are made of, how their atoms bond, which way they transform, how fast, and how they are made or found.
**Complication.** v1 divided chemistry by department: organic, inorganic, physical and analytical. A department is a community, not a claim-cell, so one claim (a reaction rate in an organic synthesis) had three homes. v1 also misstated the number of IUPAC divisions; there are eight.
**Question.** By what single dimension can every chemical claim be placed once?
**Answer.** By the question the claim answers about a substance: what it is, how it holds together, which way it goes, how fast, and how it is made or found.

## 2. Governing thought
Chemistry is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five questions about a substance: composition and identity, structure and bonding, equilibrium and energy, rates and mechanisms, and synthesis and analysis.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the question asked of a substance.** Logic inductive. Order: structural, from what a substance is to how it is made or found.
1. Composition and identity
2. Structure and bonding
3. Equilibrium and energy
4. Rates and mechanisms
5. Synthesis and analysis

**MECE test.** "The yield of this esterification at 60 °C is 85%" is A5 (a claim about making). "Its rate constant at 60 °C is k" is A4. "Its equilibrium constant is K" is A3. The same reaction gets three sentences, each with one home.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### CH.O.A1 Composition and identity
**SCQA.** Before any other claim, a substance must be named and its make-up fixed. Identity rests partly on convention (names, identifiers) and partly on measurement (composition). What is it?
**Key line.** Elements, compounds and stoichiometry, mixtures and solutions, nomenclature and identifiers, isotopes.
- **CH.O.A1.1 Elements and the periodic table.** 118 elements are named and confirmed by IUPAC; the last four names were approved in 2016. Periodicity follows from electron configuration (uses PH.O.A2.1).
  - CH.O.A1.1.1 Atomic weights are published by IUPAC (CIAAW), some as intervals reflecting natural variation.
- **CH.O.A1.2 Compounds and stoichiometry.** The law of definite proportions holds for daltonide compounds. Berthollide (non-stoichiometric) solids, such as wüstite Fe₁₋ₓO, are the stated exception.
  - CH.O.A1.2.1 The mole: since 2019 defined by fixing the Avogadro constant at 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
- **CH.O.A1.3 Mixtures and solutions.** Composition is stated by mole fraction, molality or concentration.
  - CH.O.A1.3.1 Raoult's law holds for ideal solutions. Henry's law holds for dilute solutes.
  - CH.O.A1.3.2 Colligative properties depend on solute particle number, for dilute ideal solutions.
- **CH.O.A1.4 Nomenclature and identifiers.** IUPAC names, CAS registry numbers and InChI. Warrant W12.
- **CH.O.A1.5 Isotopes and isotopic composition.** Isotope ratios as identity and tracer. The nuclear structure is PH; radiometric ages are EA.

### CH.O.A2 Structure and bonding
**SCQA.** A substance's properties follow from how its atoms are arranged and held together. Bonding models range from exact quantum chemistry to heuristic rules. What is claimed about structure, and by which model?
**Key line.** The chemical bond, molecular shape, solid-state and coordination structure, intermolecular forces.
- **CH.O.A2.1 The chemical bond.** Covalent, ionic and metallic bonding. The quantum-chemical description uses the Born–Oppenheimer approximation: nuclei treated as fixed relative to electrons.
  - CH.O.A2.1.1 Molecular orbital theory and valence bond theory as models.
  - CH.O.A2.1.2 Electronegativity (Pauling scale) as a derived, model-dependent quantity.
- **CH.O.A2.2 Molecular shape and stereochemistry.** Chirality, isomerism, conformational analysis. VSEPR is listed as a heuristic, not a law.
  - CH.O.A2.2.1 Enantiomers have identical scalar properties in an achiral environment. They differ in chiral environments, including living systems (BI, MD).
- **CH.O.A2.3 Solid-state and coordination structure.** Crystal structures; ligand field theory for transition-metal complexes.
  - CH.O.A2.3.1 Polymorphism: one compound, several crystal forms with different properties.
- **CH.O.A2.4 Intermolecular forces.** Hydrogen bonding, dipole forces, dispersion; their effect on phase and solubility.

### CH.O.A3 Equilibrium and energy
**SCQA.** Thermodynamics decides which way a reaction runs and how far. It says nothing about speed. What is claimed about the direction and extent of change?
**Key line.** Thermochemistry, chemical equilibrium, electrochemistry, acids and bases, solution thermodynamics.
- **CH.O.A3.1 Thermochemistry.** Hess's law: the enthalpy change is path-independent because enthalpy is a state function. Enthalpies of reaction enter energy balances in EN.
- **CH.O.A3.2 Chemical equilibrium.** ΔG° = −RT ln K at constant temperature and pressure. Le Chatelier's principle is listed as a heuristic for small perturbations.
  - CH.O.A3.2.1 The van 't Hoff equation gives the temperature dependence of K, assuming ΔH° constant over the range.
- **CH.O.A3.3 Electrochemistry.** The Nernst equation E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q, using activities, for a cell at equilibrium (no current). It gives ion equilibrium potentials used in BI and MS.
  - CH.O.A3.3.1 Batteries and corrosion as applications. Device performance is EN.
- **CH.O.A3.4 Acids and bases.** Brønsted–Lowry and Lewis definitions. pKa is stated in a named solvent and at a temperature.
- **CH.O.A3.5 Free energy in coupled reactions.** A favourable reaction can drive an unfavourable one when they share an intermediate. ATP coupling in cells is the BI application.

### CH.O.A4 Rates and mechanisms
**SCQA.** Thermodynamics says whether; kinetics says how fast and by what path. Mechanisms are inferred, never observed whole. What is claimed about speed and pathway?
**Key line.** Rate laws, temperature dependence, mechanisms, catalysis, photochemistry.
- **CH.O.A4.1 Rate laws.** Reaction order is determined by experiment, not by the balanced equation, except for elementary steps.
- **CH.O.A4.2 Temperature dependence.** Arrhenius k = A exp(−Ea/RT) is empirical over a stated temperature range. Eyring transition-state theory assumes quasi-equilibrium with the activated complex.
- **CH.O.A4.3 Mechanisms.** Sequences of elementary steps. The steady-state and rate-determining-step approximations hold when one step is much slower than the rest.
  - CH.O.A4.3.1 Kinetic isotope effects as mechanistic evidence.
- **CH.O.A4.4 Catalysis.** A catalyst changes the rate without changing K. Heterogeneous, homogeneous and enzyme catalysis. Michaelis–Menten kinetics assume a steady-state enzyme–substrate complex and substrate in excess. The enzyme's role in life is BI.
- **CH.O.A4.5 Photochemistry.** Reactions initiated by light; quantum yield. The level structure is PH.O.A2.4.

### CH.O.A5 Synthesis and analysis
**SCQA.** Chemistry makes substances and finds out what a sample contains. Both are practical claims with stated conditions. What was made, how, and how do we know what is there?
**Key line.** Synthesis, separation and purification, analytical measurement, materials and polymers, green chemistry.
- **CH.O.A5.1 Synthesis.** Named reactions; retrosynthetic analysis (Corey); yields under stated conditions.
  - CH.O.A5.1.1 Protecting groups and selectivity.
- **CH.O.A5.2 Separation and purification.** Distillation, chromatography, crystallisation.
- **CH.O.A5.3 Analytical measurement.** Spectroscopy (NMR, IR, UV-vis), mass spectrometry, titration. Every value carries an uncertainty and a detection limit.
  - CH.O.A5.3.1 The Beer–Lambert law holds for dilute solutions and monochromatic light, without scattering.
- **CH.O.A5.4 Materials and polymers.** Composition–property relations for designed substances. Performance against a specification is EN.
- **CH.O.A5.5 Green chemistry.** Atom economy and waste metrics. The environmental impact claim is EV.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place a claim by the question it answers of a substance.
2. A department is a bridge, never a cell.
3. Atomic level structure is PH. The bond is CH.
4. What a molecule does in a living cell is BI. Its structure and reactivity are CH.
5. A drug's action in a patient is MD. Its synthesis and structure are CH.
6. A material's fitness for a design is EN. Its composition–property law is CH.
7. A food standard is AG (uses CH for the molecule).
8. Environmental harm from a chemical is EV. Its fate and reaction are CH.
