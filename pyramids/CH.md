# CH — Chemistry

Standard v2 cited, not copied. Diagnosis: audit_physics_chemistry.md. v1 source: twelve.txt 1182–1516. Phase 2.2 deepening: each A-cell has its own SCQA, key line and named objects with conditions.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Chemistry states what substances are made of, how their atoms bond, which way they transform, how fast, and how they are made or found.
**Complication.** v1 divided chemistry by department: organic, inorganic, physical and analytical. A department is a community, not a claim-cell, so one claim (a reaction rate in an organic synthesis) had three homes. v1 also misstated the number of IUPAC divisions; there are eight.
**Question.** By what single dimension can every chemical claim be placed once?
**Answer.** By the question the claim answers about a substance: what it is, how it holds together, which way it goes, how fast, and how it is made or found.

## 2. Governing thought
Chemistry is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five questions about a substance: composition and identity, structure and bonding, equilibrium and energy, rates and mechanisms, and synthesis and analysis.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the question asked of a substance.** Logic inductive. Order: structural, from what a substance is to how it is made or found.
1. Composition and identity
2. Structure and bonding
3. Equilibrium and energy
4. Rates and mechanisms
5. Synthesis and analysis

**MECE test.** "The yield of this esterification at 60 °C is 85%" is A5 (a claim about making). "Its rate constant at 60 °C is k" is A4. "Its equilibrium constant is K" is A3. The same reaction gets three sentences, each with one home.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### CH.O.A1 Composition and identity
**SCQA.** Situation. Before any other claim, a substance must be named and its make-up fixed. Complication. Identity rests partly on convention (names, identifiers) and partly on measurement (composition). The two can disagree, as when one name covers several isotopic or polymorphic forms. Question. What is the substance, and what is it made of? Answer. Chemistry owns the element, the compound and its stoichiometry, the mixture, the naming rule and the isotopic make-up. The nucleus is PH; radiometric age is EA.
**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: identity claims; dimension: the unit whose make-up is stated; order: from the single element to the labelled sample. Members (5): Elements and the periodic table · Compounds and stoichiometry · Mixtures and solutions · Nomenclature and identifiers · Isotopes and isotopic composition.
- **CH.O.A1.1 Elements and the periodic table.** **An element is a class of atoms with one atomic number, and its place in the table follows from electron configuration.** 118 elements are named and confirmed by IUPAC; the last four names were approved in 2016. Periodicity follows from electron configuration (uses PH.O.A2.1).
  - CH.O.A1.1.1 Atomic weights are published by IUPAC (CIAAW), some as intervals reflecting natural variation.
  - CH.O.A1.1.2 Periodic trends. First ionisation energy rises across a period and falls down a group, with stated exceptions at half-filled and filled subshells.
  - CH.O.A1.1.3 Relativistic effects. For heavy elements, relativistic contraction of s orbitals is needed to explain properties such as the colour of gold and the liquidity of mercury (uses PH.O.A2.1).
  - CH.O.A1.1.4 Placement of hydrogen and of the group 3 elements in the table is CONTESTED; IUPAC has an open project on group 3 (UNVERIFIED status).
- **CH.O.A1.2 Compounds and stoichiometry.** **A compound has a fixed ratio of elements, except in stated non-stoichiometric solids.** The law of definite proportions holds for daltonide compounds. Berthollide (non-stoichiometric) solids, such as wüstite Fe₁₋ₓO, are the stated exception.
  - CH.O.A1.2.1 The mole: since 2019 defined by fixing the Avogadro constant at 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
  - CH.O.A1.2.2 Conservation of mass in a chemical reaction holds to within measurement. The mass change from bond energy is far below balance resolution.
  - CH.O.A1.2.3 An empirical formula follows from elemental analysis; a molecular formula needs a molar mass as well.
  - CH.O.A1.2.4 Limiting reagent. The theoretical yield is set by the reagent that runs out first, given the balanced equation.
- **CH.O.A1.3 Mixtures and solutions.** **A mixture's composition is a stated ratio, and its ideal behaviour holds only in stated limits.** Composition is stated by mole fraction, molality or concentration.
  - CH.O.A1.3.1 Raoult's law holds for ideal solutions. Henry's law holds for dilute solutes.
  - CH.O.A1.3.2 Colligative properties depend on solute particle number, for dilute ideal solutions.
  - CH.O.A1.3.3 Azeotropes. A mixture that boils at constant composition cannot be separated by simple distillation at that pressure.
  - CH.O.A1.3.4 Real solutions are described by activity coefficients. Debye–Hückel theory gives them for dilute electrolytes, with point ions and a continuum solvent.
- **CH.O.A1.4 Nomenclature and identifiers.** **A name or identifier is correct when it follows the stated rule set, not when it is customary.** IUPAC names, CAS registry numbers and InChI. Warrant W12.
  - CH.O.A1.4.1 One structure has one standard InChI, given the layer options. One CAS number may cover a mixture or an unspecified stereoisomer.
  - CH.O.A1.4.2 The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog rules assign R or S to each stereocentre from priority by atomic number.
- **CH.O.A1.5 Isotopes and isotopic composition.** **An isotope ratio identifies a source or traces a path, given a known fractionation.** Isotope ratios as identity and tracer. The nuclear structure is PH; radiometric ages are EA.
  - CH.O.A1.5.1 Isotope ratios are reported as δ values against named reference materials, such as VSMOW for oxygen and hydrogen.
  - CH.O.A1.5.2 Mass-dependent fractionation scales with the mass difference of the isotopes. Mass-independent fractionation needs a further mechanism.
  - CH.O.A1.5.3 Isotope labelling (²H, ¹³C, ¹⁵N, ¹⁸O) traces atoms through a reaction or a pathway (uses BI.O.A2.3).

Named objects: element, atomic number, periodic law, mole, Avogadro constant, law of definite proportions, Raoult's law (ideal solution: like interactions equal unlike), Henry's law (dilute solute), Debye–Hückel theory (dilute electrolyte, point ions, continuum solvent), colligative property, azeotrope, IUPAC name, CAS number, InChI, Cahn–Ingold–Prelog rules, isotope ratio, δ notation.

### CH.O.A2 Structure and bonding
**SCQA.** Situation. A substance's properties follow from how its atoms are arranged and held together. Complication. Bonding models range from exact quantum chemistry to heuristic rules, and the same molecule can be described by several. The quantum mechanics is owned by PH. Question. What is claimed about structure, and by which model? Answer. Chemistry owns the bond as a model of electron distribution, molecular shape, extended and coordination structure, and the forces between molecules.
**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: structural claims; dimension: the scale of the arrangement; order: one bond, one molecule, an extended solid, many molecules. Members (4): The chemical bond · Molecular shape and stereochemistry · Solid-state and coordination structure · Intermolecular forces.
- **CH.O.A2.1 The chemical bond.** **A bond is a model of shared or transferred electron density, judged by its predictions of energy and geometry.** Covalent, ionic and metallic bonding. The quantum-chemical description uses the Born–Oppenheimer approximation: nuclei treated as fixed relative to electrons.
  - CH.O.A2.1.1 Molecular orbital theory and valence bond theory as models.
  - CH.O.A2.1.2 Electronegativity (Pauling scale) as a derived, model-dependent quantity.
  - CH.O.A2.1.3 Born–Oppenheimer fails where electronic states come close, as at conical intersections. Non-adiabatic dynamics is then needed (uses PH.O.A2.4).
  - CH.O.A2.1.4 Bond order from molecular orbital theory predicts that O₂ is paramagnetic. A simple Lewis structure does not.
  - CH.O.A2.1.5 Ionic lattice energies follow the Born–Haber cycle, given Hess's law (uses CH.O.A3.1).
- **CH.O.A2.2 Molecular shape and stereochemistry.** **Shape is fixed by connectivity and configuration, and changed by conformation without breaking bonds.** Chirality, isomerism, conformational analysis. VSEPR is listed as a heuristic, not a law.
  - CH.O.A2.2.1 Enantiomers have identical scalar properties in an achiral environment. They differ in chiral environments, including living systems (BI, MD).
  - CH.O.A2.2.2 Point-group symmetry predicts which vibrations are IR or Raman active. The group theory is MA (uses MA.O.A2).
  - CH.O.A2.2.3 Conformer populations follow a Boltzmann distribution when interconversion is fast on the timescale of measurement (uses PH.O.A4.2).
  - CH.O.A2.2.4 Atropisomers are stereoisomers from hindered rotation. They are separable when the rotation barrier is high enough at the stated temperature.
- **CH.O.A2.3 Solid-state and coordination structure.** **A solid's structure is a lattice plus a basis, and a complex's structure is a metal plus its ligand set.** Crystal structures; ligand field theory for transition-metal complexes.
  - CH.O.A2.3.1 Polymorphism: one compound, several crystal forms with different properties.
  - CH.O.A2.3.2 Ligand field splitting orders ligands in the spectrochemical series. Spin state follows from splitting versus pairing energy.
  - CH.O.A2.3.3 The Jahn–Teller theorem: a non-linear molecule in an orbitally degenerate state distorts to remove the degeneracy. The theorem is MA (uses MA.O.A2).
  - CH.O.A2.3.4 Band structure describes metals and semiconductors. The band theory is PH (uses PH.O.A4.4).
- **CH.O.A2.4 Intermolecular forces.** **Non-covalent forces, weaker than bonds, set phase, solubility and molecular recognition.** Hydrogen bonding, dipole forces, dispersion; their effect on phase and solubility.
  - CH.O.A2.4.1 Boiling points within a homologous series rise with chain length, mainly through dispersion forces.
  - CH.O.A2.4.2 The hydrophobic effect is largely entropic at room temperature, from ordering of water near non-polar surfaces. Its molecular account is CONTESTED.
  - CH.O.A2.4.3 Supramolecular assembly (host–guest binding) is driven by many weak interactions acting together.

Named objects: covalent bond, ionic bond, metallic bond, Born–Oppenheimer approximation (nuclei much heavier than electrons; separated electronic states), molecular orbital theory, valence bond theory, electronegativity, Born–Haber cycle, chirality, point group, conformer, atropisomer, VSEPR (heuristic), crystal lattice, polymorph, ligand field theory (metal–ligand interaction treated through d-orbital splitting), spectrochemical series, Jahn–Teller distortion, hydrogen bond, dispersion force, hydrophobic effect.

### CH.O.A3 Equilibrium and energy
**SCQA.** Situation. Thermodynamics decides which way a reaction runs and how far. Complication. It says nothing about speed, and the laws of thermodynamics belong to PH. Chemistry applies them to reactions, cells and solutions. Question. What is claimed about the direction and extent of chemical change? Answer. Chemistry owns reaction enthalpy, the equilibrium constant, electrode potential, acidity and coupling between reactions.
**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: energetic claims; dimension: the quantity that sets direction or extent; order: heat, then free energy, then its electrical, protic and coupled forms. Members (5): Thermochemistry · Chemical equilibrium · Electrochemistry · Acids and bases · Free energy in coupled reactions.
- **CH.O.A3.1 Thermochemistry.** **Reaction enthalpy is a state-function difference, so it can be summed along any path.** Hess's law: the enthalpy change is path-independent because enthalpy is a state function. Enthalpies of reaction enter energy balances in EN.
  - CH.O.A3.1.1 Standard enthalpies of formation, at a stated standard state, combine to give any reaction enthalpy (uses PH.O.A4.1).
  - CH.O.A3.1.2 Bond enthalpies are averages over compounds. Sums of them estimate reaction enthalpy only approximately.
  - CH.O.A3.1.3 Calorimetry measures heat at constant pressure (ΔH) or constant volume (ΔU). The two differ by the change in pV.
- **CH.O.A3.2 Chemical equilibrium.** **The equilibrium constant fixes the extent of reaction at a temperature, whatever the starting mixture.** ΔG° = −RT ln K at constant temperature and pressure. Le Chatelier's principle is listed as a heuristic for small perturbations.
  - CH.O.A3.2.1 The van 't Hoff equation gives the temperature dependence of K, assuming ΔH° constant over the range.
  - CH.O.A3.2.2 K is written with activities. Using concentrations holds only where activity coefficients are near one.
  - CH.O.A3.2.3 Solubility product Ksp predicts precipitation when the ion activity product exceeds it.
  - CH.O.A3.2.4 Gibbs phase rule F = C − P + 2 gives the degrees of freedom of a system at equilibrium (uses PH.O.A4.3).
- **CH.O.A3.3 Electrochemistry.** **Electrode potential is free energy per unit charge, so it predicts direction for a redox reaction.** The Nernst equation E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q, using activities, for a cell at equilibrium (no current). It gives ion equilibrium potentials used in BI and MS.
  - CH.O.A3.3.1 Batteries and corrosion as applications. Device performance is EN.
  - CH.O.A3.3.2 ΔG = −nFE links cell potential to reaction free energy.
  - CH.O.A3.3.3 Faraday's laws: charge passed is proportional to amount of substance changed, through n and F.
  - CH.O.A3.3.4 The Butler–Volmer equation gives current versus overpotential for a single-step electron transfer. Its kinetics are CH.O.A4 (uses CH.O.A4.2).
- **CH.O.A3.4 Acids and bases.** **Acid strength is an equilibrium constant in a named solvent, not an intrinsic property.** Brønsted–Lowry and Lewis definitions. pKa is stated in a named solvent and at a temperature.
  - CH.O.A3.4.1 The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation gives buffer pH, assuming activities near concentrations and neither form exhausted.
  - CH.O.A3.4.2 Levelling. In water, acids stronger than H₃O⁺ appear equally strong.
  - CH.O.A3.4.3 Hard–soft acid–base (HSAB) theory predicts preferred pairings qualitatively. It is a heuristic with known exceptions.
- **CH.O.A3.5 Free energy in coupled reactions.** **An unfavourable step proceeds when it shares an intermediate with a more favourable one.** A favourable reaction can drive an unfavourable one when they share an intermediate. ATP coupling in cells is the BI application.
  - CH.O.A3.5.1 Coupled free energies add only when the steps share a common intermediate. Mixing in one vessel is not enough.
  - CH.O.A3.5.2 Electrolysis couples electrical work to a reaction with positive ΔG (uses CH.O.A3.3).

Named objects: Hess's law (enthalpy a state function), standard enthalpy of formation, bond enthalpy, ΔG° = −RT ln K (constant T and p, activities), van 't Hoff equation (ΔH° constant over range), solubility product, Gibbs phase rule (equilibrium, no extra constraints), Nernst equation (zero current, activities), Faraday's laws, Butler–Volmer equation (single-step electron transfer, no mass-transport limit), Brønsted–Lowry acid, Lewis acid, pKa, Henderson–Hasselbalch equation, HSAB (heuristic), Le Chatelier's principle (heuristic).

### CH.O.A4 Rates and mechanisms
**SCQA.** Situation. Thermodynamics says whether; kinetics says how fast and by what path. Complication. Mechanisms are inferred, never observed whole. Rate laws and differential equations are shared with PH and MA. Question. What is claimed about speed and pathway? Answer. Chemistry owns the rate law, its temperature dependence, the mechanism, the catalyst's effect on rate and the light-driven reaction.
**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: kinetic claims; dimension: what the claim explains about speed; order: measured rate, its temperature dependence, its path, its acceleration, its light trigger. Members (5): Rate laws · Temperature dependence · Mechanisms · Catalysis · Photochemistry.
- **CH.O.A4.1 Rate laws.** **The rate law is an experimental finding, not a reading of the balanced equation.** Reaction order is determined by experiment, not by the balanced equation, except for elementary steps.
  - CH.O.A4.1.1 Integrated rate laws give linear plots for each order. First-order half-life is independent of initial concentration (uses MA.O.A4).
  - CH.O.A4.1.2 Pseudo-first-order conditions hold when one reagent is in large excess, so its concentration is effectively constant.
  - CH.O.A4.1.3 The method of initial rates finds order in each reagent, given no product inhibition early on.
- **CH.O.A4.2 Temperature dependence.** **Rate constants rise with temperature, and a fitted barrier summarises how fast.** Arrhenius k = A exp(−Ea/RT) is empirical over a stated temperature range. Eyring transition-state theory assumes quasi-equilibrium with the activated complex.
  - CH.O.A4.2.1 Eyring analysis gives activation enthalpy and entropy. A large negative ΔS‡ is read as an ordered, associative transition state.
  - CH.O.A4.2.2 Tunnelling makes rates exceed transition-state theory for light-atom transfer at low temperature.
  - CH.O.A4.2.3 Collision theory under-predicts or over-predicts rates by a steric factor that it cannot compute.
- **CH.O.A4.3 Mechanisms.** **A mechanism is a hypothesis of elementary steps, accepted while it fits rate law, labelling and intermediates.** Sequences of elementary steps. The steady-state and rate-determining-step approximations hold when one step is much slower than the rest.
  - CH.O.A4.3.1 Kinetic isotope effects as mechanistic evidence.
  - CH.O.A4.3.2 The principle of microscopic reversibility: at equilibrium, forward and reverse paths share the same transition states.
  - CH.O.A4.3.3 Linear free-energy relations (Hammett equation) correlate rates with substituent constants, for meta and para substituents on aromatic rings.
  - CH.O.A4.3.4 Curtin–Hammett principle: when conformers interconvert faster than they react, product ratio depends on transition-state energies only.
- **CH.O.A4.4 Catalysis.** **A catalyst lowers the barrier of a path without changing the equilibrium position.** A catalyst changes the rate without changing K. Heterogeneous, homogeneous and enzyme catalysis. Michaelis–Menten kinetics assume a steady-state enzyme–substrate complex and substrate in excess. The enzyme's role in life is BI.
  - CH.O.A4.4.1 Sabatier principle: the best heterogeneous catalyst binds intermediates neither too weakly nor too strongly. Plots of rate against binding energy are volcano-shaped.
  - CH.O.A4.4.2 Langmuir adsorption assumes equivalent sites, a monolayer and no interaction between adsorbates.
  - CH.O.A4.4.3 Turnover number and turnover frequency state catalyst productivity; their site count must be stated.
  - CH.O.A4.4.4 Enzyme rate enhancement as transition-state stabilisation is the standard account. The size of a dynamics contribution is CONTESTED.
- **CH.O.A4.5 Photochemistry.** **A photochemical step starts from an excited state, so its yield is counted per photon absorbed.** Reactions initiated by light; quantum yield. The level structure is PH.O.A2.4.
  - CH.O.A4.5.1 Stark–Einstein law: one absorbed photon excites one molecule in the primary process. Multiphoton absorption at high intensity is the exception.
  - CH.O.A4.5.2 Kasha's rule: emission comes mainly from the lowest excited state of a given multiplicity. Known exceptions exist, such as azulene.
  - CH.O.A4.5.3 The Woodward–Hoffmann rules predict allowed pericyclic reactions under heat or light from orbital symmetry.

Named objects: rate law, reaction order, half-life, Arrhenius equation (empirical, stated range), Eyring equation (quasi-equilibrium with activated complex), collision theory, steady-state approximation (intermediate concentration small and constant), rate-determining step, kinetic isotope effect, microscopic reversibility, Hammett equation (meta and para aromatic substituents), Curtin–Hammett principle (fast conformer exchange), Michaelis–Menten kinetics (steady-state complex, substrate excess), Sabatier principle, Langmuir isotherm (equivalent sites, monolayer, no lateral interaction), turnover frequency, quantum yield, Stark–Einstein law, Kasha's rule, Woodward–Hoffmann rules (concerted reaction, orbital symmetry conserved), Hammond's postulate (heuristic).

### CH.O.A5 Synthesis and analysis
**SCQA.** Situation. Chemistry makes substances and finds out what a sample contains. Complication. Both are practical claims, true only under stated conditions, and both overlap with engineering and environmental fields. Question. What was made, how, and how do we know what is there? Answer. Chemistry owns the synthetic route, the separation, the measurement of composition, the composition–property law of materials and the metrics of chemical efficiency.
**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: practical claims; dimension: the stage of handling a substance; order: make, purify, measure, design for use, reduce waste. Members (5): Synthesis · Separation and purification · Analytical measurement · Materials and polymers · Green chemistry.
- **CH.O.A5.1 Synthesis.** **A synthesis claim is a route plus conditions plus a characterised product and its yield.** Named reactions; retrosynthetic analysis (Corey); yields under stated conditions.
  - CH.O.A5.1.1 Protecting groups and selectivity.
  - CH.O.A5.1.2 Overall yield of a linear route is the product of step yields. A convergent route gives a higher overall yield for the same steps.
  - CH.O.A5.1.3 Asymmetric catalysis gives one enantiomer in excess. Enantiomeric excess is a measured value, stated with its method.
  - CH.O.A5.1.4 Reproducibility of a reported yield is OPEN as a field-wide question; reported yields are often higher than repeat yields (UNVERIFIED size).
- **CH.O.A5.2 Separation and purification.** **Separation exploits a stated difference in property between components.** Distillation, chromatography, crystallisation.
  - CH.O.A5.2.1 Chromatographic retention depends on the partition coefficient between phases. Resolution needs both selectivity and efficiency.
  - CH.O.A5.2.2 Fractional distillation separates by volatility, limited by azeotropes (uses CH.O.A1.3).
  - CH.O.A5.2.3 Recrystallisation purifies when impurities stay in solution at the final temperature.
- **CH.O.A5.3 Analytical measurement.** **An analytical result is a value with an uncertainty, a detection limit and a traceable calibration.** Spectroscopy (NMR, IR, UV-vis), mass spectrometry, titration. Every value carries an uncertainty and a detection limit.
  - CH.O.A5.3.1 The Beer–Lambert law holds for dilute solutions and monochromatic light, without scattering.
  - CH.O.A5.3.2 NMR chemical shift and coupling identify local structure. Integrals are quantitative only with full relaxation between pulses.
  - CH.O.A5.3.3 High-resolution mass spectrometry gives an elemental formula from exact mass and isotope pattern, within a stated mass error.
  - CH.O.A5.3.4 Calibration by standard addition corrects for matrix effects, given a linear response.
- **CH.O.A5.4 Materials and polymers.** **A designed substance has a composition–property law; meeting a specification is EN.** Composition–property relations for designed substances. Performance against a specification is EN.
  - CH.O.A5.4.1 Polymer properties depend on molar-mass distribution. Number-average and weight-average molar mass differ unless the sample is monodisperse.
  - CH.O.A5.4.2 Step-growth polymerisation reaches high molar mass only at high conversion (Carothers equation, stoichiometric monomers).
  - CH.O.A5.4.3 Glass transition temperature marks a change in chain mobility; it depends on measurement rate (uses EN.O.A5).
- **CH.O.A5.5 Green chemistry.** **Efficiency metrics compare routes by mass of product per mass of input or waste.** Atom economy and waste metrics. The environmental impact claim is EV.
  - CH.O.A5.5.1 Atom economy is computed from the balanced equation. It ignores yield, solvent and reagents used in excess.
  - CH.O.A5.5.2 The E-factor is mass of waste per mass of product. Whether water is counted must be stated.
  - CH.O.A5.5.3 Whether a lower metric means lower environmental harm is EV's claim (uses EV.O.A3).

Named objects: retrosynthetic analysis, named reaction, protecting group, enantiomeric excess, chromatography (partition between phases), distillation, recrystallisation, NMR spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, Beer–Lambert law (dilute, monochromatic, no scattering), standard addition (linear response), detection limit, Carothers equation (step growth, equal reactivity, stoichiometric monomers), glass transition, atom economy, E-factor.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place a claim by the question it answers of a substance.
2. A department is a bridge, never a cell.
3. Atomic level structure is PH. The bond is CH.
4. What a molecule does in a living cell is BI. Its structure and reactivity are CH.
5. A drug's action in a patient is MD. Its synthesis and structure are CH.
6. A material's fitness for a design is EN. Its composition–property law is CH.
7. A food standard is AG (uses CH for the molecule).
8. Environmental harm from a chemical is EV. Its fate and reaction are CH.
