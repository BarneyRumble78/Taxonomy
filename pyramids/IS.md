# IS — Information and the record

New field, added at the owner's direction. Standard v2 cited, not copied. Why it is here: ANZSRC 46 mixes computation with the record. CS owns cost and correctness. Nothing owned the catalogue, the archival rule, or retrieval as a claim about records. This pyramid is also the natural home of the Pyramids of Knowledge themselves, as a classification.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Library, archival and information science states how records are identified, described, arranged, found and kept.
**Complication.** CS owned the algorithms, HI owned source criticism, and LN owned language. The catalogue, the classification scheme and the archive had no owner, though every pyramid depends on them.
**Question.** By what dimension can each claim about records be placed once?
**Answer.** By the stage in the life of a record the claim concerns: the item, its description, its arrangement, its retrieval, and its preservation.

## 2. Governing thought
Information science is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five stages of a record's life: items, description, arrangement, retrieval, and preservation.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the stage in the life of a record.** Order: from creation to keeping.
1. Items
2. Description
3. Arrangement
4. Retrieval
5. Preservation

## 4. Face O — Objects

### IS.O.A1 Items
**SCQA.** A record must first be identified as a unit. What is the item? Key line: works and manifestations, records and evidence, data sets, identifiers.
- IS.O.A1.1 Works and manifestations — FRBR and its successor LRM distinguish work, expression, manifestation and item.
- IS.O.A1.2 Records as evidence — an archival record is evidence of an activity. Its source criticism for history is HI.
- IS.O.A1.3 Data sets — research data as items.
- IS.O.A1.4 Persistent identifiers — DOI, ORCID, ISBN (W12).

### IS.O.A2 Description
**SCQA.** Items are found by their descriptions. How are they described? Key line: metadata standards, cataloguing rules, subject indexing, linked data.
- IS.O.A2.1 Metadata standards — Dublin Core, MARC (W12).
- IS.O.A2.2 Cataloguing rules — RDA.
- IS.O.A2.3 Subject indexing — controlled vocabularies and thesauri (MeSH, LCSH).
- IS.O.A2.4 Linked data — SKOS and RDF. The logic is MA.O.A1; the vocabulary is IS.

### IS.O.A3 Arrangement
**SCQA.** Descriptions are organised into schemes. How is knowledge arranged? Key line: classification schemes, faceted classification, archival arrangement, ontologies.
- IS.O.A3.1 Classification schemes — LCC, DDC, UDC, MSC. A scheme is W12; whether it is MECE is testable (W9 with W4).
- IS.O.A3.2 Faceted classification — Ranganathan's Colon Classification (PMEST facets).
- IS.O.A3.3 Archival arrangement — respect des fonds and original order.
- IS.O.A3.4 Ontologies — BFO and DOLCE. Their philosophical commitments are PL.
- IS.O.A3.5 This taxonomy — the Pyramids of Knowledge as a classification of claims. Its reliability is tested by the placement protocol.

### IS.O.A4 Retrieval
**SCQA.** Arranged records must be found. How well can they be found? Key line: retrieval effectiveness, relevance, bibliometrics, information behaviour.
- IS.O.A4.1 Retrieval effectiveness — precision and recall. The algorithm's cost is CS.
- IS.O.A4.2 Relevance — topical and user relevance; judged by assessors (W4).
- IS.O.A4.3 Bibliometrics — Lotka's law (author productivity) and Bradford's law (scattering) are empirical regularities within stated literatures.
- IS.O.A4.4 Information behaviour — how people seek information. The cognitive mechanism is MS.

### IS.O.A5 Preservation
**SCQA.** Records decay and formats die. How are they kept usable? Key line: physical conservation, digital preservation, access and rights, Indigenous data governance.
- IS.O.A5.1 Physical conservation — the chemistry of paper and media is CH.
- IS.O.A5.2 Digital preservation — the OAIS reference model (ISO 14721, W12).
- IS.O.A5.3 Access and rights — open access; copyright is LA.
- IS.O.A5.4 Indigenous data governance — the CARE principles; Māori data sovereignty (Te Mana Raraunga). Tradition-internal authority over the data is IK.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place by the record's life stage.
2. The algorithm's cost or correctness is CS; the retrieval effectiveness claim is IS.
3. Source criticism for a historical claim is HI; the archival rule is IS.
4. Copyright is LA.
5. Tradition-internal authority over Indigenous data is IK; its governance framework as a records rule is IS.
