## 1. SCQA

Situation. Philosophy asks what there is, how it is known, what follows from what, and what is worth doing. PhilPapers sorts its literature into seven top-level areas and 6,164 categories, and lets a category have more than one parent. Complication. Multiple parents double-count: a paper on consciousness is filed under mind and cognitive science, and a paper on Kant under history and epistemology. Traditions look like extra questions, and two PhilPapers areas are miscellany bins. Version 1 also broke its own "philosophy of X" rule for law and mind, and gave Nyāya two homes. Question. What single partition places every philosophical claim, practice and tradition exactly once, and states what PL owns against MA, MS, PO, LA, AR and RE?

## 2. Governing thought

Philosophy is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges. Its objects divide into five claim-types: being, knowledge and mind, meaning and inference, value, and the standing of the sciences and mathematics.

Restated "philosophy of X" rule (audit error 11). What X is entitled to assert goes to A5. What X may enforce, or ought to do, goes to A4. What the X-object is goes to A1 or A2. Philosophy of mind is therefore A2.3, and philosophy of law is A4.3, by this rule and not as exceptions.

### PL.O.A1 Being

SCQA. Situation: metaphysics asks what exists and what the most general features of existing things are. Complication: ontology, modality, identity, free will and philosophy of religion are taught separately. In v1 four bullets mapped to no member, and the realism member had no bullet. Question: which metaphysical questions are distinct and jointly exhaustive?

Key line. Logic inductive · plural noun metaphysical questions · dimension the feature of being asked about · order structural (what, how possibly, how through change, whether apart from us).

1. PL.O.A1.1 Ontology and existence
2. PL.O.A1.2 Modality
3. PL.O.A1.3 Causation, persistence and identity
4. PL.O.A1.4 Realism and anti-realism

Named objects.
- PL.O.A1.1 Ontology and existence. Particular and universal; object and property; tropes; substance; mereology; abstract objects against nominalism. Existence answers: materialism, idealism, dualism, neutral monism. These are answers, not further questions.
- PL.O.A1.1 also holds the god question argued from shared premises. Cosmological, design and ontological arguments are arguments here (W9). The logical problem of evil holds only if a god is defined as omnipotent, omniscient and wholly good. The ontological argument as an argument from premises any reasoner may grant is PL.O.A1.1; the RE prototype's rule agrees.
- PL.O.A1.1 holds Māori whakapapa read as an ontology of relational descent (Stewart, *Māori Philosophy*, 2021, per audit). The claim, not the community, sits here.
- PL.O.A1.2 Modality. Possible worlds as a device; de re and de dicto; essentialism. Rule: a necessity claim must say which type it is: logical, metaphysical or nomological. Modal systems K, T, S4 and S5 as calculi are MA.O.A1; which one models metaphysical necessity is PL.O.A1.2.
- PL.O.A1.3 Causation, persistence and identity. Causation answers: regularity, counterfactual dependence, process, interventionist (Woodward 2003). Persistence answers: endurance, perdurance (Lewis 1986).
- PL.O.A1.3 identity. Leibniz's law (the indiscernibility of identicals) holds for numerical identity in extensional contexts. It fails as a test inside belief and other opaque contexts. Trouble case: the statue and the clay.
- PL.O.A1.3 personal identity is the identity question asked of persons. The psychology of the self is MS.
- PL.O.A1.3 free will. Whether responsibility is compatible with determinism. Frankfurt cases (1969) (UNVERIFIED) target the principle of alternate possibilities. The empirical psychology of choice stays in MS (audit error 5).
- PL.O.A1.3 holds al-Ghazālī's occasionalist critique of necessary causal connection (audit item; date UNVERIFIED).
- PL.O.A1.4 Realism and anti-realism. Whether the world has a structure apart from our concepts. Putnam's model-theoretic argument assumes that reference is fixed only by total theory plus operational constraints (UNVERIFIED wording). Underdetermination of theory by evidence is cited here from A5.1.

### PL.O.A2 Knowledge and mind

SCQA. Situation: epistemology asks when belief is knowledge; philosophy of mind asks what a mental state is. Complication: they share a subject and not a question. The epistemology of religion was unplaced in v1. Question: which questions does this claim-type contain?

Key line. Logic inductive · plural noun questions about knowledge and mind · dimension entitlement, its source, or the nature of the mental · order structural.

1. PL.O.A2.1 Knowledge
2. PL.O.A2.2 Sources of entitlement
3. PL.O.A2.3 Mind

Named objects.
- PL.O.A2.1 Knowledge. The analysis is a deductive group: truth, belief, and a further condition. Truth: a known falsehood is not knowledge.
- PL.O.A2.1 belief condition. CONTESTED: Radford's unconfident examinee (1966) denies that knowledge entails belief. Williamson's knowledge-first view (2000) denies that knowledge is analysable at all.
- PL.O.A2.1 further condition. Justified true belief is the standard post-Gettier framing; whether it was ever the received view is disputed (Dutant 2015).
- PL.O.A2.1 Gettier cases (1963) assume two things. A false belief can be justified. Justification is closed under deduction the subject performs. Given both, justified true belief is not sufficient for knowledge.
- PL.O.A2.1 replies to Gettier: one inductive group of five. No false lemmas (Clark 1963); causal connection (Goldman 1967); reliability; tracking (Nozick 1981); virtue (Sosa). This list replaces v1's two lists (audit error 6).
- PL.O.A2.1 scepticism is not a member of that group. It claims the further condition is never met for a stated domain.
- PL.O.A2.1 structure of justification: foundationalism, coherentism, externalism, as answers to the regress.
- PL.O.A2.2 Sources of entitlement. Perception, memory, testimony, reason, introspection. Rationalism and empiricism are answers about source. A priori is a way of being entitled, not a place.
- PL.O.A2.2 social epistemology: testimony and peer disagreement. Disagreement alone does not prescribe a unique revision.
- PL.O.A2.2 epistemology of religion (audit error 19): religious experience, reformed epistemology (Plantinga), Hume on testimony to miracles. Boundary: the argument that scriptural authority is rational to accept is PL.O.A2.2. A claim resting on that authority is RE.O·T.
- PL.O.A2.3 Mind. Identity theory, functionalism, eliminativism, dualism, as answers. Intentionality.
- PL.O.A2.3 the hard problem (Chalmers 1995): why physical processing is accompanied by experience. Stated as a problem, not as a proof of dualism. MS defers here.
- PL.O.A2.3 Searle's Chinese room (1980) assumes that running a program is purely syntactic manipulation. It concludes that syntax does not suffice for semantics.
- PL.O.A2.3 the extended mind (Clark and Chalmers 1998); machine consciousness; perception (veridical and not); action as doing for a reason. Action sits here when the question is the mental cause, and in A4 when it is the reason's worth.
- PL.O.A2.3 Yogācāra read as a theory of mind and appearance (audit item; CONTESTED, alternative A1.4).

### PL.O.A3 Meaning and inference

SCQA. Situation: we say things, and some sayings follow from others. Complication: language and logic are separate departments, and MA also owns logic. v1 let PL and MA both claim consequence. Question: which questions does this claim-type contain, and where is the line with MA and LN?

Key line. Logic inductive · plural noun questions about saying and following · dimension significance or consequence · order structural.

1. PL.O.A3.1 Meaning
2. PL.O.A3.2 Consequence

Named objects.
- PL.O.A3.1 Meaning. Reference and sense (Frege's puzzle: "a = a" is trivial, "a = b" can be informative). Use as meaning. Speech acts. Literal and implicated content as concepts.
- PL.O.A3.1 truth-conditional semantics (Davidson 1967): to know a sentence's meaning is to know under which conditions it is true. It assumes a recursive truth theory for the language.
- PL.O.A3.1 names: descriptivism against direct reference. Kripke's modal argument (1980) assumes proper names are rigid designators across possible worlds.
- PL.O.A3.1 compositionality (Frege's principle) as a concept. Its empirical testing in a named language is LN.O.A3.
- PL.O.A3.1 Grice's cooperative principle as a concept of conversational rationality. Implicature patterns in a language are LN.O.A3.
- PL.O.A3.2 Consequence. Deduction preserves truth if valid. Induction does not, and can still be reasonable. Abduction infers to an explanation. Validity is form; soundness adds true premises.
- PL.O.A3.2 explosion: from a contradiction anything follows, in classical and intuitionistic logic. Relevance and paraconsistent logics reject it.
- PL.O.A3.2 holds which logic is correct, logical pluralism, and the significance of the liar and sorites paradoxes.
- PL.O.A3.2 uses MA.O.A1 for metatheorems. Gödel's first incompleteness theorem holds for a consistent, effectively axiomatised theory that interprets elementary arithmetic. What it shows about mind or truth is PL.
- PL.O.A3.2 uses CS.O.A1.5 for Curry–Howard. It pairs intuitionistic natural deduction with the simply typed lambda calculus. Classical logic needs control operators (Griffin 1990).
- PL.O.A3.2 holds Mohist logic and Mīmāṃsā's hermeneutics of injunction as reconstructed consequence and meaning claims (audit items; Mīmāṃsā CONTESTED, alternative A3.1).
- PL.O.A3.2 decision under uncertainty, when it is a consequence relation on credences. When the utilities are values, it is A4.2.
- Fallacies (affirming the consequent, equivocation, ad hominem) are a checked list in the heuristics register, not a system.

### PL.O.A4 Value

SCQA. Situation: some acts, characters, works and institutions are better than others, and we argue about which. Complication: ethics, politics, law and aesthetics are departments. PO and LA also claim legitimacy and the concept of law. Applied ethics and gender, race and sexuality had no member in v1. Question: which value questions are distinct?

Key line. Logic inductive · plural noun objects of value · dimension what is being valued · order structural (the value claim itself, the act, the institution, the work).

1. PL.O.A4.1 Metaethics
2. PL.O.A4.2 Normative and applied ethics
3. PL.O.A4.3 Political and legal philosophy
4. PL.O.A4.4 Aesthetics

Named objects.
- PL.O.A4.1 Metaethics. Cognitivism, expressivism, realism. The Frege–Geach problem: expressivism must explain how "lying is wrong" keeps its meaning inside a conditional.
- PL.O.A4.1 Moore's open-question argument (1903, §13). It assumes a correct analysis of "good" would make "is N good?" closed to a competent speaker. Moore offered it for non-naturalism. Main reply: synthetic property identity (Putnam; Boyd).
- PL.O.A4.2 Normative ethics, worked descent kept from v1. Key line inductive · answer-families · dimension the bearer of value · order outcome, constraint, character: consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics.
- PL.O.A4.2 a hybrid theory is not a fourth family until it says which bearer decides a conflict.
- PL.O.A4.2 Kant's categorical imperative is one deontic construction; rights theories are another. A constraint that forbids a small harm and permits a disaster needs a threshold or an admission.
- PL.O.A4.2 virtue rightness criterion (Hursthouse 1999): an act is right if a virtuous agent would characteristically do it in the circumstances.
- PL.O.A4.2 applied ethics (audit error 15): life, war, animals, environment, technology, professional ethics, AI moral status and alignment ethics. A case is not a new theory.
- PL.O.A4.2 owns the normative claim in professional and AI ethics. The professional duty as a legal duty is LA; the constraint on a specification is CS or EN.
- PL.O.A4.3 Political and legal philosophy. Legitimacy of coercion, political obligation, liberty, equality, authority, justice. Owner for PO.O.A1 and LA.O.A1, which cite it.
- PL.O.A4.3 Rawls (1971): parties behind a veil of ignorance, mutually disinterested and choosing under uncertainty, would choose the two principles. The maximin step assumes they cannot assign probabilities.
- PL.O.A4.3 Nozick (1974): entitlement theory, assuming self-ownership and a just initial acquisition. Berlin (1958): negative and positive liberty. Cohen on egalitarian justice (UNVERIFIED work date).
- PL.O.A4.3 the concept of law: positivism (Hart), interpretivism (Dworkin), natural law (Finnis 1980). CONTESTED: "what makes a norm law" is an entitlement question and fits A5 under the restated rule. Kept at A4.3 to keep PO and LA citing one cell.
- PL.O.A4.3 philosophy of gender, race and sexuality, when the claim is about a social category and a harm. When the claim is about what the category picks out, it is A1.1.
- PL.O.A4.3 Dussel's philosophy of liberation and critical theory's emancipation claim, as reconstructed political claims.
- PL.O.A4.3 African personhood debate: Menkiti against Gyekye on whether personhood is acquired in community (audit item; CONTESTED, alternative A1.3).
- PL.O.A4.4 Aesthetics. What a work is and why it merits attention. Intention, form, reception as answers. Hume's standard of taste (1757). Owner for AR, which applies criteria to named works.
- PL.O.A4.4 the ontology of a musical work (type and token) is A1.1, as the AR prototype already cites.

### PL.O.A5 Standing of the sciences and mathematics

SCQA. Situation: the sciences and mathematics assert laws, explanations and theorems. Philosophy asks what those assertions are entitled to. Complication: v1 called this cell "special science" but included mathematics, said "six fields" and named three, and had no key line. Cognitive science, computing and information, probability and chemistry were missing. Question: which entitlement questions does this cell hold, without absorbing any science?

Key line. Logic inductive · plural noun sciences asked about · dimension what kind of warrant the science owes · order from the general to the formal to the human.

1. PL.O.A5.1 General philosophy of science
2. PL.O.A5.2 Natural sciences
3. PL.O.A5.3 Formal sciences
4. PL.O.A5.4 Mind and social sciences

Named objects.
- PL.O.A5.1 General philosophy of science. Explanation, confirmation, law, model, reduction. A model is not the system.
- PL.O.A5.1 underdetermination: more than one theory can fit the same finite evidence. It is a limit, not a licence to ignore evidence.
- PL.O.A5.1 holds logical empiricism and naturalism as answers about entitlement (see PL.B.C5).
- PL.O.A5.2 Natural sciences. Philosophy of physics: time, probability in physics, the measurement problem. The formalism stays in PH.
- PL.O.A5.2 philosophy of chemistry (audit error 10): reduction of chemistry to physics; the chemical bond as a kind. The bond itself is CH.
- PL.O.A5.2 philosophy of biology: function, species, units of selection. Population genetics stays in BI.
- PL.O.A5.3 Formal sciences. Philosophy of mathematics: platonism, nominalism, structuralism, as answers to what a theorem is about. The proof stays in MA.
- PL.O.A5.3 philosophy of probability: frequency, propensity, subjective and logical interpretations. The measure is MA.O.A5; the agent model is EC.O.A1. CONTESTED: alternative home A3.2.
- PL.O.A5.3 philosophy of computing and information: what computation is, and whether information is physical. Cites CS.
- PL.O.A5.4 Mind and social sciences. Philosophy of cognitive science: representation and explanation in cognitive models. Cites MS.
- PL.O.A5.4 philosophy of social science: whether reasons are causes, and whether a social kind is like a natural kind. The data stay in MS, EC or PO.
- PL.O.A5.4 philosophy of history: whether historical explanation is causal or interpretive. Cites HI; HI's historiography bridge cites this cell.
- Boundary rule kept from v1. A philosophical claim that contradicts a settled measurement has left philosophy. It then owes the owner's empirical standard.

## 8. Assignment rules

1. A claim is placed by the sentence it makes, not by the department or century it comes from.
2. Logic as a claim about consequence is PL.O.A3.2. Calculi and metatheorems are MA.O.A1, cited as "uses MA.O.A1".
3. History of philosophy is reconstruction (PL.M.B5) of answers already placed on Face O. That a philosopher held a view at a date is HI.O.A1; whether the argument succeeds is PL.
4. Philosophy of X: entitlement to assert is A5; what X may enforce or ought to do is A4; what the X-object is, A1 or A2.
5. Philosophy of religion is PL. An argument from premises any reasoner may grant is PL.O.A1.1. Religious epistemology is PL.O.A2.2. The same conclusion argued from a creed is RE.O·T.
6. A moral claim argued from shared premises is PL.O.A4. One citing scripture, creed or school is RE·T. One reporting what a tradition holds is RE·S.
7. Normative legitimacy, obligation and justice are PL.O.A4.3. PO cites them; PO owns who holds power and how (PO).
8. The concept of law is PL.O.A4.3. Whether a norm is valid in a given legal system is LA (W10).
9. Causation in general is PL.O.A1.3. A legal causation test is LA; causal identification from data is EC (EC.B2 design), cited.
10. The hard problem and intentionality are PL.O.A2.3. Processing, measured, is MS.
11. Probability as measure is MA.O.A5; as an agent's choice model, EC.O.A1; its interpretation, PL.O.A5.3.
12. Curry–Howard is CS.O.A1.5; PL.O.A3.2 uses it, with the intuitionistic hypothesis stated.
13. General aesthetic value is PL.O.A4.4. A verdict on a named work is AR.
14. Meaning as a concept is PL.O.A3.1. Meaning in a particular language is LN.O.A3.
15. A tradition enters Face B only as a community of answers. Each non-Western school is named by the claim it makes, never filed as a regional bin.
16. A philosophical claim that contradicts a settled measurement owes that science's warrant, in that science's pyramid.
17. Metaphilosophy (what philosophy is) is this pyramid's master section, not a sixth claim-type.
