## Introduction

**Situation.** Chemistry studies substances, from single atoms to biological macromolecules and applied materials.

**Complication.** Chemical knowledge is recorded in many overlapping schemes, so topics about the same substance are scattered by source rather than by the question being asked.

**Question.** How is everything chemistry knows about a substance divided?

**Answer.** Chemistry is divided by the question asked of a substance, from what it is to how it is made or found: composition and identity, structure and bonding, equilibrium and energy, rates and mechanisms, and synthesis and analysis.

## Governing thought

Chemistry is divided by the question asked of a substance, from what it is to how it is made or found: composition and identity, structure and bonding, equilibrium and energy, rates and mechanisms, and synthesis and analysis.

## Key line

Group of questions. Basis: kinds. Dimension: the question asked of a substance. Order: structure. The questions run from what a substance is, through how it is arranged and how it changes, to how it is made or found.

1. Composition and identity is the question of what a substance is, covering its general kind, its compound class and its role. `CH.K1`
2. Structure and bonding is the question of how the atoms of a substance are arranged and held together, and how that arrangement shapes its response to light and theory. `CH.K2`
3. Equilibrium and energy is the question of how a substance exchanges energy, charge and matter and settles between states, phases and surroundings. `CH.K3`
4. Rates and mechanisms is the question of how and how fast a substance reacts and is transformed, and by what pathway and driving agent. `CH.K4`
5. Synthesis and analysis is the question of how a substance is made and how it is found, measured and characterised. `CH.K5`
