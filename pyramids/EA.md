## 4. Face O — Objects

### EA.O.A1 Materials

Situation. Minerals, rocks, crustal fluids and soil parent material make up the solid Earth. Complication. Chemistry claims the bond and speciation; v1 defined a mineral too narrowly and gave metamorphic facies a single P–T point. Question. Which claims about Earth materials does EA own? Answer. EA owns identity, assemblage, origin class and distribution of materials in Earth reservoirs. Speciation chemistry is CH.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: material classes; dimension: the origin or state of the material; order: crystal to fluid). Members (5): Minerals · Igneous rocks · Sedimentary rocks · Metamorphic rocks · Crustal fluids and element distribution.

- EA.O.A1.1 Minerals. IMA definition: a mineral is an element or compound, normally crystalline, formed by geological processes (Nickel 1995). Its composition is definite but not necessarily fixed, allowing solid solution. Silicates dominate the crust. Identification is by habit, cleavage, hardness, optics, XRD and microprobe. Names follow IMA (W12).
- EA.O.A1.2 Igneous rocks. Formed from melt. Texture records cooling rate. Bowen's reaction series is the idealised crystallisation order of a basaltic melt cooling slowly with equilibrium reaction. Few magmas complete it. Classification follows IUGS (W12).
- EA.O.A1.3 Sedimentary rocks. Formed by deposition and lithification. Grain size, sorting and composition are the descriptors. A sedimentary structure (cross-bed, ripple, graded bed) is evidence of the depositing process. Sedimentology is the A1 process claim; the sequence is EA.O.A3.
- EA.O.A1.4 Metamorphic rocks. Formed by solid-state change. A metamorphic facies (Eskola 1920) is a set of mineral assemblages formed over a stated range of pressure and temperature. Foliation records directed stress. The rock cycle is a path among A1.2–A1.4, not a clock.
- EA.O.A1.5 Crustal fluids and element distribution. Groundwater, brines, hydrothermal fluids and melts in the crust. Element distribution in Earth reservoirs (geochemistry as distribution) is owned here. Speciation and reaction in a natural matrix is CH (uses CH, environmental chemistry cell). Soil parent material is here; the soil body is EA.O.A4.4.

Named objects: mineral, solid solution, Bowen's series, facies, foliation, cross-bed, rock cycle, ore deposit (application instance), hydrothermal fluid.

### EA.O.A2 Internal motion

Situation. Heat and gravity drive motion in the interior and lithosphere. Complication. v1 said "magnitude is energy", stated isostasy imprecisely, and left the geodynamo and geodesy unplaced. Question. Which claims about how the Earth moves inside does EA own? Answer. EA owns plate kinematics, deformation, interior structure and fields, isostatic support, and magmatism. Wave and elasticity laws are PH.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: motion claims; dimension: what moves and at what depth; order: surface plates to deep interior and back to melt). Members (5): Plate kinematics · Deformation · Interior structure and fields · Isostasy and flexure · Magmatism.

- EA.O.A2.1 Plate kinematics. The lithosphere is divided into plates that move relative to one another. Boundaries are divergent, convergent or transform. Evidence: continental fit, rock and fossil correlation, seafloor magnetic anomalies, seismicity, and geodetic motion. Euler-pole description of plate motion holds for rigid plates on a sphere.
- EA.O.A2.2 Deformation. Stress and strain in rock. Brittle faulting dominates near the surface; ductile flow dominates at depth. A fold is geometry; a fault is a fracture with offset. Earthquake size: magnitude is a logarithmic measure. Moment magnitude Mw derives from seismic moment M₀ = μAD (rigidity, rupture area, mean slip). Radiated energy rises about 32 times per unit magnitude. Intensity is felt effect (USGS, cited in audit).
- EA.O.A2.3 Interior structure and fields. Seismology infers layering from travel times. S waves do not pass through the outer core, which is evidence it is liquid. The geodynamo (the field generated in the liquid outer core) is owned here as an Earth-state claim. The MHD equations used are PH. Geodesy as a claim — figure of the Earth, gravity field — is owned here.
- EA.O.A2.4 Isostasy and flexure. The lithosphere is in buoyant equilibrium on the asthenosphere. Airy compensation uses crustal roots; Pratt compensation uses lateral density change. Many ranges are partly supported by flexure of an elastic plate. Glacial isostatic adjustment is the observable rebound after ice load.
- EA.O.A2.5 Magmatism. Melt generation at ridges (decompression), subduction zones (flux melting) and hotspots. A hotspot track is evidence of plate motion over a source, interpreted, not seen. Volcanic eruption as a hazard event enters EA.O.A5.5.

Named objects: plate, Euler pole, transform fault, moment magnitude, seismic moment, outer core, geodynamo, geoid, Airy and Pratt isostasy, flexure, hotspot.

### EA.O.A3 Deep time

Situation. The record orders and ages events across 4.5 billion years. Complication. v1 said all time-scale boundaries are reference sections, misattributed Steno's rules and filed uniformitarianism twice. Question. Which claims about the sequence and age of Earth events does EA own? Answer. EA owns relative order, correlation, numeric age, the time-scale convention and the occurrence of life in the record. The decay law is PH.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: time claims; dimension: how the time is established; order: relative before numeric before convention). Members (5): Relative order · Correlation · Numeric age · Time-scale convention · Record of life and climate.

- EA.O.A3.1 Relative order. Steno (1669) gave superposition, original horizontality and lateral continuity. Superposition holds for undisturbed, non-overturned layered sequences. Cross-cutting relations are credited to Steno and Hutton. The principle of inclusions is Lyell's. An unconformity is evidence of missing time.
- EA.O.A3.2 Correlation. Matching strata between places by fossils, lithology, magnetic polarity, stable isotopes or tephra. An index fossil is widespread, short-lived and distinctive. A polarity reversal is a time marker, not a cause.
- EA.O.A3.3 Numeric age. The decay law N = N₀e^(−λt) is owned by PH (uses PH nuclear cell). The dating claim is owned here. It holds when the system closed at a stated time, with known initial daughter and no later gain or loss. Carbon-14 serves late-Quaternary organic matter, to about 50,000 years. U–Pb and other systems serve deep time. Luminescence, dendrochronology and varves are other numeric methods.
- EA.O.A3.4 Time-scale convention. The ICS chart divides eon, era, period, epoch and age. Phanerozoic and Ediacaran boundaries are GSSPs (reference sections). Most Precambrian boundaries are GSSAs, numeric ages such as 2500 Ma for Archean–Proterozoic. Warrant W12.
- EA.O.A3.5 Record of life and climate. Occurrence of organisms in strata is owned here; their biology is BI.O.A4.5. Mass extinctions are pattern claims about that record. Paleoclimate and paleoceanography as dated states, read from proxies, are owned here. Their mechanism cites EA.O.A4.3. The Anthropocene as a formal unit was rejected by the IUGS in March 2024 (stratigraphy.org/news/152).

Named objects: superposition, unconformity, index fossil, polarity chron, closure temperature, U–Pb, GSSP, GSSA, proxy record.

### EA.O.A4 Surface and fluid envelopes

Situation. Land, water, air, ice and soil are arranged and circulate at and above the surface now. Complication. Atmosphere, ocean and cryosphere had no owner; Darcy's law lacked hypotheses; hydrogeology sat in two halves. Question. Which claims about the present surface and its fluid envelopes does EA own? Answer. EA owns landforms and process, water on and under land, the atmosphere–ocean–climate state and circulation, the cryosphere, and the soil body.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: surface and envelope claims; dimension: which part of the surface system the claim is about; order: solid surface outward then back to the skin). Members (5): Landforms and process · Water on and under land · Atmosphere, ocean and climate · Cryosphere · Soil.

- EA.O.A4.1 Landforms and process. Geomorphology: landforms as products of rock, climate and time. Processes are weathering, mass wasting, fluvial, glacial, aeolian and coastal. A graded river adjusts slope and channel to its water and sediment load. The adjustment is a tendency, not a guarantee. Uniformitarianism is a methodological premise in EA.M.H, cited here.
- EA.O.A4.2 Water on and under land. Water balance for a drainage basin over a period: P = ET + Q + ΔS (precipitation, evapotranspiration, runoff, storage change). Groundwater and aquifer claims (transmissivity, storativity) are owned here. Darcy's law q = −K∇h holds for laminar flow (Reynolds number below about 1–10) in a saturated, rigid porous medium. Its owner is EN transport closure (uses EN.O.A2); CONTESTED, alternative PH continuum. The well is EN.
- EA.O.A4.3 Atmosphere, ocean and climate. Climate is the statistics of weather over a stated period, typically 30 years (WMO). Its state, circulation and change as a dated Earth claim are owned here. Controls are latitude, altitude, continentality, ocean currents and circulation. The governing laws (Navier–Stokes, radiative transfer, thermodynamics) are PH. Ocean circulation, sea level and marine biogeochemical state are owned here; marine biology is BI.O.A5. Köppen–Geiger is a classification (W12), not a mechanism. Climate change attribution claims owe W2 and W5. Atmospheric electricity and the upper atmosphere are refused to PH (space physics).
- EA.O.A4.4 Cryosphere. Glaciers, ice sheets, sea ice, permafrost and snow as surface mass. Mass balance (accumulation minus ablation) is the core claim. Glacier flow by Glen's flow law assumes power-law creep of polycrystalline ice with exponent near 3 (UNVERIFIED exponent range). Glacial landforms are EA.O.A4.1.
- EA.O.A4.5 Soil. The soil body: weathered parent material plus organic matter, organised in horizons. Pedology owns the profile here. Soil mechanics (effective stress, Mohr–Coulomb) is EN. Soil chemistry is CH. Soil biota are BI.O.A5.3.

Named objects: drainage basin, base level, water balance, aquifer, Darcy's law, climate normal, thermohaline circulation, mass balance, permafrost, soil horizon, Köppen–Geiger class.

### EA.O.A5 Human spatial organization

Situation. People locate, move, and make places and regions under constraint. Complication. v1 filed distance decay three ways, defined vulnerability wrongly, gave hazard and risk no cell, and had no home for coupled human–natural systems. Question. Which claims about the spatial organisation of people does EA own? Answer. EA owns location and interaction, region and place, economic and urban pattern, political space, and nature–society with risk.

Key line (inductive; plural noun: spatial-organisation claims; dimension: what organises the pattern; order: distance, then meaning, then market, then authority, then nature). Members (5): Location and interaction · Region and place · Economic and urban pattern · Political space · Nature–society and risk.

- EA.O.A5.1 Location and interaction. Site and situation; absolute and relative location. Distance decay is the claim that interaction falls with distance, modified by origin and destination size. It is owned here. Gravity-model estimation is EA.M.5. In trade, the gravity equation has structural derivations (Anderson 1979; Anderson and van Wincoop 2003); that derivation is EC. In migration, gravity models are mostly reduced-form. CONTESTED for migration.
- EA.O.A5.2 Region and place. A region is a delimited area of stated similarity (formal), flow (functional) or perception (perceptual). Place is a location invested with meaning; its warrant is W8. Area studies as a catalog of regions is refused (sector, not a cell).
- EA.O.A5.3 Economic and urban pattern. Where production and exchange happen. Central place theory predicts a hexagonal hierarchy on an isotropic plain with uniform population and transport, and rational, nearest-centre consumers. The spatial pattern is owned here. Bid-rent as allocation is EC. Urban models (concentric, sector, multiple nuclei) are ideal types.
- EA.O.A5.4 Political space. Territory and boundary as spatial claims. A boundary is a claim enforced, not a natural line. The authority behind it is PO. Its legal validity is LA.
- EA.O.A5.5 Nature–society and risk. Coupled human–natural systems: pressure, state, impact and response, under a stated boundary. This absorbs environmental science claims (Grok "EV"). Risk = hazard × exposure × vulnerability (IPCC AR6 WGII glossary). Vulnerability is the propensity to be adversely affected: sensitivity plus lack of capacity to cope and adapt. Exposure is a separate factor. The hazard cites EA.O.A2 or A4. Policy is PO; price is EC; duty is LA. Population counts are MS.O.A5.

Named objects: site, situation, distance decay, formal region, place, central place, bid-rent pattern, boundary, territory, hazard, exposure, vulnerability, risk.

Pattison (1964) mapping, corrected. Spatial tradition → A5.1 and the map pattern of A4. Earth-science tradition → A1–A4. Man–land tradition → A5.5. Area-studies tradition → refused as a catalog of regions.

### Heuristics register (outside the count)

EA.M.H. Each fails in a known case.
- Uniformitarianism: "the present is the key to the past", within the range of present processes. Fails for impacts and for early-Earth conditions.
- Water flows downhill. Fails in a pumped aquifer.
- Contour V's point upstream. Fails at an overhang or karst.
- Tobler's first law: near things are more related than distant things. Fails across barriers.

## 8. Assignment rules

1. A landform process is one claim, filed at EA.O.A4.1. The rock record of it is EA.O.A3. The equation is not filed twice.
2. GIS and cartography are methods (EA.M.4). GIScience is a bridge (EA.B.C8), not a claim about the Earth.
3. A fossil's biology is BI.O.A4.5. Its occurrence in a stratum is EA.O.A3.5.
4. Physical laws of fluids and radiation are PH. The dated state and circulation of ocean, atmosphere and ice are EA.O.A4.3–A4.4.
5. Decay law N = N₀e^(−λt) is PH. The age it gives under closure is EA.O.A3.3. Pharmacokinetic and first-order chemical half-lives are MD and CH; the registry disambiguates.
6. Groundwater flow claims are EA.O.A4.2. Darcy's law as closure is EN.O.A2 (contested). The well is EN.
7. Element distribution in Earth reservoirs is EA.O.A1.5. Speciation in a natural matrix is CH. Each cites the other.
8. Soil body and horizons are EA.O.A4.5. Effective stress and Mohr–Coulomb are EN.
9. Distance decay is EA.O.A5.1. Gravity-model estimation is EA.M.5. The trade gravity derivation is EC.
10. Bid-rent as allocation is EC. Its spatial pattern is EA.O.A5.3.
11. Boundary as spatial claim is EA.O.A5.4. Authority is PO. Validity is LA.
12. Risk = hazard × exposure × vulnerability is EA.O.A5.5. The hazard cites A2 or A4.
13. Environmental pressure–state–impact claims are EA.O.A5.5. Policy is PO; price is EC; duty is LA ("incidence is economics, the duty is law").
14. Biogeography is BI.O.A5.5 (contested). Biological C and N fluxes are BI.O.A5.4 (contested). EA cites both.
15. Human demography is MS.O.A5. Population geography cites it.
16. Seismic wave equation and elasticity are PH. Earth-structure inference is EA.O.A2.3.
