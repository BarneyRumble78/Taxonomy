# IK — top-down Minto pyramid

Built by scripts/minto_pass.py from the grouped classification. Three rules (Minto pp. 9-11): each summary states the idea of its group, members are the same kind of idea, and they are in a stated order.

## Introduction

**Situation.** Indigenous knowledge systems of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander, Māori and Pacific Peoples hold bodies of science, environmental understanding, law, health and education knowledge.

**Complication.** The claims about these systems are scattered across peoples, sciences, health, education and governance fields, so the same aspect appears in many places.

**Question.** How is everything in Indigenous knowledge divided?

**Answer.** Indigenous knowledge is divided by the aspect of a knowledge system a claim is about, running from its ordering principles through its relations to land and waters, its governance and custom, and its transmission to its research and data.

## Governing thought

Indigenous knowledge is divided by the aspect of a knowledge system a claim is about, running from its ordering principles through its relations to land and waters, its governance and custom, and its transmission to its research and data.

## Key line

Group of aspects. Basis: cause_effect. Dimension: the aspect of a knowledge system the claim is about. Order: time. The aspects run from the system's own principles, through its relations to place and its governance, to how it is passed on and finally how it meets outside research.

1. Ordering principles are the way an Indigenous knowledge system organises what is known, through its sciences, philosophies, worldviews and the technologies and innovations it generates. `IK.K1`
2. Relations to land and waters are the aspect of Indigenous knowledge concerned with how peoples know, use, conserve and manage country, sea and living things. `IK.K2`
3. Governance and custom are the aspect of Indigenous knowledge concerned with how communities are ruled, how roles and rights are defined, and how health and wellbeing are cared for. `IK.K3`
4. Transmission is the aspect of Indigenous knowledge concerned with how it is taught, spoken, written, preserved and handed on to new generations. `IK.K4`
5. Research and data are the aspect of Indigenous knowledge concerned with the methods, data technologies and global studies through which it is investigated and governed by outside research. `IK.K5`
