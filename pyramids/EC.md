## 2. Governing thought
Economics is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five scales of allocation: choice, markets, mechanisms and institutions, the aggregate economy, and welfare and public choice.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### EC.O.A1 Choice
**SCQA.** Allocation begins with agents choosing under constraints. Choice theory is precise, but people often depart from its axioms. What is claimed about how agents choose? Key line: preferences, choice under risk, behavioural departures, intertemporal choice.
- EC.O.A1.1 Preferences and demand — complete, transitive and continuous preferences have a utility representation (Debreu; proof uses MA). Demand theory follows, with the Slutsky decomposition.
- EC.O.A1.2 Choice under risk — expected utility follows from the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms (completeness, transitivity, continuity, independence).
- EC.O.A1.3 Behavioural departures — the Allais paradox violates independence; prospect theory fits loss aversion. The psychological mechanism is MS; the allocative consequence is EC.
- EC.O.A1.4 Intertemporal choice — exponential discounting is time-consistent; quasi-hyperbolic discounting is not.

### EC.O.A2 Markets
**SCQA.** Choices meet in markets, where prices coordinate them. When do markets clear, and with what result? Key line: competitive equilibrium, market power, information, factor markets.
- EC.O.A2.1 Competitive equilibrium — a general equilibrium exists given continuous, convex, locally non-satiated preferences, convex production, and endowments in the interior (Arrow–Debreu; proof MA).
- EC.O.A2.2 Market power — monopoly, oligopoly, entry. The equilibrium of the Cournot or Bertrand game is MA. Which game the firms are in is ST. The resulting prices and quantities are EC.
- EC.O.A2.3 Information — adverse selection (Akerlof), moral hazard, signalling. A market can unravel when quality is hidden and sellers know more than buyers.
- EC.O.A2.4 Factor markets — labour and capital; wages and returns.
- EC.O.A2.5 Tax incidence — in a competitive market, the burden falls by relative elasticities of supply and demand, not by who legally pays. Incidence is EC; the duty to pay is LA; computing the tax is BU.

### EC.O.A3 Mechanisms and institutions
**SCQA.** Many allocations are settled by designed rules or by institutions, not by anonymous prices. Which rules produce which allocations? Key line: mechanism design, bargaining, transaction costs, institutions.
- EC.O.A3.1 Mechanism design — the revelation principle: any equilibrium outcome of a mechanism can be achieved by a direct, truthful mechanism (proof MA). Vickrey auctions make truthful bidding dominant for private values.
- EC.O.A3.2 Bargaining — Rubinstein: with two players, alternating offers, an infinite horizon, complete information and discount factors below one, there is a unique subgame-perfect equilibrium with immediate agreement. The theorem is MA. The choice of bargaining protocol is ST. The split is EC.
- EC.O.A3.3 Transaction costs — Coase: with well-defined property rights, zero transaction costs and no wealth effects, bargaining reaches an efficient outcome whatever the initial assignment. With positive costs the assignment matters, and that is the theorem's main use.
- EC.O.A3.4 Institutions — property rights, contracts and firms as allocative rules. Political authority is PO; legal validity is LA.
- EC.O.A3.5 Repeated interaction — folk theorem: in an infinitely repeated game with a discount factor close enough to one, any feasible, strictly individually rational payoff is sustainable in equilibrium (with a full-dimensionality condition for more than two players). Theorem MA; use in collusion or cooperation EC; choice of game ST.

### EC.O.A4 The aggregate economy
**SCQA.** Output, prices and employment move together across an economy. What determines them? Key line: growth, fluctuations, money and inflation, open economies.
- EC.O.A4.1 Growth — the Solow model: with diminishing returns to capital and constant returns to scale, output per worker converges to a steady state, and long-run growth needs technical change.
- EC.O.A4.2 Fluctuations — business cycles; unemployment. The Phillips curve is an empirical relation that proved unstable once expectations adjusted.
- EC.O.A4.3 Money and inflation — the quantity equation MV = PY is an identity. A theory needs assumptions about V and Y. Central bank rules such as the Taylor rule are heuristics or policy prescriptions.
- EC.O.A4.4 Open economies — comparative advantage (Ricardo): with two goods and differing opportunity costs, both countries gain from specialisation and trade. Heckscher–Ohlin and Stolper–Samuelson hold under their stated factor and technology assumptions.
- EC.O.A4.5 Expectations and policy — the Lucas critique: relations estimated under one policy regime need not hold under another if agents' expectations change.

### EC.O.A5 Welfare and public choice
**SCQA.** Societies judge and alter allocations collectively. When is an allocation good, and can a collective choose consistently? Key line: welfare theorems, market failure, public finance, social choice.
- EC.O.A5.1 First welfare theorem — with complete markets, price-taking agents, locally non-satiated preferences and no externalities, every competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient. Theorem MA; use EC.
- EC.O.A5.2 Second welfare theorem — with convex preferences and technologies, continuity and lump-sum transfers, any Pareto-efficient allocation can be supported as a competitive equilibrium. Theorem MA; use EC.
- EC.O.A5.3 Market failure — externalities, public goods, common pool resources. Pigouvian taxes correct an externality when its marginal damage is known.
- EC.O.A5.4 Public finance — taxes and spending. A VAT is levied on value added at each stage, with credit for tax paid on inputs. A retail sales tax is levied once, on the final sale. Their economic incidence can be similar, but their collection differs.
- EC.O.A5.5 Social choice — Arrow: with at least three alternatives and a finite set of voters, no rule satisfies unrestricted domain, weak Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives and non-dictatorship together. Theorem MA; the meaning for collective choice is EC; the normative argument is PL.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place a claim by the scale at which the allocation is settled.
2. The theorem is MA, the allocation is EC, the choice of game is ST.
3. Incidence is economics; the duty is law; the computation is business.
4. A firm's accounts and capital structure are BU. The general-equilibrium price of risk is EC.
5. A psychological mechanism is MS; its allocative effect is EC.
6. Political authority over an allocation is PO; the legal rule is LA.
7. A dated economic event is HI; the explanation of its allocation is EC.
8. A normative welfare argument is PL; the efficiency claim is EC.
