## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Law states what is valid, owed and permitted within a legal system, and how a forum decides it.
**Complication.** v1's governing thought listed four relations ending "order a remedy", while its key line listed five ending "constitute the forum". This breaks R7. v1 also had no first descent.
**Question.** By what dimension can every legal claim be placed once, with all members stated identically?
**Answer.** By the legal relation the claim states. There are five relations: what counts as law, what people owe each other, what follows a wrong, what public power may do, and how a forum decides.

Its objects divide into five legal relations: sources, obligations, wrongs and remedies, public power, and forums and proof.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### LA.O.A1 Sources

**Situation.** Before any duty, a legal claim needs a valid source. Every legal system has some test for what counts as its law.
**Complication.** Systems differ in what they count as law. Common-law systems treat judicial decisions as binding sources, while civil-law systems give codes priority. Some systems also recognise custom, Indigenous law or international law, each on different terms.
**Question.** What is law in this system, and on what ranking?
**Answer.** Five kinds of source can supply valid law: legislation, case law, custom and tikanga, international law, and the validity test that ranks them. A claim is placed in A1 when it asserts that a rule is part of a stated system at a stated date.

Key line (plural noun: sources of law; dimension: the origin a rule claims; order: enacted, then judge-made, then customary, then external, then the test that ranks them). Members (5): Legislation · Case law · Custom and tikanga · International law · Validity.

- LA.O.A1.1 Legislation — **statutes and delegated legislation are valid when made by the procedure the system recognises.**
  - LA.O.A1.1.1 In New Zealand, Parliament is sovereign: a court may not strike down an Act of Parliament for inconsistency with a higher norm. This is testable against the absence of any strike-down power in the Senior Courts Act 2016 and the NZ Bill of Rights Act 1990 s 4.
  - LA.O.A1.1.2 Delegated legislation is valid only within the power granted by its parent Act. A court may hold a regulation ultra vires and of no effect. New Zealand regulations are also open to disallowance by the House.
  - LA.O.A1.1.3 Statutory meaning is fixed from text read in light of purpose and context (Legislation Act 2019 s 10). A reading that ignores stated purpose is open to challenge on this ground.
  - LA.O.A1.1.4 A later statute prevails over an earlier inconsistent one, unless the earlier one is entrenched or the later one shows no intent to repeal. Implied repeal is a defeater for any claim under A1.1.
- LA.O.A1.2 Case law — **precedent binds lower courts in common-law systems (stare decisis); its weight in civil-law systems is lower.**
  - LA.O.A1.2.1 Only the ratio decidendi binds. Obiter dicta are persuasive only. Whether a given statement is ratio is itself a question a later court may dispute.
  - LA.O.A1.2.2 Apex courts in many common-law systems may depart from their own earlier decisions. The House of Lords Practice Statement (1966) is a standard example in the English line.
  - LA.O.A1.2.3 In civil-law systems a settled line of decisions (jurisprudence constante) carries weight, but a single decision does not bind as a source. The degree of convergence between the two traditions is CONTESTED.
- LA.O.A1.3 Custom and tikanga — in New Zealand, the Supreme Court in Ellis v R (2022) held that tikanga Māori forms part of the common law, as a source to be applied with care (UNVERIFIED wording; Māori-led review required). The tikanga itself, as tradition-internal law, is owned by IK.
- LA.O.A1.4 International law — **treaties and custom are the main sources (ICJ Statute art. 38); domestic effect depends on the system.**
  - LA.O.A1.4.1 Customary international law requires general state practice accepted as law (opinio juris). A claim that lacks evidence of either element is not placeable here.
  - LA.O.A1.4.2 New Zealand is broadly dualist. An unincorporated treaty creates no directly enforceable domestic right. Courts still prefer readings of statutes consistent with international obligations.
  - LA.O.A1.4.3 A treaty that conflicts with a peremptory norm (jus cogens) is void (Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties 1969 art. 53). The list of peremptory norms is CONTESTED at its edges.
- LA.O.A1.5 Validity — **what makes a rule law in a system (for example Hart's rule of recognition). The concept analysis is PL; the claim that a rule is valid here is LA.**
  - LA.O.A1.5.1 Sources form a ranking. In New Zealand, statute overrides inconsistent common law, and common law fills gaps that statute leaves. A validity claim must state where the rule sits in this ranking.
  - LA.O.A1.5.2 Validity is distinct from efficacy. A rule may be valid but widely ignored. Measuring compliance is PO or MS work; LA states only the validity claim.

Named objects: stare decisis, ratio decidendi, ultra vires, implied repeal, purposive interpretation, dualism, opinio juris, jus cogens, rule of recognition (concept owned by PL). Purposive interpretation as a method assumes that a statute has a discoverable purpose and that text and purpose can be read together.

### LA.O.A2 Obligations

**Situation.** Valid law creates duties and rights between persons. Most legal work concerns these private relations.
**Complication.** Duties arise from different events. Some are chosen, as in contract. Some attach to things, as in property. Some arise from relationships of trust, family status, death or the state's taxing power. Each has its own formation rules and defences.
**Question.** What is owed, by whom, and on what event?
**Answer.** Five families of obligation cover the field: contract, property, trusts and equity, family and succession, and tax duty. Each is placed by the event that creates the duty.

Key line (plural noun: obligations; dimension: the event that creates the duty; order: chosen, then attached to things, then fiduciary, then status-based, then imposed by the state). Members (5): Contract · Property · Trusts and equity · Family and succession · Tax duty.

- LA.O.A2.1 Contract — **formation needs offer, acceptance, consideration (in common law) and intention to create legal relations.**
  - LA.O.A2.1.1 Damages for breach are limited by remoteness. Recoverable loss is that which arises naturally or was within the parties' contemplation (Hadley v Baxendale (1854)).
  - LA.O.A2.1.2 A contract may be set aside or varied for misrepresentation, mistake or duress. In New Zealand these rules are largely codified in the Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017.
  - LA.O.A2.1.3 Privity limits enforcement to the parties, subject to statutory exceptions. New Zealand allows a named third-party beneficiary to enforce a promise under the 2017 Act.
- LA.O.A2.2 Property — **title, possession and land registration (Torrens in NZ and Australia) state who holds rights in things.** Title to goods sold passes under the sale contract and the governing law, not under Incoterms.
  - LA.O.A2.2.1 Under a Torrens system, registration confers title that is indefeasible except for stated exceptions, chiefly fraud. In New Zealand this is set by the Land Transfer Act 2017.
  - LA.O.A2.2.2 A seller cannot pass better title than they hold (nemo dat), subject to statutory exceptions for buyers in good faith.
  - LA.O.A2.2.3 Possession gives a title good against everyone except a person with a better right. Priority between competing interests is decided by stated rules, not by fairness at large.
- LA.O.A2.3 Trusts and equity — **a trust separates legal title from beneficial interest and imposes fiduciary duties on the trustee.**
  - LA.O.A2.3.1 An express trust requires certainty of intention, subject matter and objects. A purported trust that lacks one of the three fails.
  - LA.O.A2.3.2 A fiduciary must not profit from the position or act in conflict of interest without informed consent. Breach gives equitable remedies, including account of profits.
  - LA.O.A2.3.3 In New Zealand the Trusts Act 2019 states mandatory and default trustee duties. A trust deed cannot exclude the mandatory duties.
- LA.O.A2.4 Family and succession — **status and death create duties between relatives and over estates.**
  - LA.O.A2.4.1 Under the Property (Relationships) Act 1976, relationship property is presumed to be shared equally on separation, subject to stated exceptions.
  - LA.O.A2.4.2 In decisions about a child's care, the child's welfare and best interests are the first and paramount consideration (Care of Children Act 2004).
  - LA.O.A2.4.3 Testamentary freedom is limited. Close family may claim against an estate that fails to make adequate provision for them (Family Protection Act 1955).
- LA.O.A2.5 Tax duty — **the liability to pay, set by statute.** Incidence is EC and computation is BU.
  - LA.O.A2.5.1 No tax may be levied except by or under an Act of Parliament. A charge without statutory authority is unlawful.
  - LA.O.A2.5.2 A general anti-avoidance rule lets the revenue disregard arrangements with a more than merely incidental tax-avoidance purpose (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1). Its boundary with lawful tax planning is CONTESTED in each case.

Named objects: offer and acceptance, consideration, remoteness rule, privity, Torrens title, indefeasibility, nemo dat, three certainties, fiduciary duty, equal sharing presumption, paramountcy principle, general anti-avoidance rule. The Coase theorem, often cited here, is owned by EC (uses EC); it assumes zero transaction costs and well-defined property rights.

### LA.O.A3 Wrongs and remedies

**Situation.** When duties are broken, law states the wrong and what follows. Wrongs may be private, against a person, or public, against the community.
**Complication.** The same act can be both a tort and a crime. The two use different standards of proof, different parties and different consequences. Remedies also differ: some compensate, some compel, and some punish.
**Question.** What counts as a legal wrong, and what follows from it?
**Answer.** Three members cover the field: torts as civil wrongs, crimes as public wrongs, and the remedies that follow either.

Key line (plural noun: responses to breach; dimension: who pursues the wrong and to what end; order: private wrong, public wrong, consequence). Members (3): Torts · Crimes · Remedies.

- LA.O.A3.1 Torts — **negligence requires a duty of care, breach, causation and damage.** New Zealand's accident compensation scheme (ACC) bars most personal-injury suits.
  - LA.O.A3.1.1 Breach is measured against the reasonable person in the defendant's position. Expert evidence of common practice is relevant but not conclusive.
  - LA.O.A3.1.2 Factual causation is usually tested by the "but for" question. Departures for multiple or indeterminate causes are CONTESTED across common-law systems.
  - LA.O.A3.1.3 The ACC bar covers compensatory claims for personal injury covered by the Accident Compensation Act 2001. Claims for exemplary damages remain available in New Zealand.
  - LA.O.A3.1.4 Pure economic loss is recoverable in negligence only in limited classes. The scope of those classes differs between New Zealand, Australia and England.
- LA.O.A3.2 Crimes — **liability needs the prohibited act and the fault element, unless the offence is strict.**
  - LA.O.A3.2.1 The prosecution bears the burden of proving guilt beyond reasonable doubt. Reverse onus provisions are exceptions that must be justified against NZBORA s 25(c).
  - LA.O.A3.2.2 Courts presume that a statutory offence requires fault unless Parliament clearly excludes it. In New Zealand, public welfare offences are usually strict, with a defence of total absence of fault.
  - LA.O.A3.2.3 Defences such as self-defence, compulsion and insanity negate or excuse liability. Each has stated elements in the Crimes Act 1961.
- LA.O.A3.3 Remedies — **damages, injunctions, specific performance and sentences are the legal consequences of a proved wrong.**
  - LA.O.A3.3.1 Contract damages aim to put the claimant where performance would have put them. Tort damages aim to restore the position before the wrong.
  - LA.O.A3.3.2 Specific performance and injunctions are discretionary. Courts grant them chiefly where damages would be inadequate.
  - LA.O.A3.3.3 Sentencing follows stated purposes and principles, including consistency and the least restrictive appropriate outcome (Sentencing Act 2002).

Named objects: negligence, duty of care, reasonable person standard, but-for test, ACC bar, mens rea presumption, strict liability, burden of proof, expectation measure, specific performance, injunction. The Hand formula (uses EC) models breach as a failure to take precautions cheaper than expected harm; it assumes measurable probabilities and a single welfare scale, and its use as a legal test is CONTESTED.

### LA.O.A4 Public power

**Situation.** Law also binds the state. Public bodies act only under legal authority.
**Complication.** In New Zealand no court can strike down an Act of Parliament. Yet courts review executive action, read statutes consistently with rights, and give effect to Te Tiriti where statute incorporates it. Limits on public power come from several sources with different strengths.
**Question.** What may public power do, and what limits it?
**Answer.** Four members cover the field: constitutional law, administrative law, human rights, and Te Tiriti o Waitangi as incorporated in law.

Key line (plural noun: limits on public power; dimension: the source of the limit; order: structural, then procedural and substantive review, then rights, then Tiriti). Members (4): Constitutional law · Administrative law · Human rights · Te Tiriti o Waitangi.

- LA.O.A4.1 Constitutional law — **New Zealand has no single entrenched constitution; it relies on statutes such as the Constitution Act 1986, conventions and Te Tiriti.**
  - LA.O.A4.1.1 A few electoral provisions are entrenched. Amending them requires 75 per cent of the House or a majority at a referendum (Electoral Act 1993 s 268).
  - LA.O.A4.1.2 Constitutional conventions bind politically but are not enforced by courts. A claim that a convention exists is tested by practice and by the actors' sense of obligation.
  - LA.O.A4.1.3 Whether Parliament can bind its successors by self-entrenchment is CONTESTED; the s 268 entrenchment is itself not entrenched.
- LA.O.A4.2 Administrative law — **judicial review tests public decisions for illegality, irrationality and procedural impropriety.**
  - LA.O.A4.2.1 Review concerns the lawfulness of the decision process, not its merits. A court that substitutes its own view on the merits exceeds review.
  - LA.O.A4.2.2 A decision-maker who fails to give a fair hearing breaches natural justice. NZBORA s 27(1) states this right against public decision-makers.
  - LA.O.A4.2.3 A clear promise or settled practice can found a legitimate expectation. Whether such an expectation can bind substantively, not only procedurally, is CONTESTED.
- LA.O.A4.3 Human rights — **NZ Bill of Rights Act 1990: rights may be subject to reasonable limits demonstrably justified in a free and democratic society (s 5).**
  - LA.O.A4.3.1 Courts must prefer a meaning consistent with the Act where one is available (s 6), but cannot invalidate an inconsistent enactment (s 4).
  - LA.O.A4.3.2 The Supreme Court has held that senior courts may declare an enactment inconsistent with the Act (Attorney-General v Taylor (2018)). The declaration does not affect the enactment's validity.
  - LA.O.A4.3.3 Justification under s 5 is tested by proportionality. The limit must serve a sufficiently important objective and impair the right no more than reasonably necessary.
- LA.O.A4.4 Te Tiriti o Waitangi (1840) — **its legal force depends on incorporation in statute.** The Waitangi Tribunal (est. 1975) makes recommendations, mostly not binding.
  - LA.O.A4.4.1 Where a statute requires a decision-maker to give effect to, or take account of, the principles of the Treaty, failure to do so is reviewable as illegality.
  - LA.O.A4.4.2 The Tribunal's recommendations bind the Crown only in stated statutory cases, chiefly the resumption of certain Crown forest and former state enterprise land (UNVERIFIED scope).

Named objects: parliamentary sovereignty, entrenchment, constitutional convention, judicial review, the three review grounds, natural justice, legitimate expectation, consistent interpretation (s 6), declaration of inconsistency, proportionality test. The proportionality test as a method assumes that the right and the objective can be compared on a common scale; that assumption is CONTESTED (argument PL).

### LA.O.A5 Forums and proof

**Situation.** A legal claim is settled before a forum, on facts proved to a standard.
**Complication.** Several forums may claim the same dispute. Courts, tribunals and arbitrators follow different procedures. Rules of evidence exclude some relevant material, and standards of proof differ by case type.
**Question.** What forum decides, by what procedure, on what proof?
**Answer.** Four members cover the field: jurisdiction and courts, procedure, evidence and standards of proof, and arbitration with other dispute resolution.

Key line (plural noun: elements of decision; dimension: which part of deciding the claim concerns; order: who decides, how, on what proof, outside the courts). Members (4): Jurisdiction and courts · Procedure · Evidence and standards of proof · Arbitration and alternative dispute resolution.

- LA.O.A5.1 Jurisdiction and courts — **jurisdiction states which forum may hear a claim.** The NZ Supreme Court replaced Privy Council appeals from 2004.
  - LA.O.A5.1.1 New Zealand's general courts form a hierarchy: District Court, High Court, Court of Appeal and Supreme Court. Decisions of a higher court bind those below.
  - LA.O.A5.1.2 In cross-border civil claims a court may decline jurisdiction where another forum is clearly more appropriate (forum non conveniens).
  - LA.O.A5.1.3 Which system's law governs a cross-border claim is fixed by choice-of-law rules. The forum and the governing law may differ.
- LA.O.A5.2 Procedure — **pleadings, disclosure, trial and appeal follow the forum's rules.**
  - LA.O.A5.2.1 Common-law trials are adversarial: parties lead the evidence and the judge decides on it. Civil-law trials give the judge a larger role in gathering evidence.
  - LA.O.A5.2.2 A final judgment bars the same parties from relitigating the same cause of action (res judicata). Issue estoppel extends this to issues already decided.
  - LA.O.A5.2.3 Appeals to the NZ Supreme Court require leave. Leave turns on matters of general or public importance or a substantial miscarriage of justice.
- LA.O.A5.3 Evidence and standards of proof — **beyond reasonable doubt in criminal cases; balance of probabilities in civil cases.**
  - LA.O.A5.3.1 Relevant evidence is admissible unless excluded (Evidence Act 2006 s 7). The judge must exclude evidence whose unfairly prejudicial effect outweighs its probative value (s 8).
  - LA.O.A5.3.2 Hearsay is admissible only where the circumstances give reasonable assurance of reliability and the maker is unavailable or undue expense would follow (s 18).
  - LA.O.A5.3.3 Improperly obtained evidence is excluded where exclusion is proportionate to the impropriety, on a balancing test (s 30).
- LA.O.A5.4 Arbitration and alternative dispute resolution — **awards are enforceable under the New York Convention (1958) in contracting states.**
  - LA.O.A5.4.1 Grounds to refuse enforcement of a foreign award are limited to those in Convention art. V. A court may not review the award's merits.
  - LA.O.A5.4.2 An arbitral tribunal may rule on its own jurisdiction (competence-competence), subject to later court control. New Zealand's Arbitration Act 1996 adopts the UNCITRAL Model Law.
  - LA.O.A5.4.3 Mediation produces no binding decision unless the parties settle. A settlement then binds as a contract (uses LA.O.A2.1).

Named objects: jurisdiction, court hierarchy, forum non conveniens, choice of law, adversarial procedure, res judicata, issue estoppel, standard of proof, relevance rule, hearsay rule, s 30 balancing test, New York Convention, competence-competence, UNCITRAL Model Law. Standards of proof are sometimes modelled as probability thresholds (uses ST); the model assumes fact-finders assign coherent probabilities, and its fit to legal practice is CONTESTED.

## 8. Assignment rules

2. Incidence is economics; the duty is law.
3. What law is, as a concept, is PL; what is law in this system is LA.
4. A dated event in legal history is HI; the rule then in force is LA.
5. The political cause of a law is PO.
6. Tikanga as tradition is IK; tikanga as recognised by a state court is LA.
7. A proved fact is settled for the forum only (W10 does not travel).
