## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Politics studies who holds power over a community, how they gain and keep it, and what they do with it.
**Complication.** v1 was master-level only. It mixed normative argument about legitimacy (PL) with empirical claims, and it misstated selectorate theory (the key quantity is the winning coalition).
**Question.** By what dimension can every empirical claim about political power be placed once?
**Answer.** By the aspect of power the claim concerns: its authority, its institutions, the behaviour of the governed, its policies, and its relations beyond the state.

## 2. Governing thought
Politics is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five aspects of power: authority, institutions, political behaviour, policy and administration, and relations among states.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### PO.O.A1 Authority
**SCQA.** Power is political when it claims the right to rule. Empirical politics can study whether people accept that claim, though not whether they should. What is claimed about authority? Key line: states and sovereignty, legitimacy as belief, regimes.
- PO.O.A1.1 States and sovereignty — Weber's state: a community that successfully claims a monopoly of legitimate force in a territory.
- PO.O.A1.2 Legitimacy as belief — measured acceptance of a ruler's right. Whether the rule is justified is PL.
- PO.O.A1.3 Regimes — democracy, autocracy, hybrid regimes, classified by stated criteria (V-Dem, Polity: W12 for coding rules).

### PO.O.A2 Institutions
**SCQA.** Authority acts through rules and organisations. Which rules produce which outcomes? Key line: constitutions, electoral systems, parties, legislatures and executives.
- PO.O.A2.1 Constitutional design — presidential and parliamentary systems, federalism. The legal text is LA; its political effect is PO.
- PO.O.A2.2 Electoral systems — Duverger's law: plurality rule in single-member districts tends toward two-party competition. It is a tendency with known exceptions (e.g. India, Canada).
- PO.O.A2.3 Parties — party systems, organisation, cleavages.
- PO.O.A2.4 Legislatures and executives — veto players: policy stability rises with the number and ideological distance of veto players (Tsebelis).
- PO.O.A2.5 Selectorate theory — leaders keep office by rewarding a winning coalition W drawn from a selectorate S. When W is small relative to S, leaders favour private goods over public ones (Bueno de Mesquita et al., 2003).

### PO.O.A3 Political behaviour
**SCQA.** The governed vote, protest, believe and organise. What explains their political action? Key line: voting, public opinion, participation and movements, conflict within states.
- PO.O.A3.1 Voting — the median voter theorem: with one policy dimension, single-peaked preferences and majority rule, the median voter's ideal point beats any other. Theorem MA.B.C4; use PO.
- PO.O.A3.2 Public opinion — attitudes measured by survey. The cognitive mechanism is MS.
- PO.O.A3.3 Participation and social movements — turnout, collective action. Olson: large groups under-provide collective goods without selective incentives.
- PO.O.A3.4 Conflict within states — civil war onset, coups, revolution. The conduct of the fighting is ST.

### PO.O.A4 Policy and administration
**SCQA.** Governments make and carry out policy. How is policy made and delivered? Key line: agenda and decision, bureaucracy, implementation, policy effects.
- PO.O.A4.1 Agenda and decision — punctuated equilibrium; the multiple streams model (a framework, W9).
- PO.O.A4.2 Bureaucracy — delegation, principal–agent control (theory EC.O.A2.3).
- PO.O.A4.3 Implementation — the gap between decision and delivery.
- PO.O.A4.4 Policy effects — the causal effect of a policy is owned by the field of the outcome (EC, MD, ED), and PO cites it. The political cause of the policy is PO.

### PO.O.A5 Relations among states
**SCQA.** States act in a system without a common government. What patterns hold among them? Key line: international order, cooperation and institutions, war and peace.
- PO.O.A5.1 International order — anarchy, balance of power, hegemony. Realist, liberal and constructivist claims are competing programmes (CONTESTED).
- PO.O.A5.2 Cooperation and international institutions — regimes, alliances, the UN. The treaty's legal force is LA.
- PO.O.A5.3 War and peace — the democratic peace: pairs of democracies rarely fight each other. It is an empirical regularity; its causes are CONTESTED. The strategy of coercion and conduct is ST.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place an empirical claim by the aspect of power.
2. Whether rule is justified is PL; whether people believe it is justified is PO.
3. Legal validity is LA; the political cause or effect of a law is PO.
4. The policy's causal effect belongs to its outcome field; its political origin is PO.
5. The conduct of war, coercion and the choice of game are ST; war onset and the international order are PO.
6. The theorem is MA; its political use is PO.
7. A dated political event is HI; its explanation is PO.
