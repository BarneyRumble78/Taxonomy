## Introduction

**Situation.** Philosophy asks questions about reality, knowledge, reasoning and value, and has been studied across many traditions and periods.

**Complication.** Its subfields have been listed under many overlapping labels, so the same topic appears under several headings and kinds of question are mixed together.

**Question.** How is everything in philosophy divided by the kind of claim at issue?

**Answer.** Philosophy is divided by the kind of claim at issue: what there is, how we know and think, how we reason and mean, and what is valuable.

## Governing thought

Philosophy is divided by the kind of claim at issue: what there is, how we know and think, how we reason and mean, and what is valuable.

## Key line

Group of kinds of claim. Basis: kinds. Dimension: the kind of claim at issue. Order: structure. Ordered from claims about reality, to claims about knowing it, to claims about reasoning, to claims about value.

1. Claims about being ask what exists and what ultimately is, as in metaphysics and the philosophy of religion. `PL.K1`
2. Claims about knowledge and mind ask how we know and what minds are, as in epistemology, philosophy of mind and consciousness, and the philosophy of the sciences. `PL.K2`
3. Claims about reason and meaning ask what makes inference valid and how language means, as in logic and the philosophy of language. `PL.K3`
4. Claims about value ask what is good, right, beautiful and just, as in ethics, aesthetics and political and social philosophy. `PL.K4`
