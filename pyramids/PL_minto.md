## Introduction

**Situation.** Philosophy asks the most general questions about reality, thought, language, value and inquiry.

**Complication.** The inputs mix subject matter with catalogue classes, miscellaneous headings, historical periods and educational settings, so the field has no clear shape.

**Question.** How is everything in philosophy divided by what its questions are about?

**Answer.** Philosophy is divided by what its questions are about into being, knowledge and mind, meaning and inference, value, and the standing of the sciences and mathematics.

## Governing thought

Philosophy is divided by what its questions are about into being, knowledge and mind, meaning and inference, value, and the standing of the sciences and mathematics.

## Key line

Group of kinds of philosophical question. Basis: kinds. Dimension: what the question is about. Order: structure. The pillars run from being, to knowing, to saying, to valuing, and finally to the sciences.

1. Being is the question of what exists, covering metaphysics, religion, and the nature of love, sex and sexual orientation as features of reality. `PL.K1`
2. Knowledge and mind is the question of how we know and what the mind and consciousness are, including their disorders. `PL.K2`
3. Meaning and inference is the question of how reasoning is valid and how meaning is produced and understood in language and texts. `PL.K3`
4. Value is the question of what is good, right, just and beautiful, in individual life, society and professional practice. `PL.K4`
5. The standing of the sciences and mathematics is the question of what the methods, foundations and history of inquiry, including philosophy itself, are. `PL.K5`
