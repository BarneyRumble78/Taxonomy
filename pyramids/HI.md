# HI — History

Standard v2 cited, not copied. Built up from prototype_history_language.md; its members are kept. Dates the prototype gave from memory are marked UNVERIFIED unless widely attested.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** History states what happened in the human past, when, why, what changed, and what it meant. Its claims rest on records that survive.
**Complication.** Every other pyramid cites past events: the strategist's case, the economist's crisis, the mathematician's life. v1 had no owner for the dated fact, so each field certified its own.
**Question.** What partition places every claim about the human past once, so that other pyramids can cite one owner of the dated fact?
**Answer.** Place a claim by what it asserts about the past: occurrence, time, cause, change and continuity, or meaning and memory. Every cell owes W7, source criticism of a dated record.

## 2. Governing thought
History is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five assertions about the past: occurrence, time, cause, change and continuity, and meaning and memory.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **what the sentence asserts about the human past.** Order: from the single fact to its meaning.
1. Occurrence
2. Time
3. Cause
4. Change and continuity
5. Meaning and memory

A period, region or nation is a catalogue (substrate), not a cell. "Medieval Europe" indexes claims; it does not hold them.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### HI.O.A1 Occurrence
**SCQA.** The base claim of history is that something happened. Records lie, decay and conflict. What makes "this happened" acceptable? Key line: the event, the actor, the place, the authenticity of the record.
- HI.O.A1.1 The event — the unit of record, separated from its interpretation (the fact–interpretation distinction, E. H. Carr, *What Is History?*, 1961).
- HI.O.A1.2 The actor — persons and groups; prosopography (collective biography).
- HI.O.A1.3 The place — located with EA tools; the claim of human presence is HI.
- HI.O.A1.4 Authenticity of the record — forgery and interpolation. Lorenzo Valla showed the Donation of Constantine to be a forgery (c. 1440) by philology.

### HI.O.A2 Time
**SCQA.** A fact must be placed in time to be used. Records use different calendars and clocks. When did it happen? Key line: absolute date, relative order, synchronism, periodisation.
- HI.O.A2.1 Absolute date — calendars and eras: Julian, Gregorian (introduced 1582), AH, BCE/CE.
- HI.O.A2.2 Relative order — terminus post quem and ante quem.
- HI.O.A2.3 Synchronism — aligning independent records of the same moment.
- HI.O.A2.4 Periodisation — labels such as "the Renaissance" are conventions (W12), not discoveries.
- HI.O.A2.5 Scientific dating — radiocarbon and dendrochronology dates are measurements owned by EA and PH; HI uses them.

### HI.O.A3 Cause
**SCQA.** History explains as well as records. Explanations of the past cannot be tested by re-running it. What makes a causal claim about the past acceptable? Key line: reasons of actors, conditions and structures, contingency, counterfactuals.
- HI.O.A3.1 Reasons of actors — Collingwood's re-enactment of thought (*The Idea of History*, 1946).
- HI.O.A3.2 Conditions and structures — necessary and sufficient conditions; structural causes.
- HI.O.A3.3 Contingency and chance.
- HI.O.A3.4 Counterfactual claims — they owe W7 for their premises and W9 for the inference. Unconstrained counterfactuals are a heuristic.
- HI.O.A3.5 Model-explained paths — when a growth path is explained by an economic model, the explanation is EC and HI supplies the record (cliometrics is a bridge).

### HI.O.A4 Change and continuity
**SCQA.** Many claims concern spans, not moments. Key line: durations, processes, structures over time, comparison across places.
- HI.O.A4.1 Durations — Braudel's three: longue durée, conjoncture and événement (*La Méditerranée*, 1949).
- HI.O.A4.2 Processes — industrialisation, state formation. A demographic transition is cited from MS.
- HI.O.A4.3 Structures over time — institutions, economies, environments in human time.
- HI.O.A4.4 Comparison across places — comparative history; world and global history.

### HI.O.A5 Meaning and memory
**SCQA.** The past meant things to those who lived it and to those who remember it. Key line: meaning to participants, collective memory, heritage, historiography.
- HI.O.A5.1 Meaning to participants — the history of mentalités.
- HI.O.A5.2 Collective memory — Halbwachs (1925); lieux de mémoire (Nora, 1984–92).
- HI.O.A5.3 Heritage and commemoration — monuments, museums. A heritage-law duty is LA.
- HI.O.A5.4 Historiography — what later historians made of the past. Schools of interpretation are answer-families, not cells.
- HI.O.A5.5 Oral history — a dated account owes W7 like any record. A tradition-internal account (whakapapa as authority) is IK under W13 and does not travel into A1 without W7.

## 6. Face W — Warrants

| Cell | Primary | Second | Standard | Typical defeaters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HI.O.A1 | W7 | W4 (material or forensic evidence) | two independent authenticated sources, or one with no plausible rival | forgery, single source, interpolation |
| HI.O.A2 | W7 | W4 (dating, owned by EA or PH) | date consistent across independent clocks | calendar confusion, reused material |
| HI.O.A3 | W7 | W9 + W6 | mechanism shown in the record; rival causes considered | post hoc reasoning, hindsight, teleology |
| HI.O.A4 | W7 | W6 + W2 | series built from records; method stated | survivorship of records, anachronism |
| HI.O.A5 | W7 | W8 | interpretation traced to the record | presentism, projection |

W7 does not travel: a dated record fixes the occurrence, not a physical law.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. The dated fact is history's. That an event occurred, and when, is HI; every other pyramid cites it.
2. The model-explained path is the model's (EC, PO, ST); HI supplies the record.
3. A mathematician's life is HI; the theorem is MA; MSC 01 is the bridge.
4. A physical date (radiocarbon) is a measurement in EA or PH; its use for a human event is HI.
5. A strategic lesson drawn from a case is ST; the case's facts are HI.
6. A tradition-internal account of the past is IK or RE under W13; it enters HI.O.A1 only by W7.
7. A legal rule in force at a date is LA; the history of that rule is HI.

## 9. Scope boundary

| Refused | Owner |
|---|---|
| the theorem, law or mechanism itself | MA, PH, BI and others |
| the strategic lesson | ST |
| economic explanation by model | EC |
| tradition-internal authority over the past | IK, RE (W13) |
| prehistory without human records (geologic time) | EA.O.A3 |
