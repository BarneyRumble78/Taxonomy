# CH Minto groups

## CH.O.A1
- **A substance can be classified by the question asked of it: what it basically is, what molecular framework it has, what role it plays in living systems, what practical use it serves, or which field of study frames it.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure_
  - **General chemical categories name what a substance is at the most basic level, from broad chemical entity down to the single atom.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from most general category (chemical entity) to most specific unit (atom)._
    - chemical entity
    - chemical substance
    - molecular entity
    - group
    - atom
  - **Compound classes by composition and structure describe a substance by the kind of molecular framework it has.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from simple inorganic to organic to ring-based frameworks._
    - Inorganic Chemicals
    - Organic Chemicals
    - Heterocyclic Compounds
    - Polycyclic Compounds
  - **Biochemical substances are classes of compounds defined by the role they play in living systems.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from large macromolecules to the smaller functional biomolecules._
    - Macromolecular Substances
    - Enzymes and Coenzymes
    - Hormones, Hormone Substitutes, and Hormone Antagonists
    - Carbohydrates
    - Lipids
  - **Applied chemicals are substances classified by the practical use made of them in medicine and dentistry.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from materials to finished pharmaceutical products._
    - Biomedical and Dental Materials
    - Pharmaceutical Preparations
    - Chemicals and Drugs
  - **The discipline of chemistry and its residual and forensic subfields frame the study of substances as a field of knowledge.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the whole field to its specific and residual subfields._
    - Chemistry
    - Other chemical sciences
    - Forensic chemistry
    - Other chemical sciences not elsewhere classified

## CH.O.A2
- **Chemistry fields are distinguished by the kind of question they ask of a substance, either about its makeup or about its behaviour.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from questions about what a substance is to questions about how it behaves._
  - **Composition and construction fields ask what a substance is made of, how its atoms are arranged and bonded, and how it is assembled.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from elemental composition, to atomic arrangement, to formation, to residual inorganic and organised-assembly inquiries._
    - **Fields that ask which elements make up a substance and how those elements, by chemical family, behave.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the broad inorganic field to specific element families._
      - Inorganic chemistry
      - Main group metal chemistry
      - Non-metal chemistry
      - Transition metal chemistry
    - **Fields that ask how the atoms of a substance are arranged and bonded into ordered architectures such as lattices, clusters and frameworks.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from atomic arrangement to larger assembled frameworks._
      - Crystallography
      - Metal cluster chemistry
      - Metal organic frameworks
      - Solid state chemistry
    - **Fields that ask how a substance is assembled or formed, through polymerisation, nanoscale synthesis or metal-carbon bonding.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from molecular bond formation to scaled materials._
      - Macromolecular and materials chemistry
      - Organometallic chemistry
      - Macromolecular materials
      - Nanochemistry
      - Polymerisation mechanisms
    - **Kinds of inorganic chemistry questions asked of a substance, from the specific f-block elements to the remaining general inorganic cases.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered from the specific f-block branch to the residual general category._
      - F-block chemistry
      - Inorganic chemistry not elsewhere classified
    - **Fields that ask what a material or molecular assembly is made of and how its structure and internal motion are organised.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure_
      - Structure and dynamics of materials
      - Supramolecular chemistry
      - Macromolecular and materials chemistry not elsewhere classified
  - **Behaviour and modelling fields ask how a substance responds to light and radiation, and how its properties are designed and computed in theory.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from directly measured optical properties, to theoretical design of materials, to fully computational and quantum description._
    - **Fields that ask how a substance interacts with light and what measurable properties it displays.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from optical to general physical properties._
      - Optical properties of materials
      - Physical properties of materials
      - Bioinorganic chemistry
      - Inorganic green chemistry
      - Inorganic materials (incl. nanomaterials)
    - **Fields that ask how a material can be conceived and designed in theory to achieve desired properties, and how it behaves in aggregate.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure_
      - Theory and design of materials
      - Theoretical and computational chemistry
      - Statistical mechanics in chemistry
    - **Fields that ask how a substance behaves under computation, quantum theory and its interaction with radiation.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure_
      - Computational chemistry
      - Radiation and matter
      - Theoretical quantum chemistry
      - Theoretical and computational chemistry not elsewhere classified

## CH.O.A3
- **The question asked of a substance is whether it changes, how it interacts and moves, or how it is observed and measured.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure_
  - **Questions about change ask how a substance reacts, exchanges energy and charge, or converts from one state or form to another.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from chemical reaction, to energy and charge exchange, to change of state, to conversion of form._
    - **Questions about how a substance reacts ask by what pathway, how fast, and under what catalytic or light-driven influence a chemical change occurs.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds of reaction questions arranged by structure: mechanism and catalysis, rate and dynamics, light-driven change, and the broad biochemical setting._
      - Catalysis and mechanisms of reactions
      - Reaction kinetics and dynamics
      - Photochemistry
      - Biochemical Phenomena
    - **Questions about energy and charge ask how a substance stores, exchanges and transports energy, charge or matter in solution and away from equilibrium.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds of energy and transport questions arranged by structure: energetics, electrical behaviour, solution medium, and transport processes._
      - Chemical thermodynamics and energetics
      - Electrochemistry
      - Solution chemistry
      - Transport properties and non-equilibrium processes
    - **Solid-forming processes are those in which a solid phase emerges or separates out of a solution or melt.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure: ordered solid growth first, then general solid separation._
      - Crystallization
      - Chemical Precipitation
    - **Material behavior concepts describe how a substance's properties vary with direction or how it deteriorates through reaction with its environment.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure: an intrinsic property first, then a degradation process._
      - Corrosion
      - Anisotropy
    - **Changes of state or form describe a substance converting into a different physical or chemical condition.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Listed in structural order of change type._
      - Desiccation
      - Phase Transition
      - Evolution, Chemical
      - Dimerization
  - **Questions about interaction and transport ask how a substance meets surfaces and other molecules, and how it is separated, moved or released across boundaries.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from interface uptake, to separation and transport, to movement and release, to intermolecular attraction._
    - **Questions about interfaces and uptake ask how a substance interacts with surfaces, other phases and ions, taking material in or holding it at a boundary.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds of interface questions arranged by structure: surface science, bulk uptake, surface binding, and ionization of air._
      - Colloid and surface chemistry
      - Absorption
      - Adsorption
      - Air Ionization
    - **Barrier-passage processes separate a substance from a mixture by letting only certain components cross a membrane or filter.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure; no ranking implied._
      - Dialysis
      - Filtration
    - **Gradient-driven processes ask how a substance moves or exchanges in response to concentration or charge differences.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure; no ranking implied._
      - Ion Exchange
      - Osmosis
    - **Movement and release phenomena describe how matter or energy gets through or out of a substance.**
      _basis: parts, order: structure. Listed in structural order of movement type._
      - Diffusion
      - Permeability
      - Drug Liberation
      - Energy Transfer
    - **Intermolecular interactions and structure concern how molecules attract one another, are arranged, and are probed.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Listed in structural order from interaction to structure to probe._
      - Hydrogen Bonding
      - Hydrophobic and Hydrophilic Interactions
      - Molecular Structure
      - Neutron Diffraction
  - **Questions about observation and measurement ask how a substance is imaged, quantified, characterised in bulk, and probed for energy and structure.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from defining and imaging the field, to measured quantities, to bulk properties, to energy and structure probes._
    - **Questions about observation and definition ask how a substance is imaged at the molecular scale and how the field itself and its uncategorised topics are delimited.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds of framing questions arranged by structure: the field as a whole, molecular imaging, residual topics, and the overarching phenomena category._
      - Physical chemistry
      - Molecular imaging (incl. electron microscopy and neutron diffraction)
      - Physical chemistry not elsewhere classified
      - Chemical Phenomena
    - **Measured properties are quantities that can be determined on a substance or solution.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Listed in structural order of property type._
      - Hydrogen-Ion Concentration
      - Molecular Weight
      - Osmolar Concentration
      - Particle Size
    - **Solubility, specific gravity, and viscosity are kinds of physical property, each defined by a different question asked of a substance.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Parallel kinds of physical property, listed in MeSH order with no ranking among them._
      - Solubility
      - Specific Gravity
      - Viscosity
    - **Energy and structure probes ask how a substance stores energy, ignites, presents its surface, or reveals its atomic arrangement.**
      _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from the most direct structural measurement (X-ray diffraction) to surface behavior to energetic phenomena._
      - X-Ray Diffraction
      - Surface Properties
      - Proton-Motive Force
      - Spontaneous Combustion

## CH.O.A4
- **The question asked of a substance is what it does, how it is made to react, or how it changes, and each question yields its own kind of concept.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure_
  - **A role names the function a substance plays, ranging from the general notion of role to its biological and chemical specializations.**
    _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from most general (role) to more specific (biological role, chemical role)._
    - role
    - biological role
    - chemical role
  - **Reaction-driving processes are ways a substance is made to react by an added agent such as a catalyst, water, light, or heat.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by the type of agent that drives the reaction._
    - Catalysis
    - Hydrolysis
    - Photochemical Processes
    - Pyrolysis
  - **Transformation phenomena are changes in which a substance's own bonds or molecular structure are rearranged, as in organic chemistry, redox, and polymerization.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by the kind of structural change to the substance._
    - Organic Chemistry Phenomena
    - Oxidation-Reduction
    - Polymerization

## CH.O.A5
- **The question asked of a substance sorts approaches by whether they characterise what a substance is, where it occurs and what it does, or explain what it is made of, how it is built and reacts, and what it is used for.**
  _basis: kinds, order: structure_
  - **Analytical and characterising approaches ask what a substance is, how much and where it occurs, what it does biologically, and what shape a biomolecule has.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered structurally from physical measurement to separation and sensing, biological activity, and molecular modelling._
    - **Spectrometric, electrochemical and general instrumental methods answer what a substance is and how much of it is present by measuring its physical response.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the umbrella field to specific instrumental techniques._
      - Analytical chemistry
      - Analytical spectrometry
      - Electroanalytical chemistry
      - Instrumental methods (excl. immunological and bioassay methods)
      - Analytical chemistry not elsewhere classified
    - **Separation, flow and sensing approaches answer what a substance is mixed with and where it is, by isolating components or detecting them continuously.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from separating components to moving samples to detecting them._
      - Separation science
      - Flow analysis
      - Sensor technology (incl. chemical aspects)
      - Quality assurance, chemometrics, traceability and metrological chemistry
    - **Bioassays, metabolomics and biologically active molecules ask what a substance does in a living system and which biological effects it produces.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from testing activity to profiling metabolites to the active molecules themselves._
      - Bioassays
      - Metabolomic chemistry
      - Biologically active molecules
      - Molecular medicine
    - **Modelling, characterisation, informatics and the study of proteins, peptides and glycoconjugates ask what shape a biomolecule has and how it can be designed or predicted.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered by structure, from the general field and modelling to the specific biomolecule classes._
      - Medicinal and biomolecular chemistry
      - Biomolecular modelling and design
      - Cheminformatics and quantitative structure-activity relationships
      - Proteins and peptides
      - Medicinal and biomolecular chemistry not elsewhere classified
  - **Compositional, synthetic and identity-focused approaches ask what a substance is made of, how it is built and reacts, and what it is used for.**
    _basis: kinds, order: structure. Ordered structurally from macromolecule composition to reaction and synthesis, then to identity and application._
    - **Each concept is a kind of question asked of a biological substance, from the general characterisation of macromolecules to the specific study of sugar-bearing conjugates.**
      _basis: kinds, order: degree. Ranked from the broadest question (characterising biological macromolecules in general) to the narrower one (glycoconjugates, a specific class of macromolecule)._
      - Characterisation of biological macromolecules
      - Glycoconjugates
    - **Process-focused subfields ask how organic substances react and are made, studying mechanisms, radical intermediates, deliberate synthesis and environmentally benign routes.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds of process-focused inquiry; ordered by structure, with no ranking._
      - Free radical chemistry
      - Organic chemical synthesis
      - Organic green chemistry
      - Physical organic chemistry
    - **Identity-focused and framing concepts ask what an organic substance is and what it is used for, covering the whole field, natural bioactive compounds, uncategorised topics and applications.**
      _basis: kinds, order: structure. Kinds of identity and use-focused inquiry; ordered by structure, with no ranking._
      - Organic chemistry
      - Natural products and bioactive compounds
      - Organic chemistry not elsewhere classified
      - application
