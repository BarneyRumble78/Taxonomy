## 1. SCQA

Situation: strategy is the theory of purposeful action against an adaptive opponent. It covers war, coercion, bargaining, grand strategy, irregular war, the information contest, competitive business strategy and judgement under uncertainty. Complication: its literature is filed by substrate (nuclear, cyber, business), by author or by period, so one mechanism appears under many names. Game theory is claimed by economics and mathematics, deterrence by politics, and competitive strategy by business. Question: what single partition places every strategic claim, method and heuristic exactly once, with clean borders to MA, EC, BU, PO, MS, LA, PL and HI? Answer: the four faces below, with objects divided by the feature of the strategic situation that a claim explains.

Source corpus: *The Encyclopedia of Strategy* (31 books, 10 theatres, 410 codex cards, a 10-axis Structural Diagnostic Panel, Mechanism Concordance M01–M27). External authority: LCC classes U and V.

## 2. Governing thought

Strategy is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges. Its objects are five features of the strategic situation: actors, the game, coercion, conduct, and information.

## 3. Key line

- Logic: inductive.
- Plural noun: features (of the strategic situation).
- Dimension: the feature of the strategic situation that the claim explains.
- Order: structural, from who contends to what each side knows.

1. **Actors** (ST.O.A1): who contends, how they align, and whether each can bind itself.
2. **The game** (ST.O.A2): the structure of payoffs and moves, and the moves that reshape it.
3. **Coercion** (ST.O.A3): how a threat of harm, held in reserve, changes another's choice, and how it escalates.
4. **Conduct** (ST.O.A4): how means are applied in time and space to reach a purpose.
5. **Information** (ST.O.A5): what each side knows, believes and can predict, and how that knowledge is contested.

Why this dimension. The Encyclopedia's own protocol forbids retrieval by "a scenario's own vocabulary" and requires retrieval "by structure". Its ten Panel axes are ten questions about features of the situation. They group into these five features without remainder (section 4, Panel table).

Rejected dimensions. The ten theatres mix substrate, method and record. The levels of strategy are a scale on which every mechanism recurs [luttwak-c17]. The medium or service is a substrate [wylie-c14]. Clausewitz's trinity [clausewitz-c03] does not place bargaining or forecasting. Purpose–ways–means reduces to conduct (A4.1).

CONTESTED: coercion could be folded into the game as A2.5, giving four features. It is kept separate for two reasons. Its operative move, a threat held in reserve, has its own mechanisms (M03, M06–M08, M18, M19). It also owes a different warrant: deterrence succeeds through non-events, which the game cell's warrants cannot see.

### ST.O.A1 Actors

**SCQA.** Every game has players. Strategic texts assume unitary actors, then explain failures by factions, agents and allies. What does strategy claim about the players themselves? Four things: what counts as a player, the system of players, their alignment, and their internal agency.

**Key line** (inductive; plural noun: aspects of the actor; order: from one actor to many, then inside one).
1. ST.O.A1.1 The unit: who counts as a player — state, regime, movement, population, firm, leader.
2. ST.O.A1.2 The system: the number and distribution of players' power, as the arena a player must use.
3. ST.O.A1.3 Alignment: how players combine, divide gains and defect.
4. ST.O.A1.4 Agency: whether a player acts directly or through agents, and whether it can bind itself.

### ST.O.A2 The game

**SCQA.** Strategic interaction can be written as players, moves and payoffs. The same surface situation can be zero-sum or mixed-motive, one-shot or repeated, and the classification decides the answer. What does strategy claim about the game? Four things: its class, how coordination happens, how time shapes it, and which moves change it.

**Key line** (inductive; plural noun: aspects of the game; order: from classifying the game to changing it).
1. ST.O.A2.1 Class: whether interests are opposed or admit joint gain, and what lies beneath stated positions.
2. ST.O.A2.2 Coordination: how parties converge without, or despite, communication.
3. ST.O.A2.3 Time: how sequence and repetition change what is rational.
4. ST.O.A2.4 Strategic moves: commitments, promises, randomization and redesign that change the game itself.

### ST.O.A3 Coercion

**SCQA.** Schelling separates brute force, which takes, from coercion, which makes the other yield by threat [arms-c01]. Coercion works through events that do not happen, so success is hard to observe and easy to misread. What does strategy claim about coercion? Four things: the modes of threat, the manipulation of shared risk, the structure of escalation, and the stability of mutual threat.

**Key line** (inductive; plural noun: aspects of coercion; order: from the single threat to the stability of the whole relation).
1. ST.O.A3.1 Threat modes: deterrence prevents a change; compellence forces one; both depend on credibility and on connection to the demand.
2. ST.O.A3.2 Shared risk: brinkmanship coerces by raising a risk neither side fully controls.
3. ST.O.A3.3 Escalation: conflict rises through recognized thresholds, and the structure of the steps governs risk.
4. ST.O.A3.4 Stability: whether fear of the other's first move creates an incentive to move first.

### ST.O.A5 Information

**SCQA.** Strategy is played on beliefs about capability, resolve, intention and the future. Private information, misperception, overconfidence and fat tails each defeat a plan that assumes the facts are known. What does strategy claim about information? Five things: how private information is revealed, how adversaries misperceive, how well judgement predicts, how exposure survives the tail, and how information is attacked.

**Key line** (inductive; plural noun: aspects of information; order: from equilibrium transmission to deliberate attack).
1. ST.O.A5.1 Signals: what a rational receiver infers from a costly or cheap message.
2. ST.O.A5.2 Perception: how decision-makers misread an adversary, in patterned ways.
3. ST.O.A5.3 Judgement: how accurately a strategist can estimate and forecast.
4. ST.O.A5.4 Exposure: how to hold a position that survives thin- or fat-tailed uncertainty.
5. ST.O.A5.5 The information contest: operations that create, steal, corrupt or deny the other's information.

Inner rule: a claim about inference inside an agreed game is A5.1. A claim about an operation outside that inference (deception, forgery, espionage) is A5.5.

## 5. Face M — Methods

Key line: inductive; plural noun: roles; dimension: what the practice does to a strategic claim; order: the Encyclopedia's protocol order, from diagnosis to audit. The prototype's role IDs ST.M.B1–B5 are kept.

1. Diagnose: writes the situation down — its features, its balance, its centre of gravity.
2. Model: writes the situation as a game and solves it.
3. Simulate and play: runs the contest artificially, with machines or people as the engine.
4. Compare cases: reads a claim across past contests and past strategists.
5. Estimate and score: states a probability, tests it against outcomes, and audits the plan.

Notes.
- No M4 role. A strategist rarely runs a controlled intervention on the real contest, because the opponent adapts to the probe. The probe (reconnaissance in force, a test of resolve, a market trial) is a secondary of ST.M.B3.
- Red-teaming has two homes by function. Playing the adversary in a game is ST.M.B3. Critiquing a finished plan from the adversary's view is ST.M.B5.
- Game theory ownership. Von Neumann's minimax theorem (finite two-person zero-sum games, mixed strategies) and Nash's existence theorem (finite games, mixed strategies) are MA.B.C4 (91A). Their use as an account of allocation is EC.O.A2. Their use against an adaptive opponent is ST.M.B2.
- Operations research (MSC 90) is MA.B.C4, used by ST.M.B2 and ST.M.B3.

## 8. Assignment rules

1. **Unit is the claim.** A codex card can hold two claims; each gets its own home. Five cards sit in two Concordance rows and are split by claim (section 11).
2. **Adaptive-other rule.** A claim belongs here only if its truth depends on an opponent, rival or counterpart who reacts. A choice against a non-reacting environment is EC.O.A1 (decision) or MA.B.C4 (operations research). CONTESTED exception: ST.O.A5.3 and ST.O.A5.4 are kept, because the corpus treats markets and nature as environments whose response to exposure is adversarial in effect. Alternative: move both to EC.O.A1.
3. **Threat rule.** If the operative move is a conditional threat to impose cost, the claim is ST.O.A3, whatever the instrument. A move that restructures the game without a threat is ST.O.A2.
4. **Medium rule.** Land, sea, air, space, cyber and the information environment are substrates. Coercion through a medium is A3; force applied in it is A4; information created, stolen or corrupted in it is A5.5.
5. **Level rule.** Tactical, operational, strategic and grand-strategic are a scale, not cells [luttwak-c17]. A claim about the levels themselves is ST.O.A4.1.
6. **Case rule.** A dated event (Cannae, Barbarossa, Cuba 1962) is a case. It is evidence handled by ST.M.B4; its historical truth uses HI.O.A1.
7. **Game rule (from MA and EC).** The theorem is MA, the allocation is EC, the choice of game is ST. Test: does the claim change if the opponent can change the game? If yes, ST.
8. **Politics rule.** Why polities go to war is PO.O.A5.1 (Fearon's bargaining failure). How a party coerces or fights is ST.
9. **Business rule.** The firm's conversion, cost, capital and governance are BU.O.A3. Positioning against rivals who react is ST.
10. **Mind rule.** A cognitive bias as a process is MS. Its exploitation or correction against a counterpart is ST.O.A5.2.
11. **Law and philosophy rule.** Whether a use of force is lawful is LA. Whether it is just is PL. Whether it works is ST.
12. **Artefact rule.** How a weapon or exploit works is EN or CS. What it does to the contest is ST.
13. **No Other.** A strategic claim that will not reduce to one feature is a test of the dimension, not a residual cell.

Proposed edits to other pyramids (recorded, not imposed): BU.O.A3 drops "strategy is the choice of which conversion" in favour of "uses ST.O.A1.2, ST.O.A4.2". PO.O.A5.1 keeps war onset and cites ST.O.A3.1 for deterrence and ST.O.A1.3 for alliance credibility (adopted in p2_PO.md). EC.O.A2 adds "used by ST.O.A2".
