# ED — Education

New field, added at the owner's direction. Standard v2 cited, not copied. Why it is here: the physics audit had nowhere to put physics education research, and MSC 97 treats mathematics as the object of a learning claim. Nothing owned the learning claim itself.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Education research states what people learn, under which arrangements, how it is measured, and how schools and systems organise it.
**Complication.** v1 scattered these claims. Physics education went to MS, MSC 97 sat in MA, and JEL A2 and ANZSRC 39 had no home.
**Question.** By what dimension can every claim about learning and teaching be placed once, with the subject matter left to its owner?
**Answer.** By the element of the educational arrangement the claim is about: aims, learners, teaching, assessment, or institutions.

## 2. Governing thought
Education is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five elements of an educational arrangement: aims and curricula, learners, teaching, assessment, and institutions and systems.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the element of an educational arrangement.** Order: from purpose to system.
1. Aims and curricula
2. Learners
3. Teaching
4. Assessment
5. Institutions and systems

## 4. Face O — Objects

### ED.O.A1 Aims and curricula
**SCQA.** Every arrangement serves a stated aim. What should be learned, in what order? Key line: aims, curriculum content, sequence and progression.
- ED.O.A1.1 Aims — the stated purposes of education. The normative argument for them is PL; the statement of an adopted aim is ED (W12).
- ED.O.A1.2 Curriculum content — what is taught. The truth of the content stays with its owner (a theorem is MA).
- ED.O.A1.3 Sequence and progression — learning progressions and prerequisites.
- ED.O.A1.4 National curricula — e.g. Te Whāriki (NZ early childhood) and Te Marautanga o Aotearoa (Māori-medium) as stated frameworks (W12).

### ED.O.A2 Learners
**SCQA.** Learning happens in a learner with prior knowledge. What does the learner bring, and how do they change? Key line: prior knowledge and misconceptions, development, motivation, diversity and need.
- ED.O.A2.1 Prior knowledge and misconceptions — e.g. the impetus misconception in force and motion (physics education research). The cognitive mechanism is MS.
- ED.O.A2.2 Development — age-related readiness, cited from MS.
- ED.O.A2.3 Motivation and engagement.
- ED.O.A2.4 Diversity and learning need — inclusion, special education, gifted learners.

### ED.O.A3 Teaching
**SCQA.** Teaching arrangements cause learning, or fail to. Which arrangements work, for whom? Key line: instruction, feedback, practice and retrieval, technology, culturally sustaining pedagogy.
- ED.O.A3.1 Instruction — explicit instruction versus discovery; worked examples help novices (an expertise-reversal effect is a stated defeater for experts).
- ED.O.A3.2 Feedback — effects vary widely by type; some feedback lowers performance (UNVERIFIED as a meta-analytic figure).
- ED.O.A3.3 Practice and retrieval — spacing and retrieval practice improve long-term retention. The memory mechanism is MS.
- ED.O.A3.4 Technology in teaching — effects depend on use, not presence.
- ED.O.A3.5 Culturally sustaining pedagogy — Kaupapa Māori education (kura kaupapa Māori) as an arrangement. The tradition-internal content is IK.

### ED.O.A4 Assessment
**SCQA.** Learning is inferred from performance on instruments. What does a score mean? Key line: validity, reliability, formative assessment, high-stakes testing.
- ED.O.A4.1 Validity — an argument that score interpretations are supported (W9 with W4).
- ED.O.A4.2 Reliability — score consistency; the statistics are MA.O.A5 (psychometrics as bridge).
- ED.O.A4.3 Formative assessment — assessment used to change teaching.
- ED.O.A4.4 High-stakes testing and accountability — Campbell's law: the more a quantitative indicator drives decisions, the more it is subject to corruption pressures (stated as a tendency).

### ED.O.A5 Institutions and systems
**SCQA.** Teaching happens inside schools and systems with rules. What do institutions do to learning? Key line: schools, school systems, higher education, equity and access.
- ED.O.A5.1 Schools — school effects, leadership, class size.
- ED.O.A5.2 School systems — international comparisons (PISA, TIMSS) are W4 within their sampling designs.
- ED.O.A5.3 Higher education — universities, credentials.
- ED.O.A5.4 Equity and access — gaps by group. The returns to schooling are EC; the law on schooling is LA.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place by the element of the arrangement.
2. The truth of what is taught stays with its owner. ED owns the learning claim.
3. The mind's mechanism is MS. Its educational use is ED.
4. The economic return is EC. The legal duty is LA. The political decision is PO.
5. MSC 97 is a bridge: the mathematics is MA; learning it is ED.
6. The learning line (Phase 2.1). A claim that learners changed under a stated arrangement (teaching, curriculum, assessment, institution) is ED. A claim about how learning or memory works in any mind, with no arrangement in it, is MS.O.A1.5. Test: delete the arrangement from the sentence. If the claim survives, it is MS; if it does not, it is ED. MS.O.A1.5.5 is retired and points here.
