# EV — classification framework

This is a classification of the field, not a pyramid for one problem (Minto, pp. 110–111 and 143, and Chapter 9). The introduction is Situation, Complication, Question, Answer (Chapter 4). The key line is inductive (Chapter 5). Order is time, structure, or degree (Chapter 6). A group has two to five members (Chapter 6, p. 78, and Chapter 10, p. 174). A summary defines what the group contains and why.

## What counts as environmental

An environmental topic names a human load on air, water, soil, or living systems, or the condition that results. It also names the harm, the action taken, or the limit that condition should not cross. A chemical, organism, earth science, disease, price, firm, law, place, machine, or data model is routed to its own field.

## Introduction

**Situation.** Environmental science studies coupled human and natural systems, from the load people apply to the response that follows.

**Complication.** Process science in EA and BI does not own the claim that a human pressure causes a harm.

**Question.** By what dimension can each coupled claim be placed once, with the process science left to EA, BI and CH?

**Answer.** Environmental science places each coupled human–environment claim on one link of the causal chain: pressure, state, impact, response, or limit.

## Governing thought

Environmental science places each coupled human–environment claim on one link of the causal chain: pressure, state, impact, response, or limit.

## Drivers

A driver is the activity that creates a load. The load is the pressure, so drivers are grouped under pressures and are not a sixth key-line cell.

## Key line

Group of links. Basis: cause_effect. Dimension: the link in the human–environment causal chain. Order: time.

1. Pressures are the loads that human activity places on the environment. `EV.P.1`
2. States are the measured conditions of air, water, soil, and living systems. `EV.P.2`
3. Impacts are the harms a changed state causes, and the way those harms are attributed. `EV.P.3`
4. Responses are the actions taken to change a pressure or a state. `EV.P.4`
5. Limits are the thresholds a measured state is not meant to cross. `EV.P.5`
