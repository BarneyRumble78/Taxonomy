# DE — Design and the built environment

New field, added at the owner's direction. Standard v2 cited, not copied. Why it is here: ANZSRC 33 (Built environment and design) had no core home. Architecture is neither engineering's specification cell nor art's verdict.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Design fields (architecture, urban planning, landscape, industrial and graphic design) make claims about arrangements proposed for human use.
**Complication.** EN owns whether an artefact meets a specification. AR owns the verdict on a work. Nothing owned "this arrangement serves this use in this place".
**Question.** By what dimension can each design claim be placed once?
**Answer.** By the aspect of a designed arrangement the claim is about: its use, its form, its performance in use, its place, or its life over time.

## 2. Governing thought
Design is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five aspects of a designed arrangement: use and need, form, performance in use, place and settlement, and life of the built thing.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the aspect of a designed arrangement for human use.** Order: from the need to the life of the result.
1. Use and need
2. Form
3. Performance in use
4. Place and settlement
5. Life of the built thing

## 4. Face O — Objects

### DE.O.A1 Use and need
**SCQA.** Design starts from people's use. What need does the arrangement serve? Key line: users and activities, the brief, accessibility.
- DE.O.A1.1 Users and activities — who will use it and how. Human factors cite MS.
- DE.O.A1.2 The brief — the stated programme of needs (W12 once agreed).
- DE.O.A1.3 Accessibility and universal design — designing for the widest range of users.

### DE.O.A2 Form
**SCQA.** Needs are answered by spatial and visual arrangements. What form, and why? Key line: spatial organisation, typology, visual and material language.
- DE.O.A2.1 Spatial organisation — plan, section, circulation.
- DE.O.A2.2 Typology — building and product types.
- DE.O.A2.3 Visual and material language — composition, colour, materials. An aesthetic verdict with a stated criterion is AR.O.A5.

### DE.O.A3 Performance in use
**SCQA.** A design is judged by how it works for its users. Key line: comfort and environment, wayfinding and usability, safety in use, post-occupancy evaluation.
- DE.O.A3.1 Comfort and indoor environment — thermal, acoustic, daylight. The physics is PH; structural adequacy is EN.
- DE.O.A3.2 Wayfinding and usability.
- DE.O.A3.3 Safety in use — building codes are W12; their legal force is LA.
- DE.O.A3.4 Post-occupancy evaluation — measured performance after use begins (W4).

### DE.O.A4 Place and settlement
**SCQA.** Designs sit in places and form settlements. Key line: site, urban form, landscape, planning.
- DE.O.A4.1 Site — the physical site claim uses EA.
- DE.O.A4.2 Urban form — density, street networks, mixed use.
- DE.O.A4.3 Landscape architecture.
- DE.O.A4.4 Planning — zoning and land use. The legal instrument is LA; the political decision is PO.

### DE.O.A5 Life of the built thing
**SCQA.** Built things age, are kept, adapted and removed. Key line: maintenance and adaptation, sustainability, heritage conservation.
- DE.O.A5.1 Maintenance and adaptive reuse.
- DE.O.A5.2 Sustainability — embodied and operational carbon. The environmental impact claim is EV.
- DE.O.A5.3 Conservation of built heritage — the heritage meaning is HI.O.A5.3.

## 8. Assignment rules
1. Place by the aspect of the arrangement for use.
2. Structural and system adequacy is EN; use, form and place are DE.
3. A verdict on beauty with a stated criterion is AR; design rationale is DE.
4. Planning law is LA; the planning decision is PO; land price is EC.
5. Environmental impact is EV.
