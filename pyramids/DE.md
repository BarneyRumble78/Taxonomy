# DE — Design and the built environment

New field, added at the owner's direction. Standard v2 cited, not copied. Why it is here: ANZSRC 33 (Built environment and design) had no core home. Architecture is neither engineering's specification cell nor art's verdict.

## 1. SCQA
**Situation.** Design fields (architecture, urban planning, landscape, industrial and graphic design) make claims about arrangements proposed for human use.
**Complication.** EN owns whether an artefact meets a specification. AR owns the verdict on a work. Nothing owned "this arrangement serves this use in this place".
**Question.** By what dimension can each design claim be placed once?
**Answer.** By the aspect of a designed arrangement the claim is about: its use, its form, its performance in use, its place, or its life over time.

## 2. Governing thought
Design is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges.
Its objects divide into five aspects of a designed arrangement: use and need, form, performance in use, place and settlement, and life of the built thing.

## 3. Key line
Dimension: **the aspect of a designed arrangement for human use.** Order: from the need to the life of the result.
1. Use and need
2. Form
3. Performance in use
4. Place and settlement
5. Life of the built thing

## 4. Face O — Objects

Reading rule for this face. A design claim is placed by what it says about an arrangement, not by the profession that makes it. A claim about a measured quantity (a temperature, a flow, a load) belongs to the science that owns the law. The design claim is that an arrangement meets a stated criterion for a stated user, site and period. Mathematics owns every theorem used here. Every numerical value quoted below is a convention (W12) or a model output unless it is marked as measured.

### DE.O.A1 Use and need

**SCQA.** *Situation.* Every design starts from people who will use an arrangement for some activity. *Complication.* Needs are stated by clients, inferred by designers and measured by researchers, and the three sources often disagree. *Question.* Which claims about use and need does design own, and which belong to the sciences of people? *Answer.* Design owns the claim that a stated group, activity, programme or barrier is the one an arrangement must serve. The behavioural facts belong to mind and society (uses MS). The legal duty to provide access belongs to law (uses LA).

**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: need claims; dimension: the source of the need; order: from the people, to the agreed statement, to the barriers. Members (3): Users and activities · The brief · Accessibility and universal design.

- **DE.O.A1.1 Users and activities.** **A claim about who will use an arrangement, and how, holds only for the population sampled and the activities observed.** Human factors cite MS (uses MS).
  - DE.O.A1.1.1 User research samples. A design claim about users is supported only for users the sample represents. The Nielsen–Landauer model gives the share of problems found by n evaluators as 1 − (1 − p)ⁿ. It assumes every problem has the same independent detection probability p. The popular reading that five users find about 85 percent of problems is CONTESTED, because p varies by problem and by user.
  - DE.O.A1.1.2 Activities and behaviour settings. A behaviour setting is a recurring pattern of behaviour tied to a place and a time (uses MS.O.A4.4). The floor area an activity needs is measured by observing it in comparable settings (W4). Area allowances per person are conventions (W12), not measurements.
  - DE.O.A1.1.3 Variation among users. Individuals are rarely near the mean on several body or ability dimensions at once. A design sized to the mean on each dimension therefore fits fewer people than its designers expect. The claim is testable on any multi-dimensional body-size data set. A persona is a heuristic summary, not a sample.
- **DE.O.A1.2 The brief.** **A brief is a hypothesis about need until the client agrees it, and a stipulation afterwards.** The stated programme of needs (W12 once agreed).
  - DE.O.A1.2.1 Programme and adjacency. A functional programme lists spaces, their areas and the adjacencies they require. A required adjacency is met if the plan graph contains the required connection. The net-to-gross ratio is net room area divided by gross floor area. It is a definition, and its typical value differs by building type.
  - DE.O.A1.2.2 The agreed brief as stipulation. After agreement the brief is the standard against which a design is reviewed (DE.M.5). Review against a brief is not review against a specification, which is EN. The claim that the cost of change rises as design proceeds is a heuristic, and its shape for any one project type is UNVERIFIED.
  - DE.O.A1.2.3 Problem framing and process models. A wicked problem (Rittel and Webber) is one with no definitive formulation and no stopping rule. It is a characterisation, not a theorem. The double diamond (diverge, then converge, twice) is a heuristic process model. Whether following it improves outcomes is OPEN. Staged work plans are conventions (W12).
- **DE.O.A1.3 Accessibility and universal design.** **An accessibility claim is tested against stated users and a stated standard, never against an assumed typical user.** Designing for the widest range of users.
  - DE.O.A1.3.1 Universal design principles. Seven principles are stated. They are equitable use, flexibility in use, simple and intuitive use, perceptible information, tolerance for error, low physical effort, and size and space for approach. They are design criteria (W12, W9), not empirical laws. A given design meets a principle or it does not, and expert review or trial with users can show which.
  - DE.O.A1.3.2 Dimensional envelopes and percentile design. Clear widths, turning spaces, reach ranges and ramp gradients are set by standards (W12), which differ between jurisdictions. Their legal force is LA (uses LA.O.A1.1). Percentile design sizes a feature to fit a stated share of users. It assumes body-size data exist for the user population and that the critical dimension is identified. Percentiles on several dimensions do not add, so a 90 percent fit on each does not give a 90 percent fit overall.
  - DE.O.A1.3.3 Sensory and cognitive access. Luminance contrast between adjacent surfaces aids people with low vision, and the claim can be tested by detection distance. Evidence for specific layout rules that reduce cognitive load is thinner than for physical access. The strength of that evidence for any single rule is UNVERIFIED. Perception itself is MS (uses MS.O.A1.1).
  - DE.O.A1.3.4 Models of disability. The social model locates disablement in barriers in the environment. The medical model locates it in impairment. Accessibility practice usually adopts the first. The choice is a framing, and the claim that one model fits better is CONTESTED. Its home is UNVERIFIED (alternatives MS, LA). The duty to remove barriers is LA.

Named objects: behaviour setting, persona (heuristic), functional programme, net-to-gross ratio, brief, wicked problem, double diamond (heuristic), universal design principles, percentile design. Nielsen–Landauer problem-discovery model (hypotheses: equal and independent detection probability; evaluators are drawn from the user population; CONTESTED). Percentile design (hypotheses: body-size data exist for the population; the critical dimension is identified; dimensions are not summed).

### DE.O.A2 Form

**SCQA.** *Situation.* Needs are answered by shaped space, objects and images. *Complication.* A form claim can describe a shape, give a rationale for it, or give a verdict on its beauty. These have different warrants. *Question.* Which claims about form does design own? *Answer.* Design owns the description and the rationale of spatial organisation, type, and visual and material language. The verdict on beauty with a stated criterion is AR.O.A5.1 (uses AR.O.A5.1).

**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: form claims; dimension: the level at which the form is described; order: from the whole arrangement to the surface. Members (3): Spatial organisation · Typology · Visual and material language.

- **DE.O.A2.1 Spatial organisation.** **A spatial organisation is a describable structure of rooms, connections and sections that can be represented as a graph and measured.** Plan, section, circulation.
  - DE.O.A2.1.1 Adjacency and plan graphs. A plan is represented as a graph: rooms are nodes and doors are edges. Graph measures such as degree and connectivity are mathematical (uses MA.O.A2). A required adjacency is met if the corresponding edge exists. The test is by inspection of the plan.
  - DE.O.A2.1.2 Depth and circulation. In a justified graph, depth is the number of steps from a chosen root space. Relative asymmetry is RA = 2(MD − 1)/(k − 2), where MD is mean depth from the root and k is the number of spaces. It holds for a connected graph with k above 2. Whether depth predicts use or social control is an empirical claim and is CONTESTED.
  - DE.O.A2.1.3 Section and plan depth. The section sets how far daylight and cross-ventilation reach. A rule of thumb puts useful daylight at about two to two and a half times the window head height. It is a heuristic, and it varies with glazing, sky and surface reflectance. Its stated range is UNVERIFIED. The measure is the daylight factor (DE.O.A3.1.4).
  - DE.O.A2.1.4 Visibility and enclosure. An isovist is the set of points visible from a viewpoint, given stated obstructions. It is computed from the plan (W2). The prospect–refuge hypothesis says people prefer places that give a view and cover. Its evidence is mixed, and it is OPEN.
- **DE.O.A2.2 Typology.** **A building or product type is a class of artefacts that share a stated schema, and membership can be tested against that schema.** Building and product types.
  - DE.O.A2.2.1 Types as classes. A type is defined by a function, a plan schema or an organisational schema, for example the courtyard house or the atrium office. Membership is tested by checking for the defining features. Occupancy classes in a building code are a different classification. They are defined by risk and by use, not by form (W12).
  - DE.O.A2.2.2 Persistence and transformation. A type does not fix a use, because conversion of warehouses to housing is common. A claim that form constrains use must name the constraint, such as plan depth, structural grid or floor-to-floor height. The history of a type is traced through dated buildings (W7, W6).
  - DE.O.A2.2.3 Product types and dominant designs. The dominant-design hypothesis (Utterback and Abernathy) says that after a period of variety one configuration becomes accepted and competition shifts to process. It rests on case comparison (W6). Its generality is CONTESTED. Its home is UNVERIFIED (alternative BU, uses BU).
- **DE.O.A2.3 Visual and material language.** **Composition, colour and material choices are described and justified by stated criteria, and whether they are beautiful is AR.** Composition, colour, materials. An aesthetic verdict with a stated criterion is AR.O.A5.
  - DE.O.A2.3.1 Composition and proportion. Proportion systems, such as the golden section and the Modulor, are rules for ratios. A designer may adopt one as a rule of the design, and compliance with the rule can be checked. The claim that observers prefer such ratios is CONTESTED, and its support in preference studies is weak (UNVERIFIED).
  - DE.O.A2.3.2 Colour and light. Colour notation systems, such as the Munsell and NCS systems, are stipulations (W12). The perceived colour of a surface depends on the illuminant and the surround (uses MS.O.A1.1). A colour specification must name the system and the illuminant. A measured colour value is W4.
  - DE.O.A2.3.3 Material selection and expression. A material is chosen by stated criteria: performance, durability, appearance and embodied carbon. The properties are CH and EN claims (uses CH.O.A5.4). Selection by a performance index (uses EN.M.B3) assumes the index is known and candidate properties are tabulated. Weathering is a testable design claim, for example that unprotected timber cladding silvers unevenly where exposure differs.
  - DE.O.A2.3.4 Visual communication and legibility. Legibility claims in graphic design are tested by reading speed or error rate (W3). The WCAG 2 level AA criterion sets a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (W12). That serif type reads better than sans-serif type for body text is not established (UNVERIFIED). Grouping principles come from perception research (uses MS.O.A1.1).

Named objects: plan graph, justified graph, relative asymmetry, isovist, prospect–refuge hypothesis, building type, dominant design, golden section, Modulor, Munsell system, NCS, contrast ratio. Relative asymmetry (hypotheses: connected graph; k above 2; root space stated). Isovist analysis (hypotheses: stated eye height; stated obstructions; static viewpoint). Form follows function (heuristic, CONTESTED as a rule).

### DE.O.A3 Performance in use

**SCQA.** *Situation.* A design is judged by how well it works for its users. *Complication.* Performance claims mix values predicted by models, values measured after occupation and compliance with code, and the three often differ. *Question.* Which claims about performance in use does design own? *Answer.* Design owns comfort, usability, safety and measured-use claims for a stated arrangement and stated occupants. Physics owns the laws (uses PH). Engineering owns structural and system adequacy (uses EN). Law owns the legal force of codes (uses LA).

**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: performance claims; dimension: the quality of use that is judged; order: from comfort, through usability and safety, to measured evaluation. Members (4): Comfort and indoor environment · Wayfinding and usability · Safety in use · Post-occupancy evaluation.

- **DE.O.A3.1 Comfort and indoor environment.** **A comfort claim names a person, a space, a period and a criterion.** Thermal, acoustic, daylight. The physics is PH; structural adequacy is EN.
  - DE.O.A3.1.1 Thermal comfort. The Fanger model (PMV and PPD, ISO 7730) predicts a group's mean vote. Its inputs are air temperature, radiant temperature, air speed, humidity, metabolic rate and clothing. It assumes steady conditions in a conditioned space. The adaptive model relates the acceptable indoor temperature to the running mean outdoor temperature. It assumes occupants can open windows and change clothing. Which model applies to a given building is CONTESTED (uses PH.O.A4.1).
  - DE.O.A3.1.2 Heat, moisture and air through the envelope. The U-value gives heat flow as Q = U A ΔT. It assumes steady state, one-dimensional flow and negligible heat storage, so it omits thermal mass. The Glaser method checks interstitial condensation. It assumes steady one-dimensional vapour diffusion and ignores air leakage, which can dominate moisture transport in real walls. Heat-transfer law is EN (uses EN.O.A3.3). Carbon dioxide indicates ventilation per person only where occupants are the main source (uses EV.O.A2.1).
  - DE.O.A3.1.3 Acoustic comfort. The Sabine formula gives reverberation time as T = 0.161 V/A, with V in cubic metres and A the total absorption in square metres. It assumes a diffuse sound field and evenly spread, low absorption. It fails for flat or long rooms and for strongly absorbent rooms. Sound insulation between rooms is rated by single-number ratings from a standard test (W12). Wave acoustics is EN (uses EN.O.A2.4).
  - DE.O.A3.1.4 Daylight and glare. The daylight factor is 100 × E_in/E_out under a standard overcast sky with fixed geometry. It excludes direct sun and gives no annual result. Climate-based metrics, such as useful daylight illuminance, use annual weather data and are model outputs. Glare indices are fitted to subjective ratings from a study sample, so their transfer to other users is UNVERIFIED. Light physics is PH (uses PH.O.A1.2.3).
- **DE.O.A3.2 Wayfinding and usability.** **People's success in finding a route or using a product is measured by task, not inferred from the plan or the product.**
  - DE.O.A3.2.1 Wayfinding in buildings and cities. Lynch reported five element types in residents' mental images of cities: paths, edges, districts, nodes and landmarks. The sample was a few North American cities, so generality is CONTESTED. Wayfinding errors cluster at decision points where routes branch. This is testable by tracking routes. Spatial cognition is MS (uses MS.O.A1.5).
  - DE.O.A3.2.2 Usability evaluation methods. Usability testing observes representative users doing set tasks. It assumes the participants and tasks represent real use and that the moderator does not lead. Heuristic evaluation finds problems by expert inspection against a published list. It assumes several evaluators work independently. It finds problems but not how often they occur.
  - DE.O.A3.2.3 Usability measures. The System Usability Scale has ten items and scores from 0 to 100. A score is not a percentage. Task completion, time on task and error counts are W4 when the tasks are fixed in advance. A commonly quoted average of about 68 for the scale is UNVERIFIED and population-specific.
  - DE.O.A3.2.4 Affordance and signifiers. An affordance is a possible action a thing offers, and a signifier is a cue that shows it. The claim that a flat plate invites pushing and a handle invites pulling is testable by the rate of wrong-way use on doors. The theory of perception is MS (uses MS.O.A1.1).
- **DE.O.A3.3 Safety in use.** **A safety claim states the hazard, the occupants and the criterion, and code compliance does not by itself show safety.** Building codes are W12; their legal force is LA.
  - DE.O.A3.3.1 Egress time. A design is safe for escape if the available safe egress time exceeds the required time with a margin. The available time runs until conditions become untenable. The required time is detection, alarm, pre-movement and travel. Hypotheses: a design fire is specified, occupant numbers and behaviour are assumed, and a margin is applied. Fire behaviour is EN (uses EN.O.A3.4).
  - DE.O.A3.3.2 Falls and stairs. Fall risk on stairs depends on riser and going geometry, surface, lighting and handrails. Blondel's rule, 2R + G of about 600 to 650 mm, is a comfort heuristic and not a safety limit. Codes set limits (W12) that differ by jurisdiction. The size of the injury effect of a given step geometry is UNVERIFIED.
  - DE.O.A3.3.3 Environmental crime prevention. The claim of defensible-space and crime-prevention design is that natural surveillance and territorial cues reduce opportunity for crime. Reported effects vary by setting, and the claim is CONTESTED. Crime as a social fact is MS (uses MS).
  - DE.O.A3.3.4 Prescriptive and performance codes. A code can state a solution (prescriptive) or an outcome with a way to verify it (performance-based). The New Zealand Building Code is performance-based. Acceptable solutions and verification methods are deemed to comply. Compliance is W11 against the code and W12 as a stipulation. Legal force is LA (uses LA.O.A1.1).
- **DE.O.A3.4 Post-occupancy evaluation.** **Post-occupancy evaluation measures a building in use against its own brief and its own predictions (W4).** Measured performance after use begins.
  - DE.O.A3.4.1 Occupant surveys. A standard occupant questionnaire, such as the Building Use Studies survey, gives scores that can be benchmarked across buildings. It assumes an adequate response rate and respondents who represent the occupants. Satisfaction is an input to evaluation, not a measure of performance.
  - DE.O.A3.4.2 Measured against predicted performance. The performance gap is the excess of measured operational energy over the design-stage prediction. Studies of new buildings often report a positive gap. Its size varies, and the shares due to modelling, construction and occupant behaviour are OPEN. A simulation output is a model conclusion, not evidence (DE.M.4).
  - DE.O.A3.4.3 Feedback into practice. Structured handover and aftercare processes aim to carry findings back into design. The claim that buildings with such a period use less energy than matched buildings without one is testable. It is UNVERIFIED. The claim that evaluation findings change later design practice is also UNVERIFIED.

Named objects: Fanger PMV–PPD model, adaptive comfort model, U-value, Glaser method, Sabine formula, daylight factor, useful daylight illuminance, glare index, Lynch's elements, usability test, heuristic evaluation, System Usability Scale, egress time comparison, Blondel's rule (heuristic), performance gap. Fanger model (hypotheses: steady conditions; conditioned space; six stated inputs). Sabine formula (hypotheses: diffuse field; even, low absorption). Daylight factor (hypotheses: standard overcast sky; fixed geometry). Egress time comparison (hypotheses: specified design fire; assumed occupant behaviour; stated margin).

### DE.O.A4 Place and settlement

**SCQA.** *Situation.* Designs sit on sites and add up to streets, neighbourhoods and cities. *Complication.* Place claims are also claimed by geography (the pattern as it is), economics (land price), law (the instrument) and politics (the decision). *Question.* Which claims about place and settlement does design own? *Answer.* Design owns what a proposed arrangement of site, urban form, landscape or land use is meant to do. It also owns the test of whether it does. The existing spatial pattern is EA.O.A5.3 (uses EA.O.A5.3). The instrument is LA, the decision is PO and the price is EC.

**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: place claims; dimension: the scale of the settlement arrangement; order: from the single site to the whole settlement plan. Members (4): Site · Urban form · Landscape architecture · Planning.

- **DE.O.A4.1 Site.** **A site claim states the measurable conditions of a plot and the response the design makes to them.** The physical site claim uses EA (uses EA.O.A5.1).
  - DE.O.A4.1.1 Site analysis. Site analysis records topography, sun path, wind, drainage, access and neighbours. Each item is a measurement (W4) or a mapped data set. A design response is justified only if it names the condition it answers. The method is DE.M.2.
  - DE.O.A4.1.2 Orientation and solar access. In the southern hemisphere the equator-facing side is north, and at New Zealand latitudes it receives the most winter sun. The claim follows from sun-path geometry (uses PH). Neighbouring buildings and trees change it, and a sun-path model can show by how much. The effect on energy use depends on glazing area, shading and thermal mass.
  - DE.O.A4.1.3 Hazard-informed siting. Hazard maps for flood, slope failure and ground shaking are EV and EA claims (uses EV.O.A2.6). The design claim is that floor levels, setbacks or siting respond to the stated hazard at a stated return period. It assumes the hazard model holds over the design life. Whether it holds under a changing climate is UNVERIFIED. Structural response is EN.
- **DE.O.A4.2 Urban form.** **Urban form claims describe the arrangement of buildings, streets and uses in a settlement, and are tested against measured pattern and use.** Density, street networks, mixed use.
  - DE.O.A4.2.1 Density measures. A density is a ratio and must state its numerator (dwellings, people or floor area) and its denominator (net site or gross area). The same development gives different values on each basis. Floor area ratio is gross floor area divided by site area. The claim can be tested by recomputation from plans.
  - DE.O.A4.2.2 Street networks and connectivity. Connectivity is measured by intersection density, block size and the gamma index, e/(3(v − 2)) for a planar network (uses MA.O.A2). The claim that more connected networks raise walking is observational. It needs causal identification (W5) because residents self-select where they live. Effect sizes vary between studies.
  - DE.O.A4.2.3 Mixed use and diversity. Jacobs proposed four conditions for urban diversity: mixed primary uses, short blocks, buildings of varied age, and a sufficient concentration of people. The conjunction has not been tested as a whole in a way that settles it. The claim is CONTESTED.
  - DE.O.A4.2.4 Urban morphology. The Conzen school divides urban form into three complexes: the town plan, the building fabric, and the land and building utilisation. The claim that plot patterns persist longer than the buildings on them is testable on historical maps (W7). Where production and exchange occur is EA.O.A5.3.
- **DE.O.A4.3 Landscape architecture.** **A landscape claim relates designed open space to its function for people, water and living things.**
  - DE.O.A4.3.1 Landscape character and visual effect. Landscape assessment describes character areas and judges visual change from chosen viewpoints. Hypotheses: the viewpoints are representative and the visualisations are accurate. A predicted view can be tested against photographs after construction. The judgement of sensitivity is argued (W9). A verdict on beauty is AR.
  - DE.O.A4.3.2 Green infrastructure and stormwater. Swales, rain gardens and permeable surfaces are claimed to cut peak runoff and volume for small storms, and this can be measured on site (W4). The rational method gives peak flow as Q = CiA. It assumes a small catchment, rainfall of uniform intensity lasting the time of concentration, and a constant runoff coefficient. The hydrology is EA (uses EA.O.A4.2).
  - DE.O.A4.3.3 Planting and ecological function. A planting claim names species, spacing, site conditions and function, such as shade, screening or habitat. Canopy shade lowers surface temperature beneath it, and the design claim states the cover and the magnitude. Habitat value is a BI claim (uses BI.O.A5.3). The patch–corridor–matrix model assumes species move along corridors. Its generality is OPEN. That views of vegetation reduce stress is also OPEN.
- **DE.O.A4.4 Planning.** **A planning claim states the intended pattern and the rules that would produce it, and its effect is tested against what follows.** Zoning and land use. The legal instrument is LA; the political decision is PO.
  - DE.O.A4.4.1 Zoning and land-use control. A zone rule separates uses or limits height, coverage or floor area ratio. Rules may regulate by activity, by effect or by built form, and which applies is jurisdiction-specific (uses LA.O.A4.2). Claims that a rule changed supply, price or form need causal identification (W5). Land price effects are EC (uses EC.O.A2.4).
  - DE.O.A4.4.2 Land suitability analysis. Map overlay combines mapped criteria to rank sites. It assumes the criteria can be put on a common scale, the weights are justified, and the criteria are independent. Otherwise the same factor is counted twice. A stated test shows how far the ranking changes when the weights change.
  - DE.O.A4.4.3 Participation. The IAP2 spectrum has five levels: inform, consult, involve, collaborate and empower. It assumes the decision-maker states in advance how input will be used. Arnstein's ladder has eight rungs and is a normative scheme. Whether more participation gives better plans is OPEN. The decision itself is PO (uses PO.O.A4.1).
  - DE.O.A4.4.4 Travel demand and land use. The four-step model forecasts trip generation, distribution, mode choice and route assignment. It assumes travel behaviour stays as surveyed, the area is zoned, and the model is calibrated to counts. Forecasts for new facilities are often wrong by large margins, and the typical size is UNVERIFIED. CONTESTED: alternative home EN.B.C1 (transport engineering).

Named objects: site analysis, sun path, floor area ratio, gamma index, Jacobs's conditions, Conzen's form complexes, visual impact assessment, rational method, patch–corridor–matrix model, zoning, map overlay analysis, IAP2 spectrum, Arnstein's ladder, four-step travel demand model. Map overlay analysis (hypotheses: common scale; justified weights; independent criteria). Rational method (hypotheses: small catchment; uniform rainfall over the time of concentration; constant runoff coefficient). Four-step model (hypotheses: stable travel behaviour; zoned area; calibrated to counts). Shift-share analysis of regional job change describes and does not explain, depends on the industry classification, and is owned by EC (uses EC).

### DE.O.A5 Life of the built thing

**SCQA.** *Situation.* Buildings and landscapes last decades to centuries, and outlive briefs, owners and uses. *Complication.* Claims about durability, carbon and heritage mix physical measurement, choice of boundary and judgements of value. *Question.* Which claims about the later life of a built thing does design own? *Answer.* Design owns how an arrangement is kept, changed, accounted for in resources and conserved, with the period and the boundary stated. The environmental impact is EV (uses EV). The heritage meaning is HI.O.A5.3 (uses HI.O.A5.3).

**Key line.** Inductive; plural noun: life-of-the-thing claims; dimension: what is done to the built thing over time; order: keep, then account, then conserve. Members (3): Maintenance and adaptive reuse · Sustainability · Conservation of built heritage.

- **DE.O.A5.1 Maintenance and adaptive reuse.** **A maintenance or adaptation claim states the layer of the building, the interval and the cost basis.**
  - DE.O.A5.1.1 Layers of change. Brand's six layers are site, structure, skin, services, space plan and stuff, adapted from Duffy. The claim is that the layers change at different rates, with structure slowest. The rates are UNVERIFIED and can be tested from replacement records.
  - DE.O.A5.1.2 Service life and maintenance. The factor method of ISO 15686 modifies a reference service life by seven factors. They are component quality, design level, work execution, indoor environment, outdoor environment, in-use conditions and maintenance level. It assumes the reference life comes from comparable conditions and the factors combine multiplicatively. The multiplicative assumption is CONTESTED. Planned maintenance lowers whole-life cost only at some discount rates (uses EC).
  - DE.O.A5.1.3 Adaptive reuse. A building can take a new use if its structure, floor-to-floor height, plan depth and services can meet the new requirement. The code position must also be met, at acceptable cost. Elemental cost planning builds an estimate from the cost of each element. It assumes cost data from comparable projects exist and prices are adjusted for time and place. Whether reuse beats new build on carbon depends on the boundary (DE.O.A5.2.2).
  - DE.O.A5.1.4 Weathertightness and moisture durability. A cladding system with a drained and ventilated cavity tolerates water entry better than a direct-fixed system. New Zealand's weathertightness failures are cited as evidence, but their magnitude by cladding type is UNVERIFIED. Moisture physics is PH and EN (uses PH, EN).
- **DE.O.A5.2 Sustainability.** **A carbon or resource claim about a building holds only for a stated boundary, period and data set.** Embodied and operational carbon. The environmental impact claim is EV.
  - DE.O.A5.2.1 Operational energy and carbon. Operational carbon is energy use multiplied by the emission factor of the supply. The factor varies by grid and over time. A design that is efficient on one grid may not emit least on another. The emission inventory method is EV (uses EV.O.A1.1). The energy model is DE.M.4.
  - DE.O.A5.2.2 Embodied carbon and life cycle assessment. Life cycle assessment of a building totals the burdens of materials, use and disposal. It assumes the functional unit and study period are set and the system boundary is stated. The EN 15978 modules run from product stage (A1 to A3) through construction, use and end of life. Benefits beyond the boundary (D) are declared separately. Results from different boundaries are not comparable. The treatment of biogenic carbon is CONTESTED.
  - DE.O.A5.2.3 Standards and rating tools. The Passive House standard sets a heating demand limit of 15 kWh per square metre per year (W12). That figure is UNVERIFIED against the current edition. Rating tools award points by weightings that are conventions (W12). Whether certified buildings use less energy in operation than uncertified ones is testable by metering, and the evidence is mixed (UNVERIFIED).
  - DE.O.A5.2.4 Circularity and disassembly. Design for disassembly claims that components can be recovered and reused at end of life. The reuse rate achieved in practice is OPEN. The evidence for working reuse markets for building components is limited (UNVERIFIED).
- **DE.O.A5.3 Conservation of built heritage.** **A conservation claim states the significance of a place and shows that an intervention keeps it.** The heritage meaning is HI.O.A5.3.
  - DE.O.A5.3.1 Cultural significance. The Burra Charter process bases action on the place's stated significance. Its values are aesthetic, historic, scientific, social and spiritual. It assumes significance is assessed before decisions and change is kept to the minimum needed. The meaning of the place is HI (uses HI.O.A5.3).
  - DE.O.A5.3.2 Authenticity and principles of intervention. Common principles are minimum intervention, new work distinguishable from old, and reversibility. Reversibility is CONTESTED, because few interventions can be fully undone. The Nara document treats authenticity as judged within a cultural context. The alternative is a test based on original material. The choice between them is CONTESTED.
  - DE.O.A5.3.3 Compatibility of repair materials. Repair materials should be compatible with the originals. A hard cement mortar on soft historic brick is commonly reported to concentrate moisture and stress in the brick and speed its decay. The claim can be tested on a given wall (W3, W4). Its generality is UNVERIFIED. Materials science is CH (uses CH.O.A5.4).
  - DE.O.A5.3.4 Strengthening and significance. Seismic strengthening of a historic building trades safety, cost and loss of fabric. Whether the strengthening is adequate is EN. The legal trigger for strengthening is LA (uses LA.O.A4.2). Design owns whether the chosen intervention keeps every element the statement of significance names.

Named objects: building layers, service life factor method, elemental cost planning, adaptive reuse, operational carbon, whole-life carbon, EN 15978 modules, Passive House standard, Burra Charter process, statement of significance, authenticity. Life cycle assessment of buildings (hypotheses: set functional unit and study period; stated system boundary; data from declared products). Service life factor method (hypotheses: comparable reference life; multiplicative factors). Burra Charter process (hypotheses: significance assessed first; minimum change). Elemental cost planning (hypotheses: comparable cost data; time and place adjustment).

### Heuristics register (outside the count)
Form follows function (CONTESTED as a rule); pattern languages (Alexander); "less is more"; precedent study.
- Form follows function is read as a design rule. As a rule it fails where one form serves several functions over time (DE.O.A2.2.2).
- A pattern language is a set of named design solutions to recurring problems. It is a heuristic. Whether following patterns improves measured outcomes is OPEN.
- "Less is more" and precedent study guide choice and give no warrant. Precedent study supplies cases for comparison (W6) only.
- Local rules of thumb, such as the daylight depth rule (DE.O.A2.1.3) and Blondel's rule (DE.O.A3.3.2), are labelled heuristic where they occur. They are outside the count.

Field-specific examples of each row:
- DE.O.A1. A user-research claim (W4) fails when the sample omits older users, children or users with impairments. An agreed brief (W12) fails as a warrant when the client who signed it is not the user.
- DE.O.A2. A rationale (W9) for a plan type is argued from the programme and the site. Precedent comparison (W6) fails when the precedents differ in climate, structure or use. A proportion rule offered as necessity is taste presented as necessity.
- DE.O.A3. A measured illuminance or reverberation time (W4) fails as a warrant for comfort if taken in an unoccupied room. Code compliance (W11) shows compliance, not satisfaction in use. A predicted energy figure is W2 only with its error bound stated.
- DE.O.A4. Site data (W4) and comparative studies of cities (W6) fail across contexts. A street layout that raises walking in one city may not do so in another, and self-selection of residents confounds the comparison.
- DE.O.A5. A life-cycle result (W2) holds only inside its boundary and study period. A conservation claim rests on a statement of significance (W8) and, for materials, on testing (W3).
