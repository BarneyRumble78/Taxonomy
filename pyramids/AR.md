# AR — Arts and literature

Phase 2 rebuild of the prototype (T/prototype_arts_religion.md, Part One) at Standard v2. The prototype's cells, rules and named objects are kept unless a repair is noted. Repairs made here: two-sides wording replaced by four faces; HI and LN no longer marked provisional (both now prototyped); LCC coverage count corrected (the prototype said 22 of 30; the listed landings give 27 of 30); ANZSRC 36 crosswalk added; every named object given a stable ID; one W row per Face O cell.

Owners cited: MA (maths_reconciled.md), PH, CH, EN, CS, MS, EC, LA, PO, PL, HI, LN, RE. IDs of pyramids still in rebuild are cited as the audits and prototypes give them. If a rebuild renumbers them, the "uses" edges must be updated.

## 1. SCQA

**Situation.** Libraries file the arts by medium and language: LCC M for music, N for fine arts, P for language and literature. Departments follow: literature, art history, musicology, theatre, film. **Complication.** The same kind of sentence recurs in each department under another name. "The second subject returns in the tonic" and "the volta falls at line nine" are one kind of claim. Medium-first schemes repeat the aspects in every medium. They also mix works, such as scores and novels, with claims about works. **Question.** Which single partition places every claim about a work of art exactly once, by what the claim is about? **Answer.** Five aspects of the work, from material to judgment, with the medium as a substrate and the discipline as a bridge.

## 2. Governing thought

Arts and literature is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges. Its objects are claims about five aspects of the work: medium and making, form, meaning, context and reception, and value.

## 3. Key line

- **Logic.** Inductive.
- **Plural noun.** aspects of the work.
- **Dimension.** What feature of the work the sentence is about.
- **Order.** Structural, from the material object outward to judgment.

1. Medium and making
2. Form
3. Meaning
4. Context and reception
5. Value

**MECE test.** Exclusive: a sentence about arrangement (A2) does not assert what the work represents (A3). A reception fact (A4) does not assert worth (A5). Exhaustive: a sentence naming none of the five is about the perceiver's mind (MS), the material's chemistry (CH), a structure's stability (EN), or the concept of art (PL). Each is refused by rule (section 9).

## 4. Face O — Objects

### AR.O.A1 Medium and making

**SCQA.** *S.* Every work is made of something, by someone, and exists in an object, a performance or an edition. *C.* Technique, attribution and performance practice sit in different departments. *Q.* Which making claims are distinct? *A.* Three, by stage of production.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: making claims · dimension: the stage of production the sentence concerns · order: chronological, material then maker then realization.
1. **AR.O.A1.1 Material and technique** claims what the work is made of and by what process.
2. **AR.O.A1.2 Agency and attribution** claims who made it, alone or in a workshop.
3. **AR.O.A1.3 Realization and text** claims how a work exists in instances: performance, staging, edition.

**Named objects.**
- **AR.O.A1.1.1 Buon fresco versus secco.** Pigment bound by lime as plaster sets, versus pigment applied to dry plaster with a binder.
- **AR.O.A1.1.2 Oil glazing** in fifteenth-century Netherlandish panels.
- **AR.O.A1.1.3 Lost-wax casting.** A wax model is invested, melted out and replaced by metal.
- **AR.O.A1.1.4 Pentimento.** A change of composition visible under the paint layer.
- **AR.O.A1.1.5 Dendrochronological date of a panel.** A felling date is a lower bound on the painting date. It holds only if the board's outer rings survive or sapwood is estimated. The method's chronology is EA (uses EA, UNVERIFIED cell id).
- **AR.O.A1.1.6 Whakairo technique.** Māori carving technique, as a making claim. See T/p2_ANZSRC45.md.
- **AR.O.A1.2.1 Workshop attribution grades.** "Rembrandt", "studio of", "circle of", "follower of".
- **AR.O.A1.2.2 Rembrandt Research Project** (1968–2014) as an attribution programme (UNVERIFIED dates; from the prototype's link, not rechecked).
- **AR.O.A1.2.3 Stylometric attribution.** An authorship claim supported by function-word frequencies. The model is CS's; the attribution is here.
- **AR.O.A1.2.4 Whakairo lineage of training.** Which carving school trained a carver.
- **AR.O.A1.3.1 Tuning and temperament of a performance.** Twelve-tone equal temperament divides the octave into twelve equal frequency ratios of 2^(1/12). The ratio fact uses MA; that a named instrument was so tuned is here.
- **AR.O.A1.3.2 Figured bass.** A notation the performer realizes in chords.
- **AR.O.A1.3.3 Labanotation record** (Laban, 1928) of a named choreography.
- **AR.O.A1.3.4 Stemma and copy-text.** Lachmann's stemma assumes descent by copying with shared errors. Greg's copy-text rationale (1950) chooses the text for accidentals. Each yields a claim about the authorial reading.
- **AR.O.A1.3.5 Historically informed performance claims.** Example: the extent of continuous vibrato in eighteenth-century string playing. CONTESTED; the alternative reads treatises as prescriptive, not descriptive.

### AR.O.A2 Form

**SCQA.** *S.* Critics describe arrangement: meter, harmony, composition, plot. *C.* Some formal names pick out a shared code; others pick out one work's design. Mixing them confuses the norm with the instance. *Q.* Which formal claims are distinct? *A.* Two: system and work.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: formal claims · dimension: whether the sentence concerns a shared system or one work's arrangement (binary, MECE by construction) · order: general to particular.
1. **AR.O.A2.1 Formal systems** claim the shared codes many works use.
2. **AR.O.A2.2 Work-level design** claims one work's arrangement and its departures from the system.

**Named objects.**
- **AR.O.A2.1.1 Meter and prosody.** Iambic pentameter; quantitative meter; the mora in Japanese verse.
- **AR.O.A2.1.2 Tonality.** A hierarchy of pitches around a tonic, with functional harmony. Twelve-tone serialism (Schoenberg, early 1920s) is an alternative system.
- **AR.O.A2.1.3 Raga and maqam** as melodic-modal systems.
- **AR.O.A2.1.4 Sonata form (textbook model).** Abstracted from existing works by Reicha (1826), Marx (1845) and Czerny (1848). Koch (1793) described it by modulation. It is an analytic model, not a rule composers followed.
- **AR.O.A2.1.5 Linear perspective.** A central projection onto a plane. It assumes one fixed eye, a planar picture surface and straight light paths. Under these, parallel lines not parallel to the plane meet at a vanishing point. The projective geometry uses MA; the pictorial system is here. Brunelleschi (c. 1415–20) and Alberti, *De pictura* (1435).
- **AR.O.A2.1.6 Focalization** (Genette, 1972): who sees versus who speaks. Free indirect discourse.
- **AR.O.A2.1.7 Fabula and syuzhet** (Russian Formalists): story order versus telling order.
- **AR.O.A2.1.8 Continuity editing and montage** in film.
- **AR.O.A2.1.9 Genre as formal definition.** The sonnet's fourteen lines and volta. Genre as history is AR.O.A4.3.
- **AR.O.A2.1.10 Style as shared formal traits.** "Baroque" as traits. CONTESTED; "Baroque" as dates is HI.O.A2.
- **AR.O.A2.2.1 The recapitulation of a named movement.**
- **AR.O.A2.2.2 The volta of a named sonnet.**
- **AR.O.A2.2.3 The Sydney Opera House as a design.** Its form; its stability is EN.

### AR.O.A3 Meaning

**SCQA.** *S.* Works represent, symbolise, express and allude. *C.* Iconography, theme, tone and intertextuality are taught as rival theories. They answer one question by different routes. *Q.* By which routes does content attach to a work? *A.* Four, from most direct to most mediated.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: routes of meaning · dimension: how content attaches to the work · order: structural, most direct to most mediated.
1. **AR.O.A3.1 Depiction and reference** claims what the work represents or denotes.
2. **AR.O.A3.2 Coded meaning** claims what it signifies by a convention: symbol, allegory, attribute, leitmotif.
3. **AR.O.A3.3 Expression** claims the attitude, feeling or tone the work presents.
4. **AR.O.A3.4 Intertextual meaning** claims content carried by relation to other works.

**Named objects.**
- **AR.O.A3.1.1 Subject and mimesis.** What a painting depicts; the story a ballet tells.
- **AR.O.A3.1.2 Program music.** Music tied to a stated extra-musical subject.
- **AR.O.A3.2.1 Iconographic type.** Saint Catherine identified by the wheel. Panofsky's levels (1939) are a method (AR.M.5).
- **AR.O.A3.2.2 Allegory and emblem.**
- **AR.O.A3.2.3 Leitmotif in Wagner** as an in-work code.
- **AR.O.A3.2.4 Affektenlehre** as conventional affects of Baroque figures. CONTESTED: code (A3.2) when conventional; expression (A3.3) when the claim is the felt quality.
- **AR.O.A3.3.1 Rasa** (Nāṭyaśāstra): the aesthetic flavour a drama presents.
- **AR.O.A3.3.2 Irony and the unreliable narrator** (Booth, 1961).
- **AR.O.A3.4.1 Intertextuality** (Kristeva, 1966; published 1969) as a theory of meaning by relation. The dated fact of a borrowing is AR.O.A4.3.
- **AR.O.A3.4.2 Allusion, parody, pastiche and musical quotation.**

### AR.O.A4 Context and reception

**SCQA.** *S.* Works are commissioned, published, exhibited, performed, reviewed and taught. *C.* Patronage, book history, reception theory and canon studies look like four fields. They follow one work through its social life. *Q.* Which stages of that life are distinct? *A.* Four, in time order.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: stages of the work's social life · dimension: the stage the sentence concerns · order: chronological, commission to institution.
1. **AR.O.A4.1 Production context** claims the conditions under which the work was commissioned and made.
2. **AR.O.A4.2 Circulation** claims how the work moved: publication, ownership, exhibition, performance history.
3. **AR.O.A4.3 Reception** claims how audiences and later makers took it up.
4. **AR.O.A4.4 Canon and institution** claims where the work sits in lists, collections and curricula.

**Named objects.**
- **AR.O.A4.1.1 Patronage contract.** Baxandall (1972) on contracts specifying pigments, such as ultramarine for the Virgin's robe.
- **AR.O.A4.2.1 Provenance.** The chain of ownership of a named object.
- **AR.O.A4.2.2 Publication history.** The serial novel; folio and quarto.
- **AR.O.A4.2.3 Premiere and performance history.** The 1913 Paris premiere of *The Rite of Spring*.
- **AR.O.A4.3.1 Horizon of expectations** (Jauss, 1967) and the implied reader (Iser, 1972).
- **AR.O.A4.3.2 Influence and borrowing as dated facts.** Joyce's use of the *Odyssey*. Bloom's anxiety of influence (1973) is a contested theory of it.
- **AR.O.A4.3.3 Distant reading** (Moretti, 2000). Counts over a corpus as reception evidence. The corpus model is CS's.
- **AR.O.A4.3.4 Genre as history.** The Gothic novel's emergence and audience.
- **AR.O.A4.4.1 The canon as a reading list.** Anthologies, syllabi, museum holdings. A count, not a verdict.
- **AR.O.A4.4.2 Critics' polls as data.** The *Sight and Sound* poll, decennial since 1952. The result is here; each ballot is AR.O.A5.2.

### AR.O.A5 Value

**SCQA.** *S.* Critics judge works and performances and give reasons. *C.* Philosophy owns what merit is (PL.O.A4.4). The applied verdict on a named work has no other home. *Q.* Which applied verdicts are distinct? *A.* Three, by the object judged.

**Key line.** Inductive · plural noun: verdicts · dimension: the object judged · order: structural, one work, a set of works, a realization.
1. **AR.O.A5.1 Verdict on a work** claims how one named work stands on a stated criterion.
2. **AR.O.A5.2 Comparative and canon judgments** claim how works rank, and which deserve a place.
3. **AR.O.A5.3 Judgment of a realization** claims how a performance, staging, edition or restoration stands.

**Named objects.**
- **AR.O.A5.1.1 Eliot on *Hamlet*** ("Hamlet and His Problems", 1919): "most certainly an artistic failure".
- **AR.O.A5.1.2 Architecture criticism of a named building.**
- **AR.O.A5.1.3 Xie He's six principles applied to a named painting.** The principles as general criteria are PL.O.A4.4.
- **AR.O.A5.2.1 Canon arguments.** Bloom, *The Western Canon* (1994), and its critics.
- **AR.O.A5.2.2 A prize jury's reasons; a critic's poll ballot.**
- **AR.O.A5.3.1 Record review of a named performance.** Tempo and balance judgments.
- **AR.O.A5.3.2 Judgment of a restoration.** Whether the Sistine ceiling cleaning (1980–94) removed original secco work. CONTESTED.

## 5. Face M — Methods

Five roles. Order: from the work to its world, fix it, read it, examine it, make it, set it among others.

| Role ID | Role | Maps to | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| AR.M.1 | Description and notation | M1 Represent | Scansion; critical edition; catalogue raisonné; Schenkerian graph as notation; Labanotation; shot list |
| AR.M.2 | Close reading and formal analysis | M2 Derive | Close reading; motivic and harmonic analysis; Sonata Theory (Hepokoski and Darcy, 2006) applied to a movement |
| AR.M.3 | Technical and archival examination | M3 Observe | X-radiography; infrared reflectography; XRF; dendrochronology; analytical bibliography; watermarks; provenance research |
| AR.M.4 | Making and realization | M4 Intervene | Practice-as-research; historically informed performance; reconstruction staging; conservation treatment |
| AR.M.5 | Comparison and interpretation | M6 Compare and reconstruct | Comparative literature; connoisseurship; Panofsky's iconological method; theory-driven readings |

**Boundaries of the roles.** The edition's text decision is a claim (AR.O.A1.3); notation is the role. A reading not traceable to the record has left AR.M.2. An instrument's physics and chemistry are PH and CH; the dated finding is AR. A making result counts as a claim only with a documented record (W8) or a controlled comparison (W3); CONTESTED. A theory used in reading is an answer-family. Its general truth is owed to its home pyramid (MS, EC, PL).

**Shared roles borrowed, not counted.** M5 Compute: stylometry and corpus models are CS methods (uses CS). M7 Verify: authentication boards, peer review and collation checks are community practices of the bridges.

### Heuristics register (outside the count)

- **AR.M.H1** Form follows function (Sullivan, 1896). A design maxim, not a law.
- **AR.M.H2** The rule of thirds. A composition habit, not a finding.
- **AR.M.H3** The golden section in the Parthenon or in Mozart. Mostly unmeasured; a heuristic or myth until measured.
- **AR.M.H4** Chekhov's gun. A dramaturgical economy rule.
- **AR.M.H5** Show, don't tell. A workshop rule.
- **AR.M.H6** "The test of time." Hume's argument (1757) is PL.O.A4.4; the slogan is a heuristic.
- **AR.M.H7** "The author is dead" (Barthes, 1967). The argument is PL; the slogan settles nothing.
- **AR.M.H8** The intentional fallacy (Wimsatt and Beardsley, 1946) used as a veto. The argument is PL; the veto is a heuristic.
- **AR.M.H9** Morellian details (ears, hands). A cue for AR.M.3 and AR.M.5, not a proof.

## 8. Assignment rules

1. **The work is not a claim.** A score, novel, painting or film is a substrate. Only sentences about works are filed.
2. **Medium is a substrate.** Music, painting, verse and film name bridges, never Face O cells.
3. **Direction of evidence decides arts versus history.** If the past is evidence for the work, the sentence is AR. If the work is evidence for the past, it is HI. "The contract required ultramarine" is AR.O.A4.1. "Guild records show pigment prices rising" is HI or EC.
4. **Value divides with philosophy at generality.** What merit is, and which properties confer it on any work, is PL.O.A4.4. Applying a stated criterion to named works is AR.O.A5.
5. **Valued is not valuable.** That a work is canonical, sold or polled is AR.O.A4. That it ought to be is AR.O.A5.
6. **Response mechanism is mind.** How perceivers process works in general is MS.O.A1. That a named work was received in a named way is AR.O.A4.3.
7. **Structure stays in engineering.** Whether a shell or vault stands is EN.O.A1. Its form and meaning are AR.O.A2 and AR.O.A3.
8. **Material identity stays in chemistry.** That a pigment is lead-tin yellow type I is CH.O.A1. That this panel used it is AR.O.A1.1, which uses CH.O.A1.
9. **The language system is linguistics.** English stress phonology is LN.O.A1. The metrical scheme of a poem is AR.O.A2.1. A poem's syntax as a fact of the language is LN.O.A2.
10. **Markets, rights and rule are elsewhere.** Prices and auction effects are EC.O.A2. Copyright and moral rights are LA.O.A2.2. Censorship as an act of authority is PO; its effect on the work is AR.O.A4.2.
11. **Sacred art splits.** An icon's form and making are AR. Its veneration and theological status are RE.O.A3.1. The ruling of Nicaea II (787) is an RE·T sentence.
12. **Style labels split.** "Baroque" as shared formal traits is AR.O.A2.1. "Baroque" as a span of dates is HI.O.A2. CONTESTED.
13. **No miscellaneous bucket.** Non-Western forms are filed by aspect. Raga is AR.O.A2.1; rasa is AR.O.A3.3; a haka's performance is AR.O.A1.3.
14. **Built environment (gap claim absorbed).** Design to a stated use is EN. Its aesthetic verdict is AR.O.A5. Its site is EA; its price is EC.
15. **Cited results.** Equal-temperament ratios and projective geometry use MA (uses MA.O.A2, UNVERIFIED cell). Stylometric models use CS. Dendrochronological chronology uses EA.
