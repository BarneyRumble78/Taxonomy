# Question pyramid: agricultural nitrogen and freshwater eutrophication in New Zealand

This is one problem pyramid. It is not the EV classification. The classification framework is `pyramids/EV_minto.md`.

A question pyramid has a governing thought, an SCQ introduction, a key line of yes/no issues (Minto, Chapter 9, p. 163), evidence leaves linked to classification nodes, and a hypothesis test (Appendix A).

## Introduction

**Situation.** New Zealand reports the state of rivers and lakes, and the nitrogen and phosphorus that reach them.

**Complication.** A dominant source of leached nitrate is not the same claim as the main cause of algal growth in every freshwater body.

**Question.** Is agricultural nitrogen runoff the main driver of freshwater eutrophication in New Zealand?

## Governing thought

Pastoral urine dominates leached nitrate, but the reports do not show that nitrogen is the main cause of eutrophication in every New Zealand freshwater body.

## Key line

Group of issues. Basis: kinds. Dimension: whether the issue is the source, the load, or the harm. Order: structure.

1. **Yes.** Is livestock urine the dominant source of nitrate-nitrogen that leaches from New Zealand soils? Uses `EV.P.1.1.19` (fertilisers are nutrient loads applied to land). The measured freshwater state is `EV.P.2.2`.
2. **Yes.** Did pastoral nitrogen loads to water rise from 1995 to 2015 while phosphorus loads fell? Uses `EV.P.1.5` (drivers are the activities that create a load).
3. **No.** Do these reports show that nitrogen, rather than nutrients in general, is the main cause of excessive algal growth in every freshwater body? Uses `EV.P.3.1.9` (system harm includes freshwater).

## Evidence

- Ministry for the Environment and Stats NZ, *Our freshwater 2023*, https://environment.govt.nz/publications/our-freshwater-2023/, retrieved 2026-10-12. Livestock urine is the dominant source of nitrate-nitrogen leaching. On-farm mitigations reduced phosphorus and sediment reaching rivers between 1995 and 2015, but not nitrogen. Elevated nutrient levels can lead to excessive algal growth.
- Monaghan et al. 2021, *New Zealand Journal of Agricultural Research* 64(3), 365–389, retrieved 2026-10-12 from the copy at https://landscapedna.org/references/nzj-agres-2021.pdf. Nitrogen loads from pastoral land rose from 169 to 210 Gg per year between 1995 and 2015. Phosphorus loads fell from 11.5 to 8.8 Gg per year.

## Hypothesis

**Null.** Agricultural nitrogen runoff is not the main driver of freshwater eutrophication in New Zealand.

**Alternative.** Agricultural nitrogen runoff is the main driver of freshwater eutrophication in New Zealand.

**Test.** Compare the stated source of leached nitrate with the stated cause of excessive algal growth.

**Result.** Reject the null for the source of leached nitrate. Do not reject the null for eutrophication in every freshwater body.

**What would defeat the answer.** A national study that attributes most algal-growth impairment to nitrogen rather than phosphorus, across rivers and lakes, would defeat this answer. Snelder et al. 2021 is cited by *Our freshwater 2023*, but its findings were not read here (UNVERIFIED).
