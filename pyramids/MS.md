## 1. SCQA

Situation. Psychology studies mental process and the person. Neuroscience studies the nervous system. Sociology studies patterns that outlast the persons in them. APA lists 54 divisions; ASA lists 55 sections; SfN lists 11 themes. Complication. One fact is filed three ways: a memory is a process, a hippocampal trace and a shared story. v1 also filed methods inside Side A, counted heuristics as a role and left anthropology, criminology, communication and education without a home. Question. Which single partition places every claim about mind, brain and society exactly once, with methods, warrants and bridges kept apart? Answer. Sort every claim by the unit it is about; sort practices by what they do; read each named field as a bridge.

## 2. Governing thought

Mind, brain and society is exhausted by four faces of one pyramid: objects, methods, warrants and bridges. Its objects are mental processes, nervous systems, persons, interactions and social structures.

## 3. Key line

Logic: inductive. Plural noun: units. Dimension: the unit the claim is about. Order: structural, from the smallest unit that bears a mental claim to the largest pattern.

1. Mental processes
2. Nervous systems

Boundary sentences kept from v1. The ion channel is neuroscience. The report of a memory is psychology. The distribution of that report across a class is sociology. Social psychology is the interaction unit asked with psychological methods. Cognitive neuroscience is the process unit asked with neural measures.

## 4. Face O — Objects

### MS.O.A1 Mental processes

Situation. Psychology's core is the process that turns input into report and act. Complication. Its laws hold over stated ranges, and its biases are relative to a stated norm. Question. Which processes does a claim about the working mind concern? Answer. Five, ordered from input to evaluation.

Key line (inductive; plural noun processes; dimension stage of the processing; order input to evaluation): sensation and perception · attention and consciousness · learning and memory · emotion and motivation · thought, language and judgement.

- **MS.O.A1.1 Sensation and perception.** A stimulus is transduced; a percept is constructed. Named objects:
  - MS.O.A1.1.1 Weber's law: over a mid-range of intensities, the just-noticeable difference ΔI is a roughly constant fraction k of the base intensity I (Gescheider 1997).
  - MS.O.A1.1.2 Fechner's law: if Weber's law holds and each JND is a unit step, sensation grows with the logarithm of intensity.
  - MS.O.A1.1.3 Stevens's power law: for direct magnitude estimation over a stated range, perceived magnitude is a power function of intensity.
  - MS.O.A1.1.4 Signal detection theory: under equal-variance Gaussian noise and signal distributions, d′ measures sensitivity and c (or β) measures criterion. A classical threshold confounds the two (Green and Swets 1966).
- **MS.O.A1.2 Attention and consciousness.** Selection has a cost; reportable experience has measurable conditions.
  - MS.O.A1.2.1 Selective attention and capacity limits; inattentional blindness.
  - MS.O.A1.2.2 Consciousness as reportable experience, studied with no-report controls. The hard problem is PL.O.A2; MS uses it and does not claim to solve it.
  - MS.O.A1.2.3 Hypnosis and altered states, as changes in attention and report (APA Div. 30 home).
- **MS.O.A1.3 Learning and memory.**
  - MS.O.A1.3.1 Classical conditioning: a cue comes to predict an outcome. Rescorla–Wagner model: on each trial, ΔV = αβ(λ − ΣV), where α and β are cue and outcome salience rates in (0,1], λ is the outcome's maximum, and ΣV sums all cues present.
  - MS.O.A1.3.2 Operant conditioning and schedules of reinforcement.
  - MS.O.A1.3.3 Memory systems: declarative (episodic, semantic) and nondeclarative (procedural, priming, classical conditioning, nonassociative) (Squire 2004).
  - MS.O.A1.3.4 Working-memory capacity: about 3–5 chunks when rehearsal and chunking are blocked (Cowan 2001). The 7±2 figure is a heuristic (register).
  - MS.O.A1.3.5 Forgetting. Cue-dependent retrieval failure (Tulving 1974) is one answer. Interference, decay and failed consolidation are rivals (CONTESTED; Wixted 2004).
- **MS.O.A1.4 Emotion and motivation.** A state with a trigger, a bodily change and an action tendency.
  - MS.O.A1.4.1 Appraisal accounts and basic-emotion accounts, as rival answer-families.
  - MS.O.A1.4.2 Motivation and reward as process claims. The tissue (orbitofrontal cortex, vmPFC, ventral striatum) is MS.O.A2.3.
- **MS.O.A1.5 Thought, language and judgement.**
  - MS.O.A1.5.1 Concepts and categories.
  - MS.O.A1.5.2 Judgement heuristics (availability, representativeness, anchoring; Tversky and Kahneman 1974). They are biases relative to a stated normative model. The rule of thumb used by an analyst is a different sentence, in the register.
  - MS.O.A1.5.3 Psycholinguistic processing (comprehension, production). The grammar itself is LN.
  - MS.O.A1.5.4 Creativity and problem solving (APA Div. 10 home). The aesthetic verdict is AR.
  - MS.O.A1.5.5 RETIRED in Phase 2.1 (ID not reused). Learning claims under an educational arrangement, including physics and mathematics education (MSC 97), are ED.O.A2–A4. MS keeps the general mechanism of learning and memory in MS.O.A1.5.

### MS.O.A2 Nervous systems

Situation. Neuroscience's core runs from molecule to system. Complication. v1 filed lesion, imaging and computational models here, which are methods. Question. Which tissue claims does neuroscience own? Answer. Four, ordered by scale and then by time.

Key line (inductive; plural noun levels; dimension scale of the tissue, then time; order cell to system, then development): neurons, glia and synapses · plasticity · circuits and systems · development of the nervous system.

- **MS.O.A2.1 Neurons, glia and synapses.**
  - MS.O.A2.1.1 Action potential: an all-or-none spike, given a threshold depolarisation of an excitable membrane.
  - MS.O.A2.1.2 Chemical and electrical synapses; excitation and inhibition.
  - MS.O.A2.1.3 Glia: astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia and their signalling roles (SfN Theme B).
  - The equilibrium potential (Nernst, GHK) uses CH.O.A3.3. The cell as a cell is BI; electrical signalling is native here.
- **MS.O.A2.2 Plasticity.**
  - MS.O.A2.2.1 Long-term potentiation and depression, as candidate mechanisms of learning. A changed synapse is not yet a memory; the memory is MS.O.A1.3.
- **MS.O.A2.3 Circuits and systems.**
  - MS.O.A2.3.1 Sensory and motor pathways.
  - MS.O.A2.3.2 Hypothalamus and homeostasis.
  - MS.O.A2.3.3 Valuation circuits: orbitofrontal cortex, vmPFC, ventral striatum. "Limbic system" is a contested grouping and is not used as a cell.
  - MS.O.A2.3.4 Localisation as a starting map; a function is usually a circuit.
- **MS.O.A2.4 Development of the nervous system.**
  - MS.O.A2.4.1 Overproduction and pruning.
  - MS.O.A2.4.2 Critical and sensitive periods, with their measured windows.

### MS.O.A3 Persons

Situation. Persons differ stably and change across the life course. Complication. Differences invite reification, and clinical categories are conventions. Question. Which claims concern the person as the unit? Answer. Four, ordered from stable difference to disorder.

Key line (inductive; plural noun aspects; dimension what persists or changes in the person; order from stable trait to clinical pattern): personality · intelligence and abilities · life-course development · psychopathology.

- **MS.O.A3.1 Personality.**
  - MS.O.A3.1.1 Five-factor model as the common answer. HEXACO (six factors; Ashton and Lee 2007) is the rival (CONTESTED).
  - MS.O.A3.1.2 Person–situation debate: both variances are measured.
- **MS.O.A3.2 Intelligence and abilities.**
  - MS.O.A3.2.1 g as a statistical factor (Spearman 1904), not a thing seen.
  - MS.O.A3.2.2 Heritability of intelligence: a variance fraction in a stated population, environment and time, broad (H²) or narrow (h²). The quantitative-genetics result uses BI.O.A4.
- **MS.O.A3.3 Life-course development.**
  - MS.O.A3.3.1 Stage theories as ideal types.
  - MS.O.A3.3.2 Attachment classification (Ainsworth; Main and Solomon).
  - MS.O.A3.3.3 Ageing and adult development.
- **MS.O.A3.4 Psychopathology.** The psychological process account of distress and disability.
  - MS.O.A3.4.1 Diagnostic categories as conventions: DSM-5-TR (APA 2022) and ICD-11 (WHO, in force 2022). The category is W12.
  - MS.O.A3.4.2 RDoC as the process-first alternative (CONTESTED as a basis for classification).
  - Disorder as function failure, its treatment and its trials are MD.O.A1. MS.O.A3.4 holds the process account only (CONTESTED; alternative: MD owns both).

### MS.O.A4 Interactions

Situation. Something passes between persons. Complication. Classic effects have mixed replication records. Question. Which claims concern what passes between persons? Answer. Five, ordered from the situation to the self it builds.

Key line (inductive; plural noun interactions; dimension what passes between persons; order from situation to self): social influence · social cognition · attitudes · groups and relationships · self and identity.

- **MS.O.A4.1 Social influence.** Conformity, obedience and bystander effects, each with its replication record (Burger 2009; Fischer et al. 2011 meta-analysis on bystanders). Environment–behaviour claims (APA Div. 34) sit here.
- **MS.O.A4.2 Social cognition.** Attribution; the fundamental attribution error (Ross 1977), flagged as weaker in non-WEIRD samples (Henrich et al. 2010).
- **MS.O.A4.3 Attitudes.** Cognitive dissonance (Festinger 1957), with self-perception as the rival account. Consumer attitudes (APA Div. 23) sit here; BU.O.A4.1 uses them.
- **MS.O.A4.4 Groups and relationships.** Small groups, roles, norms, status; couples and families; conversation as turn-taking; human–animal interaction (CONTESTED; alternative BI.O.A3).
- **MS.O.A4.5 Self and identity.** The self as a role taken toward others; identity as a claimed category; body and embodiment. The category's distribution is MS.O.A5.1.

### MS.O.A5 Social structures

Situation. Sociology's core is the pattern that outlasts its members. Complication. v1 mixed movement into population and sector into institution. Question. Which claims concern a pattern no single person carries? Answer. Five, ordered from position to change.

Key line (inductive; plural noun structures; dimension the kind of pattern; order from position to change): positions and inequality · organisations · institutions and culture · populations · collective action and change.

- **MS.O.A5.1 Positions and inequality.** Status, role, role conflict. Inequality as a residual in life chances after stated controls. Bases include class, race, gender, sexuality and disability.
- **MS.O.A5.2 Organisations.** Weberian bureaucracy (rule, file, career); informal structure. PO.O.A2 uses it for state bureaucracy.
- **MS.O.A5.3 Institutions and culture.** An institution is a repeated, taken-for-granted pattern: family, market, state, religion, school, media, science. Culture is shared meaning in use. Consumption as practice sits here. A paper merely set in a church or school is a sector instance.
- **MS.O.A5.4 Populations.** Births, deaths, migration and their rates; life table; cohort. Human population is owned here; non-human population dynamics are BI.O.A5.
- **MS.O.A5.5 Collective action and change.** Social movements that target a norm or an organisation. A movement contesting authority is PO.O.A3.3 (CONTESTED target rule). The free-rider result is EC.O.A5.3. Differentiation, diffusion, globalisation. Named theories of history (Marxian, functionalist) are answer-families, reconstructed under MS.M.3.

## 8. Assignment rules

1. A neural correlate is evidence for a process claim, not the process. It is labelled a correlate until a mechanism is shown.
2. The ion channel and the spike are MS.O.A2.1. The cell as a living unit is BI. The equilibrium potential uses CH.O.A3.3.
3. A diagnostic category is a convention (W12) at MS.O.A3.4.1. Disorder as function failure, treatment and trials are MD.O.A1. Treatment outcome is MD; MS.O.A3.4 cites it.
4. Psychoanalytic theory is an answer-family at MS.O.A1 or MS.O.A3, reconstructed under MS.M.3. Folk psychology is in the register.
5. The availability heuristic as a claim about people is MS.O.A1.5.2. The analyst's rule of thumb is MS.M.H. They are two sentences.
6. Heritability as a quantitative-genetic quantity is owned by BI.O.A4. MS.O.A3.2.2 uses BI.O.A4.
7. An institution as a pattern is MS.O.A5.3. A paper merely set in a church or school is a sector instance.
8. A movement targeting a norm or organisation is MS.O.A5.5. A movement contesting authority uses PO.O.A3.3. The free-rider result uses EC.O.A5.3.
9. Market as allocation is EC.O.A2; market as institution is MS.O.A5.3. Authority is PO.O.A2.
10. Human population is MS.O.A5.4. EC.O.A2.5 (labour supply) uses it. Non-human population is BI.O.A5.
11. Reference-dependent choice as a model of the economic agent is EC.O.A1.3. The bias as a mental process is MS.O.A1.5.2. Strategic exploitation of a bias against a reacting party is ST.O.A5.2.
12. A learning claim under an educational arrangement, including physics and mathematics education research (MSC 97), is ED. A claim about the mechanism of learning, independent of any arrangement, is MS.O.A1.5. The theorem or law taught is MA or PH.
13. Dated claims about past psychologists are HI. The reconstruction of a past theory as an answer is MS.M.3.
14. Audit-to-v2 ID map: A1.5 judgement → A1.5.2; A1.4 memory → A1.3; A1.6 emotion → A1.4; A1.7 consciousness → A1.2.2; A2.5 computational → MS.M.4; A5.3 organisation → A5.2; A5.4 institution and A5.5 culture → A5.3; A5.6 population → A5.4; A5.7 change → A5.5.
