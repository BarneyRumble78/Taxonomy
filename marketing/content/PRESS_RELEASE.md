## Whanganui analyst releases a new standard for classifying all human knowledge, built for the age of AI

WHANGANUI, NEW ZEALAND, [date]: Strategic analyst Chris Townsend today released Taxonomy. It is a new standard that classifies human knowledge claim by claim, showing which field owns each statement and what evidence it needs.

Taxonomy covers 25 fields, from mathematics, physics and law to history, religion and Indigenous knowledge. It is built on the pyramid principle developed by Barbara Minto at McKinsey & Company, and maps to the classification schemes universities and governments already use, including ANZSRC 2020.

"AI can now write fluent text about anything, but it cannot tell a measurement from a legal duty or an opinion," said Townsend. "Taxonomy gives people and machines a shared way to tell them apart."

The release includes a free web tool, an open-source Python engine and a developer API in beta. Its Indigenous knowledge field is provisional and will not be finalised until a Māori-led review has been held.
